Surah Mulk for kids (Surah Al-Mulk, chapter 67) is the perfect “bedtime surah”: it has 30 ayat, fills about three pages of the mushaf, and the Prophet ﷺ told us it intercedes for the one who recites it until he is forgiven. With a simple nightly routine, most school-age children can memorize it in six to ten weeks, and keep it for life by reciting it every night.

This guide gives you a nightly teaching plan by age, the authentic virtues (and one popular narration that is weak), the meaning of each section in simple words, and practical tips for tired children and busy evenings. You can follow the full Arabic and audio on [Surah Al-Mulk on Quran.com](https://quran.com/67).

> **Key takeaways**
> - Surah Al-Mulk (Surah 67) has 30 ayat, was revealed in Makkah, opens Juz 29, and fills pages 562–564 of the Madani mushaf.
> - Authentic hadith: a surah of thirty ayat interceded for a man until he was forgiven, and it is Tabarak (Sunan Abi Dawud 1400, graded hasan).
> - Learn one ayah a night (or two for older children), always reciting from the beginning before adding the new one.
> - Split the surah into five themed sections and tell your child the “picture” of each one before memorizing it.
> - The goal is not only to memorize it, but to recite it every night, together.

## Surah Al-Mulk at a glance

| Detail | Surah Al-Mulk |
|---|---|
| Surah number | 67 |
| Also called | Tabarak, after its first word |
| Number of ayat | 30 |
| Revealed in | Makkah |
| Juz | First surah of Juz 29 (often called Juz Tabarak) |
| Length | About 3 pages (pages 562–564 of the 604-page Madani mushaf) |
| Main themes | Allah’s ownership of everything, the perfect sky, the test of life, the birds, Allah’s provision |

*Al-Mulk* means “the Dominion” or “the Sovereignty”: everything belongs to Allah and is in His control. It is the first surah of Juz 29, so many children who finish Juz Amma move on to it next.

## Why teach your child Surah Al-Mulk? What the hadith say

**The authentic intercession hadith.** Abu Hurairah reported that the Prophet ﷺ said there is a surah of the Quran of thirty ayat which interceded for a man until he was forgiven, and it is *Tabarak alladhi biyadihil-mulk*. It is recorded in [Sunan Abi Dawud 1400](https://sunnah.com/abudawud:1400) (graded hasan by al-Albani), [Jami‘ at-Tirmidhi 2891](https://sunnah.com/tirmidhi:2891) (at-Tirmidhi called it hasan) and [Sunan Ibn Majah 3786](https://sunnah.com/ibnmajah:3786).

**Reciting it before sleep.** Jabir reported that the Prophet ﷺ would not sleep until he had recited Surah As-Sajdah and Surah Al-Mulk ([Jami‘ at-Tirmidhi 2892](https://sunnah.com/tirmidhi:2892)). Scholars differ on this chain: al-Albani graded it authentic, while the Darussalam edition grades it weak. Reciting Al-Mulk at night is a long-standing practice that many Muslims keep, and the intercession hadith above is reason enough to make it a nightly habit.

**A narration to be careful with.** A widely shared report says Al-Mulk is “the protector, the deliverer” from the punishment of the grave, in a story about a companion who pitched his tent on a grave ([Jami‘ at-Tirmidhi 2890](https://sunnah.com/tirmidhi:2890)). This particular narration is graded weak (Darussalam). If you want to discuss the grave with an older child, ask a trusted teacher, and keep the focus for young children on Allah’s mercy.

> **Tip:** For children, keep it simple and true: “This surah is special. The Prophet ﷺ said it asks Allah to forgive the person who recites it. So we recite it every night.”

## What Surah Al-Mulk teaches: five sections children can picture

Telling the meaning first makes memorizing faster and recitation more heartfelt. Here are five natural sections.

### Section 1 (ayat 1–5): Allah owns everything and made a perfect sky

The surah begins by praising Allah, who holds all dominion and has power over everything:

تَبَـٰرَكَ ٱلَّذِى بِيَدِهِ ٱلْمُلْكُ وَهُوَ عَلَىٰ كُلِّ شَىْءٍ قَدِيرٌ ﴿١﴾

*Tabaarakal-ladhee biyadihil-mulku wa Huwa ‘alaa kulli shay’in Qadeer.* Blessed is He in whose hand is dominion, and He is over all things competent. (67:1)

Then Allah tells us why we are here:

ٱلَّذِى خَلَقَ ٱلْمَوْتَ وَٱلْحَيَوٰةَ لِيَبْلُوَكُمْ أَيُّكُمْ أَحْسَنُ عَمَلًا ۚ وَهُوَ ٱلْعَزِيزُ ٱلْغَفُورُ ﴿٢﴾

*Alladhee khalaqal-mawta wal-hayaata liyabluwakum ayyukum ahsanu ‘amalaa, wa Huwal-‘Azeezul-Ghafoor.* He created death and life to test you as to which of you is best in deed, and He is the Exalted in Might, the Forgiving. (67:2)

Ayat 3–5 invite us to look up at the sky again and again: we will never find a crack or a flaw, and Allah has decorated the nearest sky with stars like lamps. A lovely activity: read these ayat, then go outside on a clear night and look up together.

### Section 2 (ayat 6–11): The warner came

These ayat describe the Fire and the regret of people who said no to the messengers: “If only we had been listening or reasoning” (67:10). For young children, keep the lesson simple: Allah sent messengers to warn and guide us, so we listen to them.

### Section 3 (ayat 12–15): Allah knows our secrets and made the earth easy for us

Those who fear their Lord unseen will have forgiveness and a great reward (67:12). Whether we whisper or speak aloud, Allah knows what is in our hearts (67:13). He made the earth easy to walk on, so we can travel it and eat from His provision (67:15).

### Section 4 (ayat 16–22): Who holds up the birds?

This section asks big questions. Children especially love ayah 19:

أَوَلَمْ يَرَوْا۟ إِلَى ٱلطَّيْرِ فَوْقَهُمْ صَـٰٓفَّـٰتٍ وَيَقْبِضْنَ ۚ مَا يُمْسِكُهُنَّ إِلَّا ٱلرَّحْمَـٰنُ ۚ إِنَّهُۥ بِكُلِّ شَىْءٍۭ بَصِيرٌ ﴿١٩﴾

*Awalam yaraw ilat-tayri fawqahum saaaffaatinw-wa yaqbidn; maa yumsikuhunna illar-Rahmaan; innahoo bikulli shay’im Baseer.* Do they not see the birds above them with wings outspread and folded in? None holds them aloft except the Most Merciful. Indeed He is, of all things, Seeing. (67:19)

Watch birds at the park or the riverbank together and recite this ayah. It is a lesson children remember for years.

### Section 5 (ayat 23–30): Our hearing, sight and water are gifts

Allah gave us hearing, sight and hearts, “little are you grateful” (67:23). The surah ends with a question that makes every child think:

قُلْ أَرَءَيْتُمْ إِنْ أَصْبَحَ مَآؤُكُمْ غَوْرًا فَمَن يَأْتِيكُم بِمَآءٍ مَّعِينٍۭ ﴿٣٠﴾

*Qul ara’aytum in asbaha maaa’ukum ghawran famany-ya’teekum bimaaa’im-ma‘een.* Say, “Have you considered: if your water was to become sunken into the earth, then who could bring you flowing water?” (67:30)

Translations above are from Saheeh International, as shown on Quran.com.

## Surah Mulk for kids: a nightly memorization plan

The method is simple: **recite from the beginning every night, then add one new ayah.** By the end, your child is reciting the whole surah nightly, which is the habit you want.

**Step 1: Listen for a week first.** Play the whole surah at bedtime for 5–7 nights. Choose a slow, clear reciter; on Quran.com, Mahmoud Khalil Al-Husary’s *Muallim* (teaching) recitation or Mohamed Siddiq al-Minshawi’s *Murattal* both work well for children.

**Step 2: Tell the story of Section 1.** Two or three sentences in your home language.

**Step 3: Teach ayah 1 with the echo method.** You recite a phrase; your child repeats. Five to ten times, then together.

**Step 4: Each night, recite from ayah 1 up to the last ayah learned.** Then add the next ayah. If yesterday’s ayah is shaky, do not add a new one tonight.

**Step 5: Pause at the end of each section.** Spend two or three nights just reciting the section smoothly before you tell the next section’s story.

**Step 6: Get a weekly check.** Ask a teacher or fluent reciter to listen once a week. Our guide to [tajweed rules for kids](/blog/tajweed-rules-for-kids) explains what they will listen for.

**Step 7: Keep it forever.** Once finished, recite Al-Mulk every night as part of bedtime. Nightly recitation *is* the revision plan.

### Pace by age

| Age | Nightly new portion | Roughly how long for all 30 ayat |
|---|---|---|
| 5–6 | Half an ayah, or listen only | 3–4 months (or keep listening until ready) |
| 7–8 | 1 ayah | About 7–10 weeks, including review nights |
| 9–11 | 1–2 ayat | About 4–7 weeks |
| 12+ | 2–3 ayat | About 3–4 weeks |

These are estimates, not targets. Some ayat (like 67:2 and 67:19) are long and deserve two nights.

### A sample 15-minute bedtime routine

1. Wudu and pyjamas (or the other way round).
2. Recite Surah Al-Mulk from the beginning up to the last ayah learned (5–8 minutes as it grows).
3. Learn tonight’s new ayah (3–5 minutes).
4. [Ayatul Kursi](/blog/ayat-al-kursi-for-kids), then Al-Ikhlas, Al-Falaq and An-Nas.
5. Bedtime dua, lights out.

## When your child is too tired

Bedtime is the natural time, but a child who is falling asleep will not memorize anything. Practical options:

- **Move the new lesson earlier**, after Maghrib or Isha, and keep only the recitation for bedtime.
- **Swap roles on tired nights:** you recite, they listen. Listening still counts as Quran time.
- **Shorten, don’t skip.** On very busy nights, recite just the section your child is working on.
- **Make a chart.** Thirty boxes, one per ayah, coloured in when solid. Many children find watching the chart fill up very motivating.

## Common mistakes to avoid

- **Adding a new ayah before the last one is solid.** Slower is faster.
- **Only memorizing, never reciting.** Al-Mulk is meant to be recited regularly; once learned, make it nightly.
- **Relying on transliteration.** Use it only to support audio and a teacher.
- **Turning it into fear.** Use the surah to build love and gratitude: the sky, the birds and the water.
- **Teaching weak narrations as certain.** Share the authentic intercession hadith instead.
