An Islamic bedtime routine for kids is a short, predictable sequence that follows the Prophet's ﷺ own night-time habits: wudu, dusting the bed, reciting the three Quls, Ayat al-Kursi, a dua, and lying down on the right side. Done in the same order every night, it takes 15–20 minutes and gives children both the calm they need to sleep and a lifelong habit of ending the day with Allah.

> **Key takeaways**
> - The core Sunnah steps are wudu, dusting the bed with Bismillah, the three Quls with blowing and wiping, Ayat al-Kursi, the sleeping dua and lying on the right side. All come from authentic hadith.
> - Keep the order the same every night. Predictability is what helps young children settle.
> - Canada's sleep guidelines recommend 10–13 hours for ages 3–4 and 9–11 hours for ages 5–13, with consistent bed and wake-up times.
> - Switch screens off at least an hour before bed, as the Canadian Paediatric Society advises.
> - Start small with toddlers (Bismillah and one Qul) and add a step every few weeks.

## Islamic bedtime routine for kids at a glance

| Step | What you do | Source | Time |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Screens off, lights low, Isha prayer if it's in | CPS sleep advice; Sahih al-Bukhari 568 | 60 min before |
| 2 | Brush teeth and make wudu | Sahih al-Bukhari 247 | 5 min |
| 3 | Dust the bed and say Bismillah | Sahih al-Bukhari 6320 | 1 min |
| 4 | Recite Al-Ikhlas, Al-Falaq, An-Nas; blow and wipe, three times | Sahih al-Bukhari 5017 | 2 min |
| 5 | Recite Ayat al-Kursi | Sahih al-Bukhari 5010 | 1 min |
| 6 | Tasbih: 33 SubhanAllah, 33 Alhamdulillah, 34 Allahu Akbar | Sahih al-Bukhari 3705 | 3 min |
| 7 | A short story and a chat about the day | — | 5–10 min |
| 8 | Lie on the right side and say the sleeping dua | Sahih al-Bukhari 247, 6324 | 1 min |

You don't need every step on night one. Pick two or three, keep them for a month, then add the next.

## Start the evening at Maghrib

The routine really begins at sunset. The Prophet ﷺ told parents to keep children close at nightfall, to close the doors and cover containers while saying Allah's name (Sahih al-Bukhari 3280). Bringing children inside at Maghrib, praying together and serving dinner shortly after gives the evening a natural shape.

He also "disliked to sleep before the Isha prayer and to talk after it" (Sahih al-Bukhari 568). For families, the lesson is to wind down after Isha rather than starting new activities.

**The Ottawa twist.** Prayer times swing widely here. Around the winter solstice, Maghrib is about 4:20 PM and Isha (ISNA calculation) about 5:50 PM, comfortably before a young child's bedtime. Around the summer solstice, sunset is close to 9 PM and Isha nearer 11 PM. Children under seven are not yet asked to pray regularly, so in summer let little ones go to bed at their usual time and keep the rest of the routine. Older children can pray Isha with you when it comes in.

## Step-by-step Islamic bedtime routine

### Step 1: Screens off and wind down

The Canadian Paediatric Society advises parents to "avoid screens before bedtime," to keep devices out of bedrooms and, for children who struggle to fall asleep, to allow no screens for one hour before sleep ([Caring for Kids: healthy sleep](https://caringforkids.cps.ca/handouts/healthy-living/healthy_sleep_for_your_baby_and_child)). Dim the lights, tidy up together and lay out clothes for the morning.

### Step 2: Wudu before bed

The Prophet ﷺ told al-Bara' ibn 'Azib: "Whenever you go to bed, perform ablution like that for the prayer, lie on your right side" and then say a dua ([Sahih al-Bukhari 247](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:247)). Brush teeth first, then make wudu. Many children find the cool water calming. Our step-by-step guide to [teaching kids wudu](/blog/teaching-kids-wudu) breaks it into child-sized steps.

### Step 3: Dust the bed with Bismillah

The Prophet ﷺ said to shake out the bed before lying on it, "for he does not know what has come on to it after him," and then to say (Sahih al-Bukhari 6320):

بِاسْمِكَ رَبِّ وَضَعْتُ جَنْبِي، وَبِكَ أَرْفَعُهُ، إِنْ أَمْسَكْتَ نَفْسِي فَارْحَمْهَا، وَإِنْ أَرْسَلْتَهَا فَاحْفَظْهَا بِمَا تَحْفَظُ بِهِ عِبَادَكَ الصَّالِحِينَ

*Bismika Rabbi wada'tu janbi, wa bika arfa'uh. In amsakta nafsi farhamha, wa in arsaltaha fahfazha bima tahfazu bihi 'ibadakas-salihin.* "In Your name, my Lord, I lay down my side, and by You I raise it. If You take my soul, have mercy on it, and if You return it, protect it as You protect Your righteous servants."

For under-sixes, the dusting plus "Bismillah" is plenty. Children love giving the duvet three big shakes. Add the full dua around age seven or eight.

### Step 4: The three Quls, blow and wipe

Every night, the Prophet ﷺ would cup his hands, recite Surah Al-Ikhlas, Al-Falaq and An-Nas, blow lightly into his hands and wipe over as much of his body as he could, "starting with his head, face and front of his body," three times ([Sahih al-Bukhari 5017](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:5017)).

Children usually know these surahs first, so this is the easiest Sunnah to start with. Recite them together and wipe your child's head and back for them until they can do it themselves. If they are still learning the words, our guide to [short surahs for kids](/blog/short-surahs-for-kids) has the Arabic and a memorisation order.

### Step 5: Ayat al-Kursi

When you go to bed, recite Ayat al-Kursi (Surah Al-Baqarah 2:255), "for then there will be a guard from Allah who will protect you all night long, and no Satan will come near you until the morning." The Prophet ﷺ confirmed this advice was true (Sahih al-Bukhari 5010). Children aged five and up can usually learn it in a few weeks; our [Ayatul Kursi for kids](/blog/ayat-al-kursi-for-kids) guide breaks it into chunks.

### Step 6: The tasbih of Fatimah

When Fatimah asked for a servant to ease her housework, the Prophet ﷺ taught her and Ali something "better for you both than a servant": at bedtime, say Allahu Akbar 34 times, SubhanAllah 33 times and Alhamdulillah 33 times (Sahih al-Bukhari 3705; another narration, 6318, has the numbers in a different order). Counting on fingers is good practice for little ones, and a short version (three of each) works for toddlers.

### Step 7: A story and a chat

This is the part children ask for most. Tell one prophet story at a time, repeat favourites, and finish with a single lesson. Our list of [prophet stories for kids](/blog/prophet-stories-for-kids) gives you 25 to rotate. Then ask two quick questions: "What was the best part of your day?" and "What are you thankful to Allah for?"

### Step 8: Right side and the sleeping dua

Lie down on the right side, as in the hadith of al-Bara' above, and say the short dua the Prophet ﷺ said at bedtime (Sahih al-Bukhari 6324):

بِاسْمِكَ اللَّهُمَّ أَمُوتُ وَأَحْيَا

*Bismika Allahumma amutu wa ahya.* "In Your name, O Allah, I die and I live."

Many families end with a kiss and the protection dua the Prophet ﷺ said over his grandsons, which is in our guide to [daily duas for kids](/blog/daily-duas-for-kids).

## Surahs to recite at bedtime

Beyond the three Quls and Ayat al-Kursi, two more recitations suit older children:

- **The last two verses of Surah Al-Baqarah (2:285–286).** The Prophet ﷺ said that whoever recites them at night, "that will be sufficient for him" (Sahih al-Bukhari 5009).
- **Surah Al-Mulk (67).** The Prophet ﷺ said there is a surah of thirty verses that intercedes for a person until they are forgiven, and named it as Tabarak alladhi biyadihil-mulk ([Jami' at-Tirmidhi 2891](https://sunnah.com/tirmidhi:2891), graded hasan). Many families recite it nightly. Our [Surah Al-Mulk for kids](/blog/surah-al-mulk-for-kids) plan builds up to it a few verses at a time.

## The bedtime routine by age

Sleep hours below are from the [Canadian 24-Hour Movement Guidelines](https://csepguidelines.ca/guidelines/early-years/), which recommend consistent bedtimes and wake-up times at every age.

| Age | Recommended sleep | Islamic steps to include | Length |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1–2 | 11–14 hours (incl. naps) | Bismillah, dust the bed, Al-Ikhlas, "Bismika Allahumma amutu wa ahya" | 10 min |
| 3–4 | 10–13 hours (incl. a nap) | Add Al-Falaq and An-Nas with blowing and wiping | 15 min |
| 5–8 | 9–11 hours | Add wudu, Ayat al-Kursi, short tasbih, a prophet story | 20 min |
| 9–13 | 9–11 hours | Add the full bed dua, full tasbih, last two verses of Al-Baqarah or Al-Mulk | 20–25 min |

For a four- or five-year-old who needs to be at school by 8:45 AM, working back from a 7:00 AM wake-up puts lights-out between about 7:00 and 8:00 PM.

## When your child is scared or has a bad dream

Take bedtime fears seriously. The CPS advice is clear: "Don't ignore bedtime fears." Stay calm, sit close and reassure.

The Prophet ﷺ taught that "a good dream is from Allah, and a bad dream is from Satan." Whoever sees something they dislike should spit lightly (without saliva) three times to the left and seek refuge in Allah, "for it will not harm him" ([Sahih al-Bukhari 6995](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:6995)). For a child, that becomes: blow gently to the left three times, say *A'udhu billahi minash-shaytanir-rajim*, turn over and recite Al-Ikhlas together. It gives them something to do, and it works on the fear.

> **Tip:** Make a picture chart of the routine at child height: a toothbrush, a water drop for wudu, a blanket, cupped hands, a book, a pillow. Children who can "read" the next step argue far less about bedtime.

## Making the routine stick

- **Same order, every night.** Even when you're out late, do a two-minute version: three Quls and the sleeping dua.
- **Do it with them.** Children learn the adhkar by hearing you say them, not from being told to.
- **Give each child a job.** One shakes the bed, one leads Al-Ikhlas, one turns off the light.
- **Praise the habit, not the perfection.** "You remembered to lie on your right side!" builds ownership.
- **Plan for travel and Ramadan.** Keep the Islamic steps even when bedtime shifts.

An Islamic bedtime routine for kids doesn't need to be long. Wudu, a shaken-out bed, three short surahs, Ayat al-Kursi, a story and "Bismika Allahumma amutu wa ahya" on the right side: that's the whole Sunnah, and it fits in twenty minutes.
