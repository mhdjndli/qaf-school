The best Sunday activities for kids in Ottawa mix one anchor (a weekly class, a museum, a market or a car-free parkway) with plenty of unstructured family time. This guide gives you a repeatable Sunday plan rather than a one-off list: what is actually open on Sundays, what fits each season and age, what is free, and how to fit Quran, Arabic and prayer into the day without turning it into another school day.

> **Key takeaways**
> - Ten Ottawa Public Library branches, including Nepean Centrepointe and Greenboro, have Sunday hours. Check the branch page before you go, because Sunday schedules change seasonally.
> - The Canada Science and Technology Museum and the Canadian Museum of Nature are both open on Sundays; Ingenium museums are free daily from 4 to 5 PM.
> - The Lansdowne Farmers' Market runs on Sundays, and the NCC's Weekend Bikedays close parkways to cars from spring to fall.
> - A weekly Sunday program (for example, a weekend Islamic school or QAF Sunday Camp for ages 8 to 15) gives kids structure and friends, and gives parents a predictable rhythm.

## A simple Sunday rhythm you can repeat

A good Sunday has three ingredients: one outing or program, one slow block at home, and one shared family moment. Kids who go to public school all week often need a day that feels different, not a second school day packed with errands.

In our experience, children do best when Sunday has a predictable shape. "We go to class, then the library, then dinner at Teta's" is easier for a child than a different surprise every week. You can still change the outing; just keep the rhythm.

For Muslim families, Sunday is also the most natural day for faith learning. Weekday evenings are crowded with homework and activities, while Sunday has room for Quran review, a short family halaqa, or a weekly class with friends who share the same values.

Here is a template many families find workable. Adjust it to your children's ages and your prayer times.

| Time | What happens | Ideas |
|---|---|---|
| Morning | Slow start and breakfast together | Pancakes, Fajr catch-up talk, tidy rooms |
| Late morning | The anchor activity | A Sunday class, museum, market or bike ride |
| Midday | Lunch and Dhuhr | Packed lunch at a park, or home |
| Afternoon | Free play or a second, lighter outing | Library, playground, visit friends |
| Late afternoon | Quiet reset | Reading, Quran review, board games |
| Evening | Family dinner and prep for Monday | Pack bags, lay out clothes, early bed |

**Tip:** In Ottawa's short winter days, Dhuhr, Asr and Maghrib can all fall inside a typical Sunday outing. Check the day's prayer times in the morning and pack a travel prayer mat so prayer is part of the plan, not an interruption.

## Free Sunday activities in Ottawa

You do not need a big budget to make Sundays special. These options cost nothing or very little.

### 1. Ottawa Public Library on Sundays

The [Ottawa Public Library lists ten branches with Sunday hours](https://collections.biblioottawalibrary.ca/en/blogs/10-branches-open-sundays-all-summer): Beaverbrook, Cumberland, Greenboro, Hazeldean, Main, Nepean Centrepointe, North Gloucester, Orléans, Ruth E. Dickinson and Stittsville. For families near QAF, Nepean Centrepointe is the closest of these.

Let each child pick a "Sunday book" to read before bed that week. Ask at the desk about Islamic picture books, Arabic titles, and museum Access Passes that you can borrow for free admission.

### 2. Lansdowne Farmers' Market

The [Ottawa Farmers' Market at Lansdowne](https://ottawatourism.ca/en/see-and-do/ottawa-farmers-market) is a Sunday tradition for many families, held outdoors in the warmer months and inside the Aberdeen Pavilion in winter. Kids can talk to growers, sample fruit, and practise paying with coins.

Give each child a small budget and one job, such as choosing the apples for the week. It turns a market walk into a lesson in money, food and gratitude.

### 3. NCC Weekend Bikedays

From spring to fall, the [NCC's Weekend Bikedays](https://ncc-ccn.gc.ca/places/weekend-bikedays) close sections of the capital's parkways to cars so families can bike, scoot, run and walk safely. It is one of the best ways for a nervous young cyclist to build confidence on a smooth, wide road.

Bring water, helmets and a snack, and choose a short out-and-back route so tired legs can turn around early.

### 4. Parks, trails and Gatineau Park

Every Ottawa neighbourhood has playgrounds, and Gatineau Park is a short drive across the river for easy hikes and fall colours. Our guide to [things to do with kids in Ottawa](/blog/things-to-do-with-kids-ottawa) covers seasonal favourites in more detail.

### 5. Free museum hour

Ingenium's museums, including the Canada Science and Technology Museum, offer free admission every day from 4 to 5 PM, Sundays included. One focused hour on a single gallery is often better for young kids than a full exhausting day.

## Paid Sunday outings worth the money

When you do spend, choose activities your children will talk about all week.

### 1. Canada Science and Technology Museum

The [Canada Science and Technology Museum](https://ingenium.ca/scitech/en/visit) is open Tuesday to Sunday from 9 AM to 5 PM. Youth admission (ages 3 to 17) is listed at $12.50 and a family ticket at $51, before tax; children 2 and under are free. Advance tickets are recommended.

**Best for:** Kids aged 5 to 12 who like trains, building and buttons.

### 2. Canadian Museum of Nature

The [Canadian Museum of Nature](https://nature.ca/en/visit-us/plan-your-visit/admission-fees/) is open Sundays from 9:30 AM to 4 PM, with dinosaurs, a mammal gallery and live insects. Children 3 to 12 are listed at $19, toddlers are free, and booking online saves 10%.

**Best for:** Younger kids and animal lovers. The museum's architecture and exhibits are also a natural way to talk about Allah as Al-Khaliq, the Creator.

### 3. Drop-in swimming and skating

City of Ottawa pools and arenas run drop-in public swims and skates on weekends, with schedules posted on each facility's page on ottawa.ca. Drop-in fees are modest, and multi-visit passes lower the cost further.

**Tip:** Family swim times fill up, especially in winter. Arrive early or reserve online if your facility allows it.

## Sunday ideas by age

Different ages need different Sundays. Use this as a starting point.

### Ages 3 to 5

- Short outings of 60 to 90 minutes, then home for a nap or quiet time.
- Library storytime or picture-book browsing.
- Playground plus a snack picnic.
- A simple Sunday dua routine: the dua for leaving the house and the dua before eating, said together.

### Ages 6 to 9

- A museum gallery with a "find five things" challenge.
- Bike or scooter practice on car-free parkways.
- A weekly class where they see the same friends every week.
- A family Quran review: each child recites one short surah they know.

### Ages 10 to 15

- Give them a say in planning. Let them choose the outing every second Sunday.
- Volunteer together, for example at a food drive or a mosque clean-up.
- A structured program with peers, such as a weekend Islamic school, a youth halaqa, or a Sunday camp that mixes Quran with sport and STEM.
- Cooking Sunday dinner together, from shopping list to cleanup.

## Indoor Sundays for Ottawa winters

From December to March, Ottawa Sundays can be cold, dark early and icy. Plan a few indoor go-tos so you are not stuck deciding at 10 AM.

- **Library afternoon:** Choose a Sunday-open branch and stay for an hour.
- **Indoor pool:** A family swim burns energy on the coldest days.
- **Home "Sunday project":** Build a model mosque from cardboard, bake bread, or start a Ramadan countdown calendar in late winter.
- **Board game hour:** Rotate who chooses the game; younger kids can team up with an adult.
- **Quran and story circle:** Ten minutes of recitation, then one of the [prophet stories for kids](/blog/prophet-stories-for-kids) read aloud with hot chocolate.

For more home-based ideas, see our list of [screen-free weekend activities for kids](/blog/screen-free-weekend-ideas).

## Keeping Sunday restful (not overscheduled)

It is easy to fill Sunday with so many good things that nobody gets rest. A few guidelines help:

1. **One anchor, not three.** Choose one main outing or program per Sunday.
2. **Protect the evening.** Keep the last few hours for dinner, bath and preparation for Monday.
3. **Rotate the big outings.** Museum one week, market the next, a home project the week after.
4. **Keep a "rainy day list" on the fridge.** It stops the "I'm bored" negotiation before it starts.
5. **Build faith in gently.** A short, warm Quran review or story beats a long lecture every time.

The Prophet Muhammad (peace be upon him) taught that the deeds most beloved to Allah are those done regularly, even if small ([Sahih al-Bukhari 6464](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:6464)). A ten-minute Sunday Quran circle you keep for a year will do more than an ambitious plan you drop after three weeks.
