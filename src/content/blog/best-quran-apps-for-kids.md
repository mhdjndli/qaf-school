The best Quran apps for kids depend on what your child needs right now: listening and following along, learning to read with tajweed, memorizing surahs, or simply enjoying Quran stories. For most families, a free Quran reader like Quran.com plus one child-focused learning app covers almost everything.

We looked at the apps parents ask about most, checked that each one is currently available on the App Store and Google Play in Canada (as of October 2026), and compared them on price, ages, platforms and what they are genuinely good for.

> **Key takeaways**
> - Quran.com is the best free all-round app for listening, following along and looping verses for memorization.
> - Thurayya is built specifically for children, with a Juz Amma memorization plan and AI pronunciation feedback.
> - Tarteel suits older children and teens who can read Arabic and are actively memorizing.
> - Learn Quran Tajwid is a strong step-by-step course for learning to read with tajweed.
> - No app replaces a qualified teacher. Use apps for listening and revision, and keep a parent nearby.

## How we chose these apps

We focused on apps that do something useful for a child’s Quran learning and that we could confirm are actively available. For each, we looked at:

- **Accuracy:** a proper Mushaf text and established reciters.
- **Child-friendliness:** simple navigation, short sessions, and no ads in children’s areas.
- **Learning value:** does it help a child listen, read, memorize or understand?
- **Parent control:** can you supervise, track progress or limit use?

We left out apps that have not been updated in years, and we have not included any app we could not confirm is available.

## Best Quran apps for listening and memorizing

### 1. Quran.com (Free)

**What it is:** The official app and website from Quran.com, run by the Quran Foundation. It offers an ad-free Mushaf, many reciters, translations and word-by-word display. On Android, the app is listed as “Quran for Android – Quran.com”.

**Best for:** Listening to a surah on repeat, following along with the Arabic, and checking meanings with your child.

**Price and ages:** Free and ad-free. Suitable for all ages with a parent choosing the surah and reciter.

**Platforms:** [Quran.com](https://quran.com) on the web, plus iOS and Android apps.

**Our take:** This is the first app we recommend to every family. Pick one slow, clear reciter, set a verse to repeat, and play it at breakfast or bedtime. The word-by-word view is excellent for parents who want to explain meanings in simple language. It is not designed as a children’s app, so stay nearby and choose the content yourself.

### 2. Thurayya: Quran for Kids (Free tier, subscription)

**What it is:** A Quran memorization app made for children by [Alphazed](https://www.thealphazed.com/), the company behind the Amal Arabic app. Its App Store listing describes a step-by-step Juz Amma plan, real-time AI speech recognition that corrects pronunciation, Prophet stories, child-friendly ahadith, multiple child profiles and parent progress tracking.

**Best for:** Young children starting to memorize short surahs, especially in families where parents are still learning Arabic themselves.

**Price and ages:** Free to download with limited access; the full curriculum requires a subscription, with a free trial on eligible plans. The developer lists it for ages 3 to 15. It is ad-free.

**Platforms:** iOS and Android.

**Our take:** Thurayya is one of the few Quran apps genuinely designed around a child’s attention span. AI feedback is a helpful nudge, but it is not a tajweed teacher; treat it as a practice partner between lessons. Pair it with our list of [short surahs for kids](/blog/short-surahs-for-kids) so you know the meanings to share.

### 3. Tarteel: AI Quran Memorization (Free core, Premium)

**What it is:** A recitation and memorization app from [Tarteel](https://www.tarteel.ai/) that listens as you recite. Free features include following along with your recitation, unlimited listening to reciters, and voice search to find a verse. Premium adds live memorization mistake detection, hidden-verse mode, goals and mistake history.

**Best for:** Older children and teens who can read Arabic and are memorizing beyond Juz Amma.

**Price and ages:** Free core features; Premium is a paid subscription with a free trial. The app is rated 4+, but it is built for general users rather than young children, so we suggest it from around age 9 with a parent setting it up.

**Platforms:** iOS and Android. It needs microphone access and an internet connection for voice features.

**Our take:** For a motivated 10- to 14-year-old doing hifz, Tarteel’s mistake detection makes solo revision far more productive. It works best alongside a teacher who checks tajweed; an app can tell you a word was wrong, but a teacher explains why. Our guide to [Quran memorization for kids](/blog/quran-memorization-for-kids) explains how to structure revision around it.

## Best apps for learning to read the Quran

### 4. Learn Quran Tajwid (Free to download, Pro)

**What it is:** A structured tajweed course from Learn Quran (Yayasan Learn Quran), available since 2013. Lessons run from the alphabet, harakat, sukoon, shaddah and tanween through madd, rules of nun sakinah and meem sakinah, waqf and makharij. Each topic has narrated audio and a quiz, and learners can record themselves to compare with the narration or send to a teacher.

**Best for:** Children aged about 7 and up who are learning to read, or older kids who read but need to fix their tajweed.

**Price and ages:** Free to download, with Pro subscriptions and lifetime options for full access. Check the current price in your store.

**Platforms:** iOS and Android (iPad recommended when studying with a teacher).

**Our take:** This is the closest thing to a digital qaida with a tajweed course attached. It works well as homework between classes. If your child is still choosing a primer, see our comparison of [Noorani Qaida vs Qaida Baghdadia](/blog/noorani-qaida-vs-qaida-baghdadia).

### 5. Ayat – Al Quran (Free)

**What it is:** A digital Mushaf from King Saud University’s electronic Mushaf project. It shows a scanned printed Mushaf, with a choice of the Madinah Mushaf, a colour-coded tajweed Mushaf or the Warsh narration, many reciters, ayah repeat with intervals, and several Arabic tafsir.

**Best for:** Children who are moving from a qaida to reading from a real Mushaf, especially using the colour-coded tajweed edition.

**Price and ages:** Free. Suitable from around age 8 with guidance.

**Platforms:** iOS and Android.

**Our take:** Reading on a familiar printed page layout helps children transition to a paper Mushaf. The colour-coded tajweed view is a great visual reminder of rules your child is learning in class.

## Best app for understanding meanings

### 6. Al Quran (Tafsir & by Word) by Greentech (Free)

**What it is:** A free, ad-free Quran app from Greentech Apps Foundation with word-by-word translations in many languages, more than 170 translations and tafsirs, 80+ reciters with repeat options, and daily reading goals.

**Best for:** Older children (around 9+) and parents who want to understand what each word means.

**Price and ages:** Free and ad-free.

**Platforms:** iOS and Android.

**Our take:** Understanding even a few key words makes memorization stick. Try exploring one ayah a day together, pointing to each Arabic word and its meaning. For building vocabulary, our guide to the [best Arabic apps for kids](/blog/best-arabic-apps-for-kids) has more options.

## Best apps for Quran stories and young children

### 7. Miraj: Quran and Islam for Kids (Subscription)

**What it is:** An app from Sidra Media ([Miraj Stories](https://www.mirajstories.com/)) with animated Prophet stories, interactive books, audiobooks and mini games on topics like the names of Allah, prayer and wudu. Its library includes stories from the Quran.

**Best for:** Children aged 4 to 9 who learn through stories.

**Price and ages:** Free to download; full access requires a subscription, with a trial on some plans. The developer lists ages 4 to 9.

**Platforms:** iOS and Android.

**Our take:** Miraj is not a recitation tool. It is a lovely way to bring Quranic stories to life so that, when your child memorizes Al-Fil or hears about Prophet Yunus, the story is already familiar.

### 8. Muslim Kids TV (Membership)

**What it is:** A streaming service from Milo Productions with a large library of Islamic videos, games and eBooks, including stories of the Quran, the Prophets and the Companions.

**Best for:** Families who want one place for Islamic videos and Quran stories for younger children.

**Price and ages:** Free to download; content requires a monthly or yearly membership. The developer describes it as suitable for ages 2 to 12.

**Platforms:** iOS, Android, web and smart TV apps.

**Our take:** Choose it for wholesome, story-based Islamic content rather than structured Quran learning. Watch together when you can and talk about what you saw. Our list of [Islamic cartoons for kids](/blog/best-islamic-cartoons-for-kids) covers more options.

### 9. Kahf Kids (Free features, Premium)

**What it is:** An Islamic video app with cartoons, nasheeds and Prophet stories, plus a Quran reader with recitation and translations, duas, quizzes and games. Its standout feature is parental controls: daily limits, channel blocking, music settings and PIN-protected settings, with app blocking on supported devices.

**Best for:** Families who want Islamic content and screen-time controls in one app.

**Price and ages:** Free learning features; Premium with a 7-day free trial for eligible accounts. The App Store lists it as made for young children.

**Platforms:** iOS and Android.

**Our take:** Useful if you want a single, controlled space for your child’s screen time. As with any video app, set limits and keep it a supervised treat rather than a default activity.

## How to choose the right Quran app for your child

- **Ages 3–5:** Listening comes first. Quran.com on repeat, plus a story app like Miraj in small doses. Thurayya can work with a parent beside them.
- **Ages 6–8:** Add a structured app: Thurayya for memorization or Learn Quran Tajwid for reading.
- **Ages 9–12:** Learn Quran Tajwid or Ayat for reading; Tarteel for serious memorization; Greentech’s Al Quran for meanings.
- **Teens:** Tarteel and a full Mushaf app, with a teacher checking regularly.

If you are still deciding between a tutor on a screen and a teacher in a room, our guide to [online vs in-person Quran classes](/blog/online-vs-in-person-quran-classes) can help.

## Tips for supervised use

Quran apps are only as good as the habits around them. A few rules make a big difference:

1. **Set up child accounts and controls.** Use [Apple’s parental controls](https://support.apple.com/en-ca/105121) or [Google Family Link](https://families.google/familylink/) to approve downloads and block in-app purchases. Our guide to [parental controls for Muslim families](/blog/parental-controls-muslim-families) walks through the setup.
2. **Keep devices in shared spaces.** Quran practice in the living room, not behind a closed door.
3. **Choose content yourself.** Pick the surah, reciter and lesson so your child is not scrolling.
4. **Short, purposeful sessions.** Ten focused minutes of listening and repeating is better than an hour of tapping.
5. **Make it a family media plan.** The Canadian Paediatric Society’s [guidance on digital media for school-aged children](https://cps.ca/en/documents/position/digital-media) recommends a family plan and favours screen use that is educational, active or social over passive use.
6. **Recite to a person.** After using an app, have your child recite to you or a teacher. Real listening and gentle correction are what build accurate recitation.
7. **Watch for warning signs.** If your child resists stopping, or app time crowds out sleep, play or reading, scale back.

> **Tip:** Turn on Do Not Disturb during Quran practice, and on an iPhone or iPad use Guided Access to keep your child inside one app. Download audio in advance where the app allows it, so a weak connection does not interrupt the lesson.
