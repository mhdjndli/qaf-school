Ayatul Kursi for kids is one of the best “next steps” after the short surahs: it is a single ayah (Al-Baqarah 2:255), the Prophet ﷺ called it the greatest ayah in the Book of Allah, and it is recited every night before sleep. Because it divides neatly into nine short phrases, most children can learn it one phrase at a time over a few weeks.

Below you will find the full Arabic text (Uthmani script, from [Quran.com](https://quran.com/2/255)), a phrase-by-phrase transliteration and child-friendly meaning, the authentic virtues, and a step-by-step teaching plan for different ages.

> **Key takeaways**
> - Ayatul Kursi is the 255th ayah of Surah Al-Baqarah, in Juz 3. It is one ayah, but a long one, made of nine short phrases.
> - It is the greatest ayah of the Quran (Sahih Muslim 810), and reciting it at bedtime brings Allah’s protection until morning (Sahih al-Bukhari 2311).
> - Teach it by ear first, one phrase at a time, linking each new phrase to the ones before.
> - Explain the meaning in simple words: Allah is alive, never tired, never sleeps, knows everything and owns everything.
> - Make it a habit: after salah and at bedtime every night.

## What is Ayatul Kursi?

Ayatul Kursi (“the Verse of the Throne,” also spelled Ayat al-Kursi) is the 255th ayah of Surah Al-Baqarah, the second surah of the Quran. It is named after the word *Kursi* that appears in it: “His Kursi extends over the heavens and the earth.” It describes who Allah is, His life, His knowledge and His power, in one ayah.

| Detail | Ayatul Kursi |
|---|---|
| Reference | Surah Al-Baqarah 2:255 |
| Juz | Juz 3 (which begins at 2:253) |
| Length | One ayah, nine short phrases |
| When it is recited | Before sleep and after the obligatory prayers |
| Good age to start | Listening from any age; memorizing often from 4–6, depending on the child |

## Why children should learn Ayatul Kursi: the authentic virtues

These are well-established narrations you can share with your child:

- **The greatest ayah.** The Prophet ﷺ asked Ubayy ibn Ka‘b which ayah in the Book of Allah was the greatest. Ubayy answered with Ayatul Kursi, and the Prophet ﷺ struck him on the chest and said, “May knowledge be pleasant for you” ([Sahih Muslim 810](https://sunnah.com/muslim:810)).
- **Protection at bedtime.** In a long narration, Abu Hurairah was taught that whoever recites Ayatul Kursi on going to bed will have a guard from Allah with him, and no devil will come near him until morning. The Prophet ﷺ confirmed that this was true ([Sahih al-Bukhari 2311](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:2311)). Children love this story, because Abu Hurairah caught the thief three times.
- **After the obligatory prayers.** Abu Umamah reported that whoever recites Ayatul Kursi after every obligatory prayer, nothing but death will prevent him from entering Paradise. It was reported by an-Nasa’i and graded authentic by Ibn Hibban and others ([Bulugh al-Maram 220](https://sunnah.com/bulugh/2/220)).

> **A note on accuracy:** a longer version of the “after every prayer” narration, attributed to ‘Ali and adding protection for the reciter’s house and neighbours, is graded weak; al-Bayhaqi himself said its chain is weak ([Mishkat al-Masabih 974](https://sunnah.com/mishkat:974)). Teach children the authentic narrations above.

## Ayatul Kursi: the full Arabic text

ٱللَّهُ لَآ إِلَـٰهَ إِلَّا هُوَ ٱلْحَىُّ ٱلْقَيُّومُ ۚ لَا تَأْخُذُهُۥ سِنَةٌ وَلَا نَوْمٌ ۚ لَّهُۥ مَا فِى ٱلسَّمَـٰوَٰتِ وَمَا فِى ٱلْأَرْضِ ۗ مَن ذَا ٱلَّذِى يَشْفَعُ عِندَهُۥٓ إِلَّا بِإِذْنِهِۦ ۚ يَعْلَمُ مَا بَيْنَ أَيْدِيهِمْ وَمَا خَلْفَهُمْ ۖ وَلَا يُحِيطُونَ بِشَىْءٍ مِّنْ عِلْمِهِۦٓ إِلَّا بِمَا شَآءَ ۚ وَسِعَ كُرْسِيُّهُ ٱلسَّمَـٰوَٰتِ وَٱلْأَرْضَ ۖ وَلَا يَـُٔودُهُۥ حِفْظُهُمَا ۚ وَهُوَ ٱلْعَلِىُّ ٱلْعَظِيمُ ﴿٢٥٥﴾

Read it slowly with your child while listening to a recitation on [Quran.com](https://quran.com/2/255). The small marks above some words show where a reciter may pause, and they match the nine phrases below.

## Ayatul Kursi for kids, phrase by phrase

Each phrase below has the Arabic, a simple transliteration and a meaning a child can understand. The formal translation is adapted from Saheeh International, as shown on Quran.com.

### 1. Allah is alive and looks after everything

ٱللَّهُ لَآ إِلَـٰهَ إِلَّا هُوَ ٱلْحَىُّ ٱلْقَيُّومُ

*Allaahu laaa ilaaha illaa Huwal-Hayyul-Qayyoom*

Allah: there is no god except Him, the Ever-Living, the One who keeps everything going. **For kids:** Allah is always alive, and He takes care of the whole world, all the time.

### 2. Allah never gets sleepy

لَا تَأْخُذُهُۥ سِنَةٌ وَلَا نَوْمٌ

*Laa ta’khudhuhoo sinatunw-wa laa nawm*

Neither drowsiness overtakes Him nor sleep. **For kids:** We get tired and need to sleep. Allah never gets sleepy and never sleeps.

### 3. Everything belongs to Allah

لَّهُۥ مَا فِى ٱلسَّمَـٰوَٰتِ وَمَا فِى ٱلْأَرْضِ

*Lahoo maa fis-samaawaati wa maa fil-ard*

To Him belongs whatever is in the heavens and whatever is on the earth. **For kids:** The sky, the stars, the oceans, the animals, and even our toys: all of it belongs to Allah.

### 4. No one speaks for others without His permission

مَن ذَا ٱلَّذِى يَشْفَعُ عِندَهُۥٓ إِلَّا بِإِذْنِهِۦ

*Man dhal-ladhee yashfa‘u ‘indahooo illaa bi-idhnih*

Who is it that can intercede with Him except by His permission? **For kids:** On the Day of Judgement, no one can ask Allah to help someone else unless Allah allows it.

### 5. Allah knows the past and the future

يَعْلَمُ مَا بَيْنَ أَيْدِيهِمْ وَمَا خَلْفَهُمْ

*Ya‘lamu maa bayna aydeehim wa maa khalfahum*

He knows what is before them and what will be after them. **For kids:** Allah knows everything that happened yesterday and everything that will happen tomorrow.

### 6. We only know what Allah teaches us

وَلَا يُحِيطُونَ بِشَىْءٍ مِّنْ عِلْمِهِۦٓ إِلَّا بِمَا شَآءَ

*Wa laa yuheetoona bi-shay’im-min ‘ilmiheee illaa bimaa shaaa’*

And they encompass nothing of His knowledge except what He wills. **For kids:** Everything we know, Allah taught us. He knows much, much more.

### 7. His Kursi is bigger than the heavens and the earth

وَسِعَ كُرْسِيُّهُ ٱلسَّمَـٰوَٰتِ وَٱلْأَرْضَ

*Wasi‘a Kursiyyuhus-samaawaati wal-ard*

His Kursi extends over the heavens and the earth. **For kids:** Allah’s Kursi is something great that belongs to Him, and it is bigger than the whole sky and the whole earth.

### 8. Looking after it all never tires Him

وَلَا يَـُٔودُهُۥ حِفْظُهُمَا

*Wa laa ya’ooduhoo hifzuhumaa*

And their preservation does not tire Him. **For kids:** Holding up the sky and the earth never makes Allah tired, not even a little.

### 9. Allah is the Most High, the Most Great

وَهُوَ ٱلْعَلِىُّ ٱلْعَظِيمُ

*Wa Huwal-‘Aliyyul-‘Azeem*

And He is the Most High, the Most Great. **For kids:** Nothing is higher or greater than Allah.

Transliteration is only a bridge. Letters like ح, ع, ق, ط and ظ have no English equivalent, so always pair it with audio and, ideally, a teacher.

## How to teach Ayatul Kursi to kids, step by step

**Step 1: Let them hear it every day.** Play it at bedtime and in the car for a week or two before formal teaching starts. Young children absorb the rhythm long before they learn the words.

**Step 2: Tell them what it means.** Use the “for kids” lines above. A child who knows they are saying “Allah never sleeps” recites it with feeling.

**Step 3: Teach phrase 1 only.** Say it slowly; your child repeats. Five to ten repetitions, spread across the day, are better than twenty in a row.

**Step 4: Add the next phrase, then link.** Once phrase 1 is solid, teach phrase 2, then recite 1 + 2 together. Always link the new phrase to everything before it.

**Step 5: Use the “three-sentence” checkpoints.** Phrases 1–3, 4–6 and 7–9 make three natural parts. Celebrate each part.

**Step 6: Recite the whole ayah together, then alone.** First in unison, then your child leads and you only help when they get stuck.

**Step 7: Have it checked.** Ask a teacher, imam or fluent reciter to listen once. Small errors (such as ح said as ه) are easy to fix early and hard to fix later. Our guide to [makharij for kids](/blog/makharij-for-kids) explains where each letter comes from.

**Step 8: Make it part of daily life.** After each salah, at bedtime with the three Quls, and whenever your child feels scared.

### A realistic pace by age

| Age | Pace | Roughly how long |
|---|---|---|
| 3–4 | Listening daily; one phrase every week or two | Several months, and that is fine |
| 5–6 | One phrase every 2–4 days | About 3–5 weeks |
| 7–9 | One or two phrases a day | About 1–2 weeks |
| 10+ | Two or three phrases a day | A few days to a week |

These are guides, not targets. Every child is different; consistency matters more than speed.

## Pronunciation trouble spots

These are the places where children (and adults) most often slip:

- **Al-Hayy (ٱلْحَىُّ):** the ح is a breathy sound from the middle of the throat, not an English “h.”
- **Al-Qayyoom (ٱلْقَيُّومُ):** ق comes from the very back of the tongue, deeper than “k.” The double ي is held.
- **‘indahu, ya‘lamu, ‘ilmihi, al-‘Aliyy, al-‘Azeem:** five words with ع. Practise this letter on its own first.
- **Al-‘Azeem (ٱلْعَظِيمُ):** the ظ is a heavy “th” (as in “this”) with the tongue between the teeth, not a “z.”
- **Long vowels:** “laaa ilaaha illaa” and “bimaa shaaa’” are stretched. Clapping the beats helps children hold them.

## Making Ayatul Kursi a daily habit

Memorizing it is the beginning; living with it is the goal. Three anchors work well for families:

1. **Bedtime.** Recite Ayatul Kursi together, then Al-Ikhlas, Al-Falaq and An-Nas. Our [Islamic bedtime routine](/blog/islamic-bedtime-routine) guide shows how to build this into a calm ten minutes.
2. **After salah.** Even a five-year-old who prays one or two prayers with you can recite it afterwards.
3. **Moments of fear.** When your child is scared of the dark or a storm, reciting it together reminds them that Allah never sleeps. For the duas that go with the rest of the day, see our list of [daily duas for kids](/blog/daily-duas-for-kids).

## Common mistakes parents make

- **Teaching the whole ayah at once.** It overwhelms young children. Nine phrases, one at a time.
- **Relying on transliteration alone.** It hides the throat letters and builds errors.
- **Skipping the meaning.** Without meaning, it becomes a string of sounds.
- **Over-explaining the Kursi.** Keep it simple and true: it is something great that belongs to Allah, bigger than the heavens and the earth. Leave detailed tafsir for when they are older.
- **Sharing weak virtues as facts.** Stick to the authentic narrations above.
