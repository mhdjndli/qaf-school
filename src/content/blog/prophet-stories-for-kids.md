Prophet stories for kids are the true accounts of the 25 prophets named in the Quran, told simply so that children come to love them and learn from them. Each prophet carries one clear lesson a child can hold onto: Nuh's patience, Ibrahim's courage, Yusuf's forgiveness, Yunus's dua. This guide lists all 25 with the lesson, the Quran reference and a line you can say at bedtime.

> **Key takeaways**
> - The Quran names 25 prophets and tells us Allah sent many more whose stories we were not told (Surah An-Nisa 4:164).
> - Give each prophet one memorable lesson. Young children remember a feeling and a picture, not a timeline.
> - Tell the stories from the Quran and authentic hadith, and say "alayhis salam" (peace be upon him) after each name.
> - Soften the frightening parts for under-sixes, and choose books and cartoons that do not draw the prophets' faces.
> - Start with Adam, Nuh, Ibrahim, Musa, Yunus and Muhammad ﷺ, then add the rest over a year.

## Why prophet stories matter for children

Allah Himself calls these accounts the best of stories:

نَحْنُ نَقُصُّ عَلَيْكَ أَحْسَنَ ٱلْقَصَصِ

"We relate to you the best of stories" ([Surah Yusuf 12:3](https://quran.com/12/3)). The same surah ends: "There was certainly in their stories a lesson for those of understanding" (12:111).

For a child, a prophet story is a safe way to meet big ideas. Fear, jealousy, loss and courage all appear, and every story ends with Allah's help. The Prophet ﷺ also taught that the prophets are like brothers whose "religion is one" ([Sahih al-Bukhari 3443](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:3443)), and the Quran teaches us to say, "We make no distinction between any of His messengers" (Surah Al-Baqarah 2:285). Children learn early that Musa and Isa (peace be upon them) belong to them too.

## Prophet stories for kids: all 25 with lessons

Each entry gives the story in a few lines and a sentence to say to your child. Read the verses yourself first so you tell the story as the Quran tells it.

### From Adam to Ibrahim

**1. Adam.** Allah made Adam, the first human, and taught him the names of all things (2:31). Adam and Hawwa made a mistake in the garden, and they turned straight back to Allah: "Our Lord, we have wronged ourselves" (7:23). *Say:* "Everyone makes mistakes. Adam teaches us to say sorry to Allah."

**2. Idris.** The Quran calls him "a man of truth and a prophet" and says Allah raised him to a high station (19:56–57). *Say:* "Allah loves people who always tell the truth."

**3. Nuh.** Nuh called his people to Allah for 950 years (29:14). Most did not listen, yet he kept going, built the ark as Allah taught him, and was saved with the believers and the animals. *Say:* "Nuh never gave up, even when it was hard."

**4. Hud.** The people of 'Ad were very strong and boasted, "Who is greater than us in strength?" (41:15). Hud reminded them that Allah, who made them, is stronger. *Say:* "Being strong is a gift from Allah, not a reason to show off."

**5. Salih.** Allah sent the people of Thamud a special she-camel as a sign, with a turn to drink on its own day (26:155). Salih asked them to leave her unharmed, but they broke the agreement (7:73–79). *Say:* "When we make a promise, we keep it."

### The family of Ibrahim

**6. Ibrahim.** As a young man, Ibrahim showed his people that their idols could not help anyone. When they threw him into a fire, Allah said, "O fire, be coolness and safety upon Abraham" (21:69). *Say:* "Ibrahim was brave because he knew Allah was with him."

**7. Lut.** Lut lived among people who did very bad things and would not stop. He kept telling them to do what is right, and Allah saved him (21:74). *Say:* "Even when everyone is doing something wrong, we can choose what is right."

**8. Ismail.** Ismail helped his father build the Ka'bah, and the two of them prayed, "Our Lord, accept this from us" (2:127). The Quran praises him as "true to his promise" (19:54). *Say:* "Ismail helped his dad with a big job for Allah."

**9. Ishaq.** The angels brought Ibrahim and his wife Sarah the good news of a son, Ishaq, when they were old, and Sarah smiled (11:71). He became "a prophet from among the righteous" (37:112). *Say:* "Allah gives us good things at just the right time."

**10. Yaqub.** When Yaqub lost his son Yusuf, he said, "Patience is most fitting" (*fa sabrun jamil*, 12:18). He also told his children to stay on Allah's religion all their lives (2:132). *Say:* "When we feel sad, we can be patient and trust Allah."

**11. Yusuf.** Yusuf's brothers were jealous and threw him into a well. Years later, when Allah had made him powerful, he forgave them: "No blame will there be upon you today" (12:92). *Say:* "Yusuf forgave his brothers. We can forgive too."

### Prophets of patience and fairness

**12. Ayyub.** Ayyub was very sick for a long time and lost much of what he had. He called on Allah, "You are the most merciful of the merciful," and Allah healed him and gave him back his family (21:83–84). *Say:* "When we're sick, we can be patient and make dua."

**13. Shu'ayb.** Shu'ayb told the people of Madyan to measure and weigh fairly and not cheat anyone (7:85). *Say:* "Muslims are honest when they share, trade and play games."

**14. Musa.** Baby Musa's mother placed him in the river, trusting Allah's promise to bring him back (28:7). Later Musa faced Pharaoh, and Allah parted the sea for him (26:63). Before his big task he prayed, رَبِّ ٱشْرَحْ لِى صَدْرِى وَيَسِّرْ لِىٓ أَمْرِى, "My Lord, expand for me my chest and ease for me my task" (20:25–26). *Say:* "When something feels scary, we ask Allah to make it easy."

**15. Harun.** Musa asked Allah to make his brother Harun his helper (20:29–30), and Allah told them both to speak to Pharaoh "with gentle speech" (20:44). *Say:* "Good helpers are kind and speak gently."

**16. Dhul-Kifl.** The Quran mentions him among "the patient" and "the outstanding" (21:85, 38:48), with few other details. *Say:* "Allah remembers people who are patient."

### The kings and the callers

**17. Dawud.** Allah gave Dawud the Zabur and a voice so beautiful the mountains and birds glorified Allah with him, and He made iron soft in his hands (34:10). The Prophet ﷺ said Dawud ate from the earnings of his own work ([Sahih al-Bukhari 2072](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:2072)). *Say:* "Dawud was a king who still worked with his hands."

**18. Sulayman.** Allah taught Sulayman the speech of birds (27:16). When an ant warned the others to hide from his army, he smiled and thanked Allah (27:18–19). *Say:* "Sulayman was careful even with tiny ants, and he said thank you to Allah."

**19. Ilyas.** Ilyas asked his people why they prayed to an idol called Ba'l and left Allah, "the best of creators" (37:125). *Say:* "We only worship Allah."

**20. Al-Yasa.** He is named with the prophets whom Allah preferred (6:86) and among "the outstanding" (38:48). *Say:* "Allah chose Al-Yasa to be one of the very best people."

**21. Yunus.** Yunus left his people in anger and ended up inside a huge fish. In the darkness he called, لَّآ إِلَـٰهَ إِلَّآ أَنتَ سُبْحَـٰنَكَ إِنِّى كُنتُ مِنَ ٱلظَّـٰلِمِينَ, "There is no deity except You; exalted are You. Indeed, I have been of the wrongdoers" (21:87). The Prophet ﷺ said that any Muslim who calls on Allah with this dua is answered ([Jami' at-Tirmidhi 3505](https://sunnah.com/tirmidhi:3505), graded sahih). *Say:* "Allah heard Yunus even inside a fish, so He always hears you."

### The final prophets

**22. Zakariya.** Zakariya was old and had no children, but he kept making dua, saying he had never been disappointed in calling on Allah (19:4). Allah gave him Yahya. The Prophet ﷺ said Zakariya was a carpenter (Sahih Muslim 2379). *Say:* "Keep asking Allah, even for big things."

**23. Yahya.** Allah gave Yahya wisdom while still a boy, and he was "dutiful to his parents" (19:12–14). *Say:* "Even children can be wise and good to their parents."

**24. Isa.** Isa was born to Maryam without a father, by Allah's command, and spoke as a baby: "Indeed, I am the servant of Allah" (19:30). He healed the sick by Allah's permission (3:49), and Allah raised him up to Himself (4:158). *Say:* "Isa is a great prophet of Allah, and Muslims love him."

**25. Muhammad ﷺ.** The last of the prophets (33:40), sent "as a mercy to the worlds" (21:107). He was an orphan, a shepherd (every prophet herded sheep, [Sahih al-Bukhari 2262](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:2262)), and the kindest of people. *Say:* "Our Prophet ﷺ was kind to children, animals and everyone."

## How to tell prophet stories to young children

**Tell it from the Quran first.** Many popular retellings add details from unreliable sources. Keep to what the Quran and authentic hadith say, and when a detail isn't known, say, "Allah didn't tell us that part."

**Use respect words.** Say *alayhis salam* after each prophet's name and *sallallahu alayhi wa sallam* (ﷺ) after Muhammad's. Children copy the habit quickly.

**Avoid pictures of the prophets.** Mainstream scholars hold that the prophets should not be drawn or depicted. Good children's books show the ark, the fish or the ants, never the prophet's face. Our guides to [Islamic books for kids](/blog/best-islamic-books-for-kids) and [Islamic cartoons for kids](/blog/best-islamic-cartoons-for-kids) flag this.

**Soften the scary parts.** For under-sixes, focus on how Allah saved the believers rather than on punishment. "The people who didn't listen were not saved" is enough at four.

**Finish with one question.** "When did you need to be patient like Ayyub today?"

> **Tip:** Children ask for the same story again and again. That is how they learn. Let Yunus be the bedtime story for a whole week before moving on.

## Prophet stories by age

| Age | What works | Start with |
|---|---|---|
| 2–3 | One-minute stories, props (toy boat, plush fish), one sentence of lesson | Nuh, Yunus, Sulayman and the ant |
| 4–5 | Three-to-five-minute stories, simple sequence, acting it out | Adam, Ibrahim, Musa, Yusuf, Muhammad ﷺ |
| 6–7 | Full story arc, the dua each prophet made, linking to Quran verses | Ayyub, Zakariya, Shu'ayb, Dawud |
| 8–12 | Reading the verses with translation, comparing prophets, lessons for real life | All 25, plus the five "of determination" (33:7, 46:35) |

## Activities that bring the stories to life

- **Nuh's ark sort.** Pair toy animals two by two into a box ark while you tell the story.
- **Sulayman's ant walk.** Look for ants outside and practise stepping carefully around them.
- **Yunus's dua jar.** Write the dua of Yunus on a card. Each time your child uses it when scared or sorry, add a pebble.
- **Prophet of the month.** Put a name card on the fridge, tell that story weekly, and end the month with a short quiz and a treat.

## Bringing it all together

You don't need to master all 25 at once. Pick one prophet, tell the story from the Quran in simple words, give it one lesson and one dua, and let your child hear it many times. Over a year, those 25 stories become 25 friends your child can turn to: Yunus when they are scared, Yusuf when they are hurt, Ayyub when they are sick and Muhammad ﷺ every single day.
