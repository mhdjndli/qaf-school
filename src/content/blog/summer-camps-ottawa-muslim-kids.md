An Islamic summer camp in Ottawa usually means a masjid-run day camp: Quran and Islamic lessons in the morning, then sports, crafts, swimming and field trips, with prayer in congregation built into the day. In summer 2026, at least eight Muslim day camps ran across Barrhaven, Kanata, Stittsville, Hunt Club, the west end and Orléans, plus two overnight camps in the Ottawa Valley. Most were advertised in spring, and several sold out. This guide lists what ran in 2026, what it cost, and how to plan for summer 2027.

> - Weekly-priced masjid day camps charged about $125 to $180 a week in 2026, and session-based camps were often cheaper per week ($200 to $400 for three to five weeks).

## Masjid day camps by area

### Barrhaven and Nepean

**SNMC Masjid Summer Camp** at 3020 Woodroffe Ave ran two blocks in 2026: Camp A in July and Camp B in August, Monday to Friday from 9 AM to 3:30 PM. The day included Quran classes, congregational prayer, lessons on iman and worship, and outdoor games. Families could register for one block, not both, and fees were non-refundable.

The **MAC Youth Junior Summer Camp** moved to the Barrhaven Islamic Centre at 3971 Greenbank Rd for August 2026. Its program listed a daily Quran program and Islamic reminders, field trips and workshops, a swimming program, sports and crafts, with paid before- and after-care.

The **OISL Kids Summer Camp**, run by the Ottawa Islamic Soccer League team (listed as the Ottawa Islamic Sports Association), ran all summer at Wazoson Public School on Kilbirnie Drive. It is the best fit for sporty kids: soccer, basketball and team games, plus Quran and Islamic learning. It was also the most flexible, with daily and weekly rates.

### West end, Kanata and Stittsville

MAC's camp ran in July at Abraar Secondary School, 1085 Grenon Ave, for ages 4 to 13 at $180 a week.

The [Kanata Muslim Association](https://www.kanatamuslims.ca) ran two camps. Its full-day masjid camp at 351 Sandhill Rd included Quran and Islamic lessons, workshops, water activities and weekly field trips, with a cheaper four-day option without trips. Its new afternoon **Hikmah Camp** in Stittsville was a half-day program from noon to 4 PM, which can work well for families with a parent at home in the mornings. KMA's July weeks sold out in 2026, with a waitlist.

The [Stittsville Muslim Association](https://stittsvillemuslims.ca/) camp at 1469 Stittsville Main St ran under the theme "Character of a Believer," mixing Quran, STEM, sports and field trips, with before- and after-care for an extra fee and a sibling discount.

### Hunt Club and Ottawa South

The Assunnah Muslims Association's **Iqraa camp** at 1216 Hunt Club Rd was the most academic option on the list. It focused on Quran memorization and Islamic studies, including seerah, aqidah and acts of worship, adjusted to each child's level. Choose it if your main goal is Quran progress over the summer.

### Orléans and the east end

[Masjid Bilal](https://www.masjidbilal.org/summer-camp/), run by the Islamic Society of Cumberland, offered two three-week sessions from 9:30 AM to 2:30 PM, with games, crafts and field trips to Ottawa museums. Pricing was per session with a family cap, which makes it one of the better values for larger families. It is the main Islamic summer camp option for east-end families.

## Overnight Muslim camps near Ottawa

Overnight camp is a different experience: cabins, canoes, campfires and Fajr by the lake. Two Muslim-run camps use **Camp Smitty in Eganville**, in the Ottawa Valley.

- [Muslim Youth of Ottawa](https://myo.camp/) is volunteer-led and has run programs since the 1980s. In 2026 it held a leader-in-training session and a youth camp for boys and girls aged 9 to 19, and it offers camp subsidies.
- [Camp Deen](https://www.campdeen.com) is a not-for-profit Muslim camp first established in 1973. Its 2026 session ran August 23 to 29, and cabins and waterfront activities are separated by gender.

If your child has never slept away from home, start with MYO's monthly hikes or a short session. A child who has met a few campers beforehand settles in much faster.

## What do Islamic summer camps cost?

In 2026, weekly-priced masjid day camps in Ottawa charged roughly **$125 to $180 per week**. Session-based camps were often cheaper per week: Masjid Bilal charged $200 for a three-week session, and SNMC $280 to $380 for a block of three to four weeks. A full eight-week summer ran from about $795 to $999 at camps that offered it.

Ways to keep costs down:

- **Ask about sibling discounts.** Several camps, including SMA and KMA, offered them.
- **Book the full session.** Multi-week and full-summer rates were noticeably cheaper per week.
- **Ask about subsidies.** MYO advertises subsidies, and many masjids will quietly help a family that asks.
- **Watch for early-bird pricing.** SMA's 2026 early-bird full-summer rate was $750 instead of $795.
- **Check refund rules.** Some camps charged cancellation fees from mid-June, gave no refunds once camp started, or were non-refundable from the start.

## How to choose an Islamic summer camp in Ottawa

Every camp above combines faith and fun, but the balance varies. Decide what matters most to you, then ask these questions.

### 1. What does the Quran time actually look like?

Ask how many minutes a day, who teaches it, and whether children are grouped by level. A camp that splits kids into a beginners' qaida group and a memorization group will get more done than one that recites together.

### 2. How are boys and girls grouped?

Most Ottawa masjid day camps in 2026 were mixed, with boys and girls together. Ask whether activities, swimming and prayer are separated for older children, if that matters to your family.

### 3. Who are the counsellors?

Many masjid camps are staffed partly by teenage and university-age counsellors, and several Ottawa Muslim organizations have received Canada Summer Jobs funding to hire them. That can be wonderful: kids look up to young Muslim role models. Ask how many adults supervise, whether staff have first aid training and police checks, and who is in charge on site.

### 4. What happens on field trips and swim days?

Ask where the trips go, how children travel, and the ratio on outings. For swimming, ask about modest swimwear and how changing is supervised.

### 5. How are prayers handled?

In July, Dhuhr in Ottawa falls around 1:15 PM and Asr in the late afternoon, so a full-day camp usually includes Dhuhr in congregation. That is one of the biggest benefits of an Islamic camp: your child prays with friends every day without having to ask.

### 6. Does the schedule fit your work day?

A 9-to-3 camp with no after-care is hard for two working parents. Compare hours and extended care before you compare prices.

## A sample summer plan for 2027

Many families mix camp types across the summer. One approach:

1. **Late June to July:** a masjid day camp for the Quran and friendship (July weeks fill first, so book these early).
2. **Early August:** a sports or specialty camp, such as a city camp, a soccer camp or a STEM program. See our guide to [STEM programs for kids in Ottawa](/blog/stem-programs-for-kids-ottawa).
3. **Mid-August:** a few nights at an overnight Muslim camp for ages 9 and up.
4. **Last week of August:** a home week to reset routines, review surahs and prepare for school.
