Muslim kids in public school in Ontario can pray, fast, wear hijab, eat halal and take Eid off, because schools have a legal duty to accommodate religious practice. What makes the difference is how well parents prepare: knowing what to ask for, talking to the teacher early, and building strong faith at home and on weekends. This guide covers prayer, Ramadan, Eid, food, dress, the health curriculum and friendships, with a step-by-step plan for requesting accommodation.

> **Key takeaways**
> - Ontario schools must accommodate students' religious practices up to the point of undue hardship, and every board must have a religious accommodation guideline.
> - Prayer, Ramadan fasting, Eid absences, halal food, modest dress and exemption from specific health lessons can all be arranged. Ask early and in writing.
> - In Ottawa's short winter days, Dhuhr (and sometimes Asr) falls during school hours, so a prayer plan matters most from about November to February.
> - Faith at home and a weekly Islamic program give public-school kids the knowledge, friends and confidence to stay strong.

## Your child's rights in Ontario schools

Every Ontario school board must have an equity and inclusive education policy that includes a guideline on religious accommodation. That requirement comes from the Ministry of Education's [Policy/Program Memorandum 119](https://www.ontario.ca/document/education-ontario-policy-and-program-direction/policyprogram-memorandum-119), which ties it to the duty to accommodate under the Ontario Human Rights Code.

The [Ontario Human Rights Commission's policy statement on religious accommodation in schools](https://www.ohrc.on.ca/en/policy-statement-religious-accommodation-schools) explains how this works in practice. Schools must accommodate creed-related needs up to the point of undue hardship, and only three factors count: cost, outside sources of funding, and health and safety. Inconvenience is not a valid reason to refuse.

Accommodation is a conversation, not a demand. The best outcome is one that respects your child's faith and keeps them included with their classmates. Most teachers want to help; they simply need to know what your child needs.

**Catholic schools:** Catholic boards are publicly funded but faith-based, with religious education and prayer built into the day. Many Muslim families attend them happily, but expectations differ. Ask the principal directly how they handle prayer, Ramadan and religious activities before you register. Ottawa has both public and Catholic boards in English and French, so it is worth comparing before you register.

## Praying at school

Prayer is often the first accommodation families need, and the one that matters most to children who are learning to own their salah.

### When prayer falls during school hours

School usually runs from mid-morning to mid-afternoon, so Dhuhr is the main prayer that overlaps. In Ottawa, the overlap is greatest in winter: once clocks go back in November, Dhuhr falls around midday, and on the shortest days Asr can begin before dismissal. In late spring, Dhuhr shifts later and may fall after school or at lunch.

Check a local prayer timetable each season and share the relevant window with the teacher.

### What a prayer accommodation can look like

The OHRC's guidance gives examples such as a private space for prayer, access to a nearby washroom for washing before prayer, and not requiring participation in school activities during obligatory prayer. In practice, for a young child this might be:

- Praying Dhuhr at lunch recess in a quiet corner of the library or an empty classroom.
- A small travel prayer mat kept in their cubby or backpack.
- A teacher who knows that a five-minute absence after lunch is for salah.

For high school students, many schools have a Muslim Students' Association that organizes a regular prayer space and Friday prayer. The OHRC describes one example of a high school providing a designated private space for weekly Friday congregational prayer.

**Tip:** Teach your child to make wudu quickly and tidily at a school sink, and to carry a small towel. Teachers notice and appreciate it. Our guide on how to [teach kids salah](/blog/teach-kids-salah) includes a short checklist for praying away from home.

### Young children

Children in JK to Grade 2 are not yet required to pray, so many families let them pray at home and introduce school prayer gradually around ages 7 to 10. The goal is a positive association, not pressure.

## Ramadan and Eid at school

Ramadan 2027 is expected to begin around early February, with Eid al-Fitr expected around March 9 or 10, 2027, depending on moon sighting. That puts the whole month inside the school term, during Ottawa's late-winter days.

### Fasting children

If your child is fasting all or part of the day:

- **Tell the teacher in writing** a week or two before Ramadan, including whether your child is fasting fully, partly, or practising.
- **Ask for a quiet place at lunch** such as the library, so they are not sitting with food in front of them.
- **Discuss physical activity.** Light participation is usually fine, but a fasting child may need to sit out intense running.
- **Ask the teacher to let you know** if your child seems unwell; breaking a practice fast is perfectly fine for children.

For age-by-age guidance on how much to fast, see our guide to a child's [first Ramadan fast](/blog/first-ramadan-fast-for-kids).

### Sharing Ramadan with the class

Many teachers welcome a short presentation or story about Ramadan. A simple book read-aloud, a lantern craft, or dates for the class (after checking allergy rules) turns your child's month into something classmates respect rather than question.

### Eid at school

Most Muslim families keep children home on Eid, and Ontario boards accommodate this. The Ottawa-Carleton District School Board publishes a [calendar of religious and creed-based days of observance](https://www.ocdsb.ca/news-events/calendar/religious-creed-based-days-of-observance) and says it avoids scheduling events, evaluations and field trips on many of these dates, including the evening before observances that begin at sundown.

Because Eid depends on moon sighting, tell the teacher about both possible dates in advance and confirm the night before. Ask for any missed work to be sent home, and let your child bring in a small treat to share when they return.

## Food, dress and physical education

### Food, parties and school events

Food is where many small, everyday challenges come up: pizza lunches, class parties, cooking units and treats.

- **In September,** let the teacher know your child eats halal only, and ask to be told about food events ahead of time.
- **For pizza or hot-lunch days,** cheese or vegetarian options often work; check how the program handles meat.
- **For parties,** offer to send a halal treat for your child, or volunteer to help plan.
- **Gelatin and marshmallows** are common hidden issues in candy. Pack a few halal alternatives in a labelled bag the teacher can keep.

### Dress, modesty and PE

Students can wear religious dress such as hijab at school. The OHRC's [Policy on preventing discrimination based on creed](https://www.ohrc.on.ca/en/policy-preventing-discrimination-based-creed) treats dress codes as an area where sincerely held religious practice must be accommodated up to the point of undue hardship. Practical issues usually come up in physical education:

- **Hijab in sports:** Sports hijabs are designed for movement and are widely available.
- **PE clothing:** Leggings or track pants under shorts are generally fine; ask the teacher if unsure.
- **Changing rooms:** Older students can ask for a private place to change.
- **Swimming units:** Ask about full-coverage swimwear or an alternative activity.

## Curriculum, celebrations and friendships

### The health curriculum

In Ontario, parents can request that their child be exempted from the Human Development and Sexual Health expectations in the Grades 1 to 8 Health and Physical Education curriculum. Under [Policy/Program Memorandum 162](https://www.ontario.ca/document/education-ontario-policy-and-program-direction/policyprogram-memorandum-162), schools must notify parents at least 20 school days before that instruction, and parents return a signed form each school year.

The exemption covers all of that grade's expectations in this area, not selected lessons, and there is no academic penalty. You choose whether your child stays in class without taking part, goes to another supervised space in the school, or is released to you.

Whether you choose an exemption or not, talk with your child at home about these topics in a way that reflects your values, at an age-appropriate level. Many families find it helps to review the curriculum overview first so they know what is coming.

### Holidays, celebrations and friendships

Public schools mark many celebrations through the year, from Halloween to Christmas to Valentine's Day. Each family draws its own lines. Some participate in the secular parts and skip religious activities; others ask for alternatives. Our guide to [Christmas at school for Muslim families](/blog/christmas-at-school-muslim-families) walks through respectful approaches by age.

Friendships matter even more. Encourage your child to invite classmates home, to be the kind friend who includes others, and to explain their faith simply when asked: "I pray five times a day," "We fast in Ramadan," "Eid is our celebration." Children who can say this calmly are rarely teased for long.

If your child experiences bullying or Islamophobic comments, write down what happened, contact the teacher and principal promptly, and ask what steps the school will take. Follow up in writing if needed.

## Step-by-step: requesting a religious accommodation

**Step 1: Know what you need.** List the specific accommodations, such as Dhuhr prayer at lunch, Eid absences, or fasting support.

**Step 2: Talk to the teacher first.** A friendly conversation at the start of the year solves most issues.

**Step 3: Put it in writing.** Send a short email summarizing what you agreed, with expected dates for Ramadan and Eid.

**Step 4: Ask about the board's guideline.** If something is unclear, ask the principal for the board's religious accommodation guideline.

**Step 5: Review before each season.** Check in again before winter prayer times and before Ramadan.

**Step 6: Escalate calmly if needed.** If a reasonable request is refused, speak with the principal, then the superintendent, keeping records of each conversation.

## Building strong faith outside school hours

Accommodations protect your child's practice, but they do not teach it. That happens at home, at the masjid, and in weekly programs where your child learns alongside other Muslim children.

- **A short daily routine:** Ten minutes of Quran after school or before bed keeps reading and memorization moving.
- **A weekly program:** A weekend school or Sunday program gives children knowledge, Muslim friends and mentors.
