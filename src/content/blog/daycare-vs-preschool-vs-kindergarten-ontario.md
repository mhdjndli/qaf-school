The difference between daycare vs preschool vs kindergarten in Ontario comes down to age, hours, cost and who regulates them. **Daycare** (licensed child care) is full-day care for children from infancy, licensed under the Child Care and Early Years Act. **Preschool** usually means a shorter, play-based program for children about 2.5 to 4, often run by a licensed child care centre. **Kindergarten** is the free, two-year school program (JK and SK) that begins the September your child is four. Many Ontario children move through all three, and the overlaps are where parents get confused.

> **Key takeaways**
> - Daycare and preschool are child care, licensed by the Ministry of Education under the Child Care and Early Years Act, 2014. Kindergarten is part of the school system.
> - Kindergarten in Ontario public and Catholic schools is free and full-day for children who turn four (JK) or five (SK) by December 31.
> - Through the Canada-wide Early Learning and Child Care (CWELCC) system, fees at enrolled licensed programs for children under six are capped at $22 a day, about $19 on average. Ontario has not yet reached $10 a day.
> - Licensed centres must meet minimum staff-to-child ratios, such as 1:5 for toddlers and 1:8 for preschoolers.
> - Once your child starts JK, they may still need licensed before- and after-school care, which can also qualify for fee reductions.

## What is daycare in Ontario?

Daycare is the everyday name for **licensed child care**: care for children under 13 provided by a licensed child care centre or a licensed home child care agency. In Ontario, child care is regulated by the Ministry of Education under the [Child Care and Early Years Act, 2014](https://www.ontario.ca/laws/statute/14c11) and its regulations.

There are two main licensed options, according to Ontario's [types of child care page](https://www.ontario.ca/page/types-child-care):

- **Licensed child care centres**, which care for infants, toddlers, preschoolers and sometimes school-age children in group rooms.
- **Licensed home child care**, where an individual provider, overseen by a licensed agency, cares for up to six children under 13 (including their own children under four) in a private home, with no more than three under two.

There is also **unlicensed child care**. Unlicensed providers are not inspected, though the ministry investigates complaints, and they may care for no more than five children under 13, including their own children under four, with no more than three under two.

### Staff-to-child ratios in licensed centres

Ratios are one of the biggest quality differences between options. Ontario's [child care centre licensing manual](https://www.ontario.ca/document/child-care-centre-licensing-manual/part-3-ratios-and-group-size) sets these minimums:

| Age group | Staff-to-child ratio | Maximum group size |
|---|---|---|
| Infant (under 18 months) | 3:10 | 10 |
| Toddler (18 to 30 months) | 1:5 | 15 |
| Preschool (30 months and up) | 1:8 | 24 |
| Kindergarten age | 1:13 | 26 |
| Primary/junior school age | 1:15 | 30 |

These are minimums, maintained at all times except for limited reduced-ratio periods such as rest time. Infant rooms never qualify for reduced ratios.

## What is preschool in Ontario?

"Preschool" has two meanings in Ontario, which is part of the confusion.

**First, it is a licensing age group.** In a child care centre, the preschool room serves children from 30 months (2.5 years) until they start kindergarten. A full-day daycare and a "preschool" can be the same building with the same licence.

**Second, it is a type of program.** Many parents use "preschool" or "nursery school" to mean a shorter, part-day, play-based program, often a few mornings a week, for children about 2.5 to 4. These are typically licensed child care centres too, with the same ratios. Some co-operative and Montessori programs fall in this category.

A preschool is the right choice when you want your child to experience a group setting, routines, and early social skills before JK, but you do not need full-day care. It is also a gentle introduction to separation for children who have been home with a parent.

### Is a "Montessori preschool" different?

Many Montessori programs for children aged 2.5 to 6 (often called "Casa" or "Children's House") operate as licensed child care centres. A Montessori school that enrols four- and five-year-olds may instead be a private school offering kindergarten. Ask whether the program is licensed child care or a private school, because it affects ratios, oversight and whether CWELCC fee reductions apply. ## What is kindergarten in Ontario?

Kindergarten is a free, two-year, full-day school program for four- and five-year-olds, according to [Ontario's kindergarten page](https://www.ontario.ca/page/kindergarten). Junior Kindergarten (JK) is the first year and Senior Kindergarten (SK) is the second. Children are eligible for JK in September of the year they turn four, as long as they turn four by December 31.

Kindergarten is not compulsory. Ontario requires children to attend school starting in September of the year they turn six. But most families enrol in JK, and for good reason: it is free, full-day and built around the provincial Kindergarten Program.

Key features:

- **Educator team:** Classes with more than 16 children are led by a certified teacher and an early childhood educator.
- **Play-based learning:** Since September 2026 the curriculum (The Ontario Curriculum, Kindergarten, 2026) is organized into four strands: Belonging and Contributing, Self-Regulation and Well-Being, Foundations of Language and Mathematics, and Problem Solving and Innovating. We explain each in [The Ontario Kindergarten Program explained](/blog/ontario-kindergarten-program-explained).
- **School hours:** Kindergarten follows the school day and calendar, so many families still need care before and after school and in the summer.

Private kindergartens, including Islamic kindergartens, are also an option. They charge tuition and set their own hours. QAF's full-day JK/SK, for example, runs 9:00 a.m. to 3:00 p.m., Monday to Friday, from September to June, and follows Ontario's four frames with daily Arabic and Quran added.

## CWELCC and $10-a-day child care: where things stand

The **Canada-wide Early Learning and Child Care (CWELCC)** system is a federal-provincial agreement to lower licensed child care fees for children under six. Ontario joined in 2022 with a goal of $10-a-day average fees by March 2026.

Here is where it stands as of fall 2026:

- **Fee cap:** Since January 1, 2025, fees at licensed programs enrolled in CWELCC have been capped at a maximum of $22 a day for eligible children, bringing the average down to about $19 a day.
- **$10 a day not yet reached:** The March 2026 target was missed. On September 29, 2026, [Canada and Ontario announced a four-year extension](https://www.canada.ca/en/employment-social-development/news/2026/09/canada-and-ontario-agree-to-four-year-extension-of-canadaontario-canada-wide-early-learning-and-child-care-agreement.html) with nearly $12 billion in federal funding from 2027 to 2031. Ontario committed to keeping average fees at $19 a day until at least June 30, 2027. The governments said they would keep discussing long-term fees.
- **Who qualifies:** Children under six in a licensed centre or licensed home child care program that has enrolled in CWELCC. Children who turn six in the first half of the year generally stay eligible until June 30 if they are in a preschool, kindergarten or family age group program.
- **Not all programs are enrolled.** Many licensed providers have joined, but not all. Unenrolled and unlicensed programs charge market rates.

Ask each program for a written breakdown of its daily fee and anything charged separately.

### Fee subsidy on top of CWELCC

Families with lower incomes may pay even less through a **child care fee subsidy**, which is calculated on family income and can bring fees close to zero. In Ottawa, subsidy is managed by the City. ## How they fit together: a typical timeline

Most Ontario families follow some version of this path:

**Step 1: Parental leave (0 to about 12 or 18 months).** Many children are at home through the first year.

**Step 2: Daycare or home child care (about 12 months to 4).** Licensed infant and toddler spaces are the hardest to find, so join waitlists early, ideally during pregnancy.

**Step 3: Preschool room or part-day program (2.5 to 4).** Children either move up within their daycare or join a part-day preschool.

**Step 4: JK at age 4.** Your child starts kindergarten the September of the year they turn four. Our [junior kindergarten guide](/blog/junior-kindergarten-ottawa-guide) covers registration.

**Step 5: Before- and after-school care (4+).** In schools offering kindergarten to Grade 6, Ontario requires boards to provide before- and after-school programs where there is enough demand. These are often licensed and, for children under six, can qualify for CWELCC reductions.

### Born late in the year?

Ontario's cut-off is December 31, so a child born in December starts JK at three years and eight or nine months, among the youngest in the class. Because JK is optional, some families wait a year; in that case, your child would normally join SK with their age group the following September rather than start in JK. Our [JK readiness checklist](/blog/jk-readiness-checklist) can help you think it through.

## How to choose: questions that matter

Labels matter less than what happens in the room. When you visit any daycare, preschool or kindergarten, ask:

- **Is it licensed?** Ask to see the licence, and check the program on Ontario's online list of licensed child care.
- **What are the ratios and group sizes in my child's room?**
- **Who are the staff?** Ask how many are Registered Early Childhood Educators or certified teachers.
- **What does a typical day look like?** Look for long stretches of play, outdoor time and calm routines.
- **What are the full costs?** Include registration, meals, late fees, and whether CWELCC applies.
- **How do you handle food, prayer and religious holidays?** For Muslim families, ask about halal meals or packed lunches, and how Eid is handled.

## The Ottawa angle

In Ottawa, you apply for licensed centres, licensed home child care, nursery schools and before- and after-school programs through the City's **Ottawa Child Care Registry and Waitlist (OCCRW)**. You create one family profile, choose programs, and wait for offers. Unlicensed home child care is not on the Registry. The City also manages child care fee subsidy. Ottawa's four school boards all offer free full-day kindergarten, and most schools host before- and after-school programs that you also apply for through the registry. Our guide to [Ottawa school boards compared](/blog/ottawa-school-boards-compared) explains the kindergarten differences between boards.

## Conclusion

Daycare, preschool and kindergarten are three stages, not three competing choices. Daycare provides full-day licensed care from infancy. Preschool gives children aged 2.5 to 4 a group setting, often part-day. Kindergarten begins at four, is free in public and Catholic schools, and starts formal schooling through play. With CWELCC capping fees at $22 a day for enrolled programs, licensed care before JK is more affordable than it was, but waitlists are long, so plan early.
