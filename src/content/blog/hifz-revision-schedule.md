A good hifz revision schedule divides every session into three parts: **sabaq** (the new lesson), **sabqi** (recent lessons) and **manzil** (older memorization on a rotating cycle). The amounts change as your child memorizes more: a beginner might revise one or two older surahs a day, while a student with ten juz might revise a full juz daily. This guide gives ready-to-use revision tables by stage, a sample week, the traditional seven-manzil cycle and what to do when revision falls behind.

> **Key takeaways**
> - Every hifz session should include sabaq, sabqi and manzil, in that priority order when time is short: protect revision first.
> - As memorization grows, manzil takes a bigger share of the time. Beginners might spend half their time on revision; advanced students often spend most of it.
> - A common target is cycling through everything memorized every 1–2 weeks, adjusted by the teacher.
> - When revision weakens, pause new lessons. It is faster to strengthen than to re-memorize.

## What is a hifz revision schedule?

**A hifz revision schedule is a fixed plan for how much new Quran a student memorizes each day and which older portions they revise, so that everything memorized is recited regularly and not forgotten.** It is the difference between memorizing the Quran and keeping it.

The Prophet ﷺ said: “It is a bad thing that some of you say, ‘I have forgotten such-and-such verse of the Qur’an,’ for indeed, he has been caused (by Allah) to forget it. So you must keep on reciting the Qur’an because it escapes from the hearts of men faster than camel do” ([Sahih al-Bukhari 5032](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:5032)).

Revision is also the prophetic pattern. Jibril reviewed the Quran with the Prophet ﷺ once every year, and twice in the year he passed away ([Sahih al-Bukhari 4998](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:4998)). If revision was built into the Prophet’s ﷺ relationship with the Quran, it belongs in every child’s routine too.

## Sabaq, sabqi and manzil explained

| Part | Meaning | What it covers | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Sabaq** | New lesson | Today’s new lines or page | Moves memorization forward |
| **Sabqi** | Recent revision | Roughly the last 5–10 lessons, or the current juz | Locks new lessons in before they fade |
| **Manzil** | Older revision | Everything memorized before the sabqi, on a rotation | Keeps the whole hifz alive |

Different schools use slightly different definitions, especially for sabqi, so ask your child’s teacher how they apply each term. The principle is the same everywhere: new, recent and old, every day.

For the basics of how a young child starts this cycle, see our guide to [Quran memorization for kids](/blog/quran-memorization-for-kids). This article focuses on the schedule itself as memorization grows.

## Hifz revision schedule by stage

Use this table as a starting point, then let the teacher fine-tune it. “Pages” refers to the standard 604-page, 15-line mushaf.

| Amount memorized | Daily sabaq | Daily sabqi | Daily manzil | Full revision cycle |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Juz Amma stage** (under 1 juz) | 1–3 short ayat or a few lines | This week’s lessons | 1–2 older surahs | All known surahs every week |
| **1–3 juz** | 3 lines to half a page | Last 5–7 lessons | A quarter to half a juz | Every 1–2 weeks |
| **4–10 juz** | Half a page to 1 page | Last 7–10 lessons | Half a juz to 1 juz | Every 1–2 weeks |
| **11–20 juz** | Half a page to 1 page | Current juz | 1–2 juz | About every 10–14 days |
| **21–30 juz** | Half a page to 1 page | Current juz | 2–3 juz | About every 10 days |
| **Completed hafiz** | None | Weak pages flagged by the teacher | 1 manzil (about a seventh) to 3 juz | Every 7–10 days |

Notice what happens as you move down the table: the sabaq stays roughly the same size, while the manzil grows. That is intentional. The more your child knows, the more of each session revision should take.

### How to split the time

A simple guideline for dividing each session:

| Stage | Sabaq | Sabqi | Manzil |
|---|---|---|---|
| Beginners (Juz Amma) | About 50% | About 25% | About 25% |
| 1–5 juz | About 35% | About 30% | About 35% |
| 6–15 juz | About 25% | About 25% | About 50% |
| 16–30 juz | About 20% | About 20% | About 60% |

For a part-time student with 30 minutes on a school night, that might mean 10 minutes on sabaq, 10 on sabqi and 10 on manzil at the early stages, shifting to 6, 6 and 18 later on. Full-time students do the same split over several hours.

> **Tip:** Do manzil first when your child is fresh, such as after Fajr or before school. Sabaq can happen later in the day, but older revision should never be the thing that gets skipped.

## A sample weekly hifz revision schedule

Here is a realistic week for an 11-year-old in regular school who has memorized five juz (Juz 26–30) and is now memorizing Juz 25, working with a teacher three evenings a week.

| Day | Sabaq | Sabqi | Manzil |
|---|---|---|---|
| Monday (class) | Half a page, recited to teacher | Last 7 lessons | Juz 30 |
| Tuesday (home) | Half a page | Last 7 lessons | Juz 29 |
| Wednesday (class) | Half a page, recited to teacher | Last 7 lessons | Juz 28 |
| Thursday (home) | Half a page | Last 7 lessons | Juz 27 |
| Friday (home) | No new lesson | Whole week’s lessons | Juz 26 |
| Saturday | Rest or light listening | — | Recite favourite surahs in salah |
| Sunday (class) | Teacher tests the week | Current juz so far | Weakest juz of the week |

This student completes a full manzil cycle every week, and the teacher hears every new lesson. As the memorized portion grows, the manzil per day will need to grow too, or the cycle will stretch to two weeks. ## The seven-manzil cycle for advanced students

Many mushafs mark the Quran into seven parts, called manzils, so that it can be recited in full each week. This connects to the Prophet’s ﷺ advice to Abdullah ibn Amr: “Recite the whole Qur’an in one month’s time,” and when he asked for more, “Then finish the recitation of the Qur’an in seven days, and do not finish it in less than this period” ([Sahih al-Bukhari 5054](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:5054)). The longer narration adds that, in old age, Abdullah used to recite a seventh of the Quran to some of his family during the day, checking what he would recite at night ([Sahih al-Bukhari 5052](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:5052)).

| Manzil | Surahs | Pages (604-page mushaf) |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Al-Fatiha to An-Nisa (1–4) | 1–106 |
| 2 | Al-Ma’idah to At-Tawbah (5–9) | 106–207 |
| 3 | Yunus to An-Nahl (10–16) | 208–281 |
| 4 | Al-Isra to Al-Furqan (17–25) | 282–366 |
| 5 | Ash-Shu’ara to Ya-Sin (26–36) | 367–445 |
| 6 | As-Saffat to Al-Hujurat (37–49) | 446–517 |
| 7 | Qaf to An-Nas (50–114) | 518–604 |

A weekly khatm is a high target, usually for completed huffaz or very advanced full-time students. Most children work up to it gradually, starting with a two- or three-week cycle.

## How to run revision at home: step by step

**Step 1: Agree on the plan with the teacher.** Write down the daily sabaq size, what counts as sabqi and the manzil rotation.

**Step 2: Fix the order.** Manzil, then sabqi, then sabaq. If time runs out, it is the new lesson that waits.

**Step 3: Listen without the mushaf open.** Hold the mushaf yourself and follow along while your child recites from memory. If you do not read Arabic, play a recitation alongside and listen for pauses or hesitation.

**Step 4: Mark mistakes.** Use a pencil to put a small mark beside any ayah where your child stumbled. One Ottawa hafizah told [CBC Ottawa](https://www.cbc.ca/news/canada/ottawa/memorizing-qur-an-ottawa-hafiz-1.6799786) that her teacher marked her mushaf where she made mistakes in review, and she still revises from that same copy.

**Step 5: Revisit marked pages.** Any page with two or more marks gets extra revision the next day.

**Step 6: Use salah and audio.** Encourage your child to recite manzil portions in sunnah prayers and to listen to the current juz in the car.

**Step 7: Record every session.** A simple log of what was revised and how strong it was makes weak spots visible. Our [hifz progress tracker](/blog/hifz-progress-tracker) guide has templates.

## When revision falls behind

Every hifz student hits a point where revision weakens. Common signs: the sabqi needs prompting, a juz that was strong now has gaps, or the manzil keeps getting skipped.

What to do:

1. **Pause sabaq for one or two weeks.** Use the time to strengthen the sabqi and the weakest juz.
2. **Shrink the cycle.** Temporarily revise smaller portions more often until accuracy returns.
3. **Re-memorize, do not just re-read.** For badly weakened pages, treat them as a new sabaq for a few days.
4. **Check the pace.** If revision keeps collapsing, the daily sabaq is too large. Reduce it.
5. **Talk to the teacher early.** The sooner a weak juz is caught, the easier it is to repair.

Remember the Prophet’s ﷺ teaching that the most beloved deeds to Allah are the most regular, even if small ([Sahih al-Bukhari 6464](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:6464)). A smaller daily plan that never breaks is stronger than an ambitious one that keeps failing.
