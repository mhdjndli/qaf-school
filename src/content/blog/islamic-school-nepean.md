Searching “Islamic school Nepean” puts you in the right part of Ottawa. Nepean has more full-time Islamic schools than any other part of the city, including Ottawa Islamic School, Ummaty School, Abraar School and QAF School’s Islamic JK and SK on Dynes Rd. This guide walks Nepean families through the options, real commute times, nearby mosques, public school choices and weekend Quran and Arabic programs.

> **Key takeaways**
> - Nepean has at least five full-time Islamic schools within about a 20-minute drive of each other, from Fisher Heights to Bells Corners.
> - QAF School is at 900 Dynes Rd, just off Prince of Wales Dr, and offers a full-day Islamic JK and SK for ages 4 and 5, plus a Sunday Camp for ages 8 to 15.
> - Most Nepean neighbourhoods are 5 to 20 minutes from Dynes Rd by car outside rush hour.
> - Free school-board Arabic classes run on Saturday mornings at three Nepean public schools: Carleton Heights, Knoxdale and Sir Winston Churchill.

## How Nepean’s Islamic schools are spread out

It helps to think of Nepean’s Islamic schools in three clusters.

**The Prince of Wales and Fisher corridor.** QAF on Dynes Rd and Ottawa Islamic School on Coral Ave sit less than two kilometres apart, in the quiet residential pocket between Carleton Heights, Fisher Heights and the Rideau River. Families from Courtland Park, Carleton Heights, Fisher Heights, Rideauview and Hog’s Back can often reach either one in under five minutes.

**Merivale and Meadowlands.** Ummaty School on Colonnade Rd S and Abraar Elementary on Fieldrow St serve the Merivale Rd and Meadowlands Dr area. These work well for families in Crestview, Parkwood Hills, Craig Henry and around Merivale Mall.

**Bells Corners and the west.** Tarbiyah Learning Academy at Jami Omar is the natural choice for families in Bells Corners, Arlington Woods, Lynwood Village and Qualicum, and it is also close for families in the eastern edge of Kanata.

> **Tip:** Before you compare curriculum, map the three schools closest to you and drive each route once at 8:30 AM on a weekday. A school that looks 10 minutes away on a Sunday can be 25 minutes in September traffic.

## Mosques and Islamic centres in and near Nepean

A school is only one part of a child’s Muslim community. These are the masajid Nepean families most often use. Prayer times and programs change, so check with each centre.

- **Qurtuba Islamic Centre, 1950 Merivale Rd.** A Muslim Association of Canada (MAC) centre in central Nepean, close to Merivale Gardens and Craig Henry. See the [Qurtuba Islamic Centre page](https://centres.macnet.ca/qic/) for programs.
- **Jami Omar, 3990 Old Richmond Rd, Bells Corners.** A long-established west-end masjid that also houses Tarbiyah Learning Academy.
- **Masjid ar-Rahmah, 1216 Hunt Club Rd.** Just across the Rideau River in South Keys, roughly a 10-minute drive from Dynes Rd via Hog’s Back Rd and Riverside Dr.
- **SNMC, 3020 Woodroffe Ave.** The South Nepean Muslim Community centre at the south end of Nepean, home to SNMC Albushra School and a youth program. Check SNMC directly for current children’s events.
- **Barrhaven Islamic Centre, Greenbank Rd.** Another MAC centre serving south Nepean. It listed Quran halaqa classes for 2026–27, with a waitlist, when we checked.

For Jumu’ah, many Nepean parents simply choose the masjid closest to work. For children, a masjid with a regular weekend or evening Quran class matters more than size.

## Public and Catholic schools serving Nepean

Many Nepean families mix and match: an Islamic school for the early years, then a public or Catholic school later, or the other way round. Four boards serve Nepean:

- **Ottawa-Carleton District School Board (OCDSB):** English public schools, including Carleton Heights Public School on Prince of Wales Dr and Knoxdale Public School on Greenbank Rd. Use the [OCDSB school locator](https://www.ocdsb.ca/our-schools/school-locator) to find your designated school.
- **Ottawa Catholic School Board (OCSB):** English Catholic schools, including St. Rita and St. Augustine near Carleton Heights. See the [OCSB school list](https://www.ocsb.ca/our-schools/).
- **CEPEO and CECCE:** the French public and French Catholic boards, open to families with French-language rights.

All of these offer free full-day JK and SK. If you are weighing that option against a faith-based school, our honest comparison of [Islamic school vs. public school in Ontario](/blog/islamic-school-vs-public-school-ontario) and our guide to [Ottawa’s four school boards](/blog/ottawa-school-boards-compared) lay out the trade-offs.

## Weekend Quran and Arabic options in Nepean

If your child is in public school during the week, Nepean has good weekend options.

**Free OCDSB Arabic classes.** The OCDSB’s International Languages program runs Arabic on Saturday mornings from 9:30 to 12:00 at Carleton Heights Public School (1600 Prince of Wales Dr), Knoxdale Public School (168 Greenbank Rd) and Sir Winston Churchill Public School (49 Mulvagh Ave). The 2026–27 year runs from September 12 to June 19, it is open to JK to Grade 8 students from any board, and Ontario residents pay only a $20 activity fee. Details are on the [OCDSB International Languages page](https://www.ocdsb.ca/programs-learning/continuing-education/international-languages-program/international-indigenous-languages-elementary-program).

**QAF Sunday Camp.** For ages 8 to 15, every Sunday from 11 AM to 3 PM at 900 Dynes Rd. Children study Quran in small groups, plus Arabic reading and writing, math and STEM, and soccer. Tuition is $190 a month from September to June, with junior and senior classes. More on the [Sunday Quran and Arabic camp in Ottawa](/sunday-camp).

**Masjid programs.** Local masajid run Quran halaqat and seasonal children’s programs, but schedules are often posted late in the summer. Ask Qurtuba, Jami Omar and the Barrhaven Islamic Centre directly, and ask early, because spots can fill.

For the full city-wide list, including Sunday schools at the Ottawa Main Mosque and Saturday schools in Westboro, see our guide to [weekend Islamic schools in Ottawa](/blog/weekend-islamic-schools-ottawa). If Arabic is your main goal, compare the options in [Arabic classes for kids in Ottawa](/blog/arabic-classes-for-kids-ottawa).

## Family life in Nepean near Dynes Rd

One of the quiet advantages of the Dynes Rd area is how much green space is within walking distance. Families often pair pick-up with a stop at the park, especially in spring and fall.

- **Hog’s Back Park:** An NCC park at Hog’s Back Rd and Riverside Dr with views of Hog’s Back Falls, picnic tables and free parking from May to early December. See the [NCC’s Hog’s Back Park page](https://ncc-ccn.gc.ca/places/hogs-back-park).
- **Mooney’s Bay Park:** A large city park and beach on the Rideau River, popular with families in summer.
- **Rideauview Park and Carleton Heights Park:** Neighbourhood parks a short walk or drive from Dynes Rd. Carleton Heights also has a community centre on Apeldoorn Ave.
- **Libraries:** The Emerald Plaza branch on Merivale Rd is about 7 minutes away, and Nepean Centrepointe is the large district branch for central Nepean. Both offer free children’s programs; check the library’s events calendar for current times.

These small details matter more than parents expect. A school close to parks, a library and the family’s masjid makes it easier to build a routine where faith, learning and play all happen in the same neighbourhood.

## How Nepean parents choose between Islamic schools

Every family weighs things differently, but these questions come up again and again on our tours:

1. **Which grades do I need, and for how long?** QAF is JK and SK only. Ottawa Islamic School runs to Grade 12, Ummaty to Grade 8 and Abraar Elementary to Grade 6. If you want one school for ten years, that narrows the list. If you want the strongest possible start at 4 and 5, a specialist kindergarten may suit you better.
2. **What does a typical day look like?** Ask how long children work uninterrupted, how much is play-based and how Quran and Arabic fit in. Our post on [what a day looks like at QAF](/blog/what-a-day-looks-like-at-qaf) is one example of the detail worth asking for.
3. **What is the full cost?** Compare monthly tuition, registration and resource fees, and sibling discounts. Our [guide to Ottawa Islamic school fees](/blog/ottawa-islamic-school-fees-explained) explains how schools structure costs.
4. **How will my child move on?** For JK and SK, ask whether the program follows Ontario’s kindergarten curriculum so the transition to Grade 1 anywhere is smooth.

## Is QAF right for your Nepean family?

QAF is a good fit if your child is turning 4 or 5, you live within roughly 20 minutes of Prince of Wales Dr, and you want a full-day Islamic kindergarten with a calm, Montessori-inspired classroom. Children have a long morning work cycle from 9:20 to 11:00, daily Arabic immersion, short surahs from Juz Amma, duas and adab, all aligned with Ontario’s kindergarten curriculum. The school year runs from September to June. Tuition is $690 a month for 10 months plus a $190 annual resource fee, with a sibling discount, and parents get daily photos and updates through ClassDojo.

QAF is probably not the right fit if you need care before 8:45 AM or after 3:15 PM, if your child is under 4, or if you want one school through Grade 8. In that case, one of the K–8 or K–12 schools above may suit you better. For children aged 8 to 15, our [Sunday Camp](/sunday-camp) may be the better match.
