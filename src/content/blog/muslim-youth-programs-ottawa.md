Muslim youth programs in Ottawa are spread across the city's mosques, community associations, sports leagues and volunteer-run camps, and most of them are not easy to find on Google. This guide maps the main options by type and area, from youth halaqas and gym nights to overnight camp and weekend Quran and Arabic classes, and gives you a checklist for choosing the right fit for a child aged 8 to 15.

> **Key takeaways**
> - Most Muslim youth programming in Ottawa runs through mosques and community associations, so the masjid closest to you is the best first call.
> - Programs fall into five types: youth circles (halaqas), sports and gym nights, weekend learning (Quran, Arabic, Islamic Studies), camps, and one-off events like sleepovers or youth iftars.
> - Schedules change every season. Muslim Link's Ottawa events page and each mosque's own site or social account are the most current sources.
> - Look for clear supervision, age-grouped activities, registration records and a named coordinator before you sign up.

## What counts as a Muslim youth program?

A Muslim youth program is any regular activity, run by or for the Muslim community, that gives children and teens a place to build friendships, faith and skills with peers who share their values. That can mean a weekly halaqa, a Friday-night gym session at a masjid, a Sunday Quran class, a soccer league or a summer camp.

Parents usually look for one of three things. Some want **belonging**: a group where their child is not the only Muslim. Others want **structured learning**, such as Quran and Arabic. Many want a **healthy social outlet**, especially for tweens and teens who spend the week in public school. The best programs offer a bit of all three.

## Mosque-based youth programs by area

### West end: Kanata, Stittsville and Bells Corners

The [Kanata Muslim Association](https://www.kanatamuslims.ca) runs one of the busiest youth calendars in the west end. Its listings include **Boys STR!VE** and **Girls EmpowerHER** (separate boys' and girls' programs), **Kids Badminton**, and family events like FamFajr. KMA also runs the Hikmah Saturday school and, in summer 2026, ran both a full-day masjid camp and a new afternoon Hikmah Camp.

In Bells Corners, Jami Omar Mosque has a gymnasium and lists free youth gym time for boys and for girls on separate evenings. Gym nights are simple, but for many teens that is exactly the draw: a safe place to play basketball with Muslim friends on a Friday night. Days have shifted between seasons, so call or email the masjid first.

The Stittsville Muslim Association is smaller and volunteer-run, but it hosts kids' events and workshops and ran a summer camp in 2026.

### South and Barrhaven

The [South Nepean Muslim Community (SNMC)](https://snmc.ca) at 3020 Woodroffe Ave has a dedicated SNMC Youth program, a gym used for children's events, and gatherings such as a bi-weekly mothers-and-daughters halaqa. It is also home to SNMC Albushra School. Barrhaven also has MAC's Barrhaven Islamic Centre on Greenbank Road.

Further east along Hunt Club, the **Assunnah Muslims Association** (Masjid ar-Rahmah and the AMA Community Centre at 1216 Hunt Club Rd) runs youth days and youth iftars, often for boys aged 9 to 13, along with Quran, Arabic and Islamic Studies classes and summer programming. Many of its events are announced on social media rather than a website.

### Central, downtown and east

The Ottawa Mosque at 251 Northwestern Ave, run by the Ottawa Muslim Association, is one of the city's longest-established mosques and lists Quran and education programs. Downtown, the Centretown Mosque at 397 Kent St runs a Kids Education program for ages 6 to 16 covering hifz, Arabic and Islamic Studies, and it has hosted teen events such as youth sleepovers.

In Orléans, Masjid Bilal (Islamic Society of Cumberland) runs Quran programs and a summer camp, and announced a "Fajr Knights" winter-break Fajr challenge for December 19, 2026 to January 3, 2027. A challenge like that is a clever way to get teens to the masjid during the holidays.

## Camps, outdoor programs and sports

### Overnight camp with Muslim Youth of Ottawa

[Muslim Youth of Ottawa](https://myo.camp/) describes itself as volunteer-led since the 1980s. Its main program is an overnight summer camp at Camp Smitty near Eganville, with cabins, canoes, campfires and prayer in congregation, for boys and girls aged 9 to 19. In 2026 it ran a leader-in-training (LIT) session and a youth session, plus new archery and outdoor-skills weekends. During the year it organizes monthly hikes, halaqas and service days, and it offers camp subsidies.

For many Ottawa Muslim teens, a few nights away at a faith-based camp is the experience they remember most. If your child is nervous, the year-round hikes are a gentle way to meet the group first.

### Day camps at local masjids

Almost every large masjid in the city runs a summer day camp, usually with Quran in the morning and sports, crafts and field trips in the afternoon. We cover prices, dates and what to check in our guide to [Islamic summer camps in Ottawa](/blog/summer-camps-ottawa-muslim-kids).

### Muslim sports leagues

The Ottawa Islamic Soccer League started its first season in 2024. It runs Sunday games from spring to early fall for U4 to U18 at Lytle Park, weeknight practices in summer, and an indoor futsal season in fall and winter, with a financial assistance application.

## Weekend learning programs for youth

Not every youth program is social. Many families want a steady weekly class that keeps Quran and Arabic moving forward while their child attends public school. Ottawa has several of these, from masjid Saturday schools to Sunday programs. Our guide to [weekend Islamic schools in Ottawa](/blog/weekend-islamic-schools-ottawa) compares them in detail, and [Quran classes for kids in Ottawa](/blog/quran-classes-for-kids-ottawa) covers Quran-only options.

The **QAF Sunday Camp** sits between a weekend school and a youth program. It runs Sundays from 11 AM to 3 PM at 900 Dynes Rd in Nepean for ages 8 to 15, in junior and senior classes. Each Sunday combines Quran, Arabic reading and writing, math and STEM, and soccer, so children get real learning and a run-around with Muslim friends in one block. It costs $190 a month from September to June.

For teens specifically, see our guide to [Quran classes for teens in Ottawa](/blog/quran-classes-for-teens-ottawa). For ages 9 to 12, our post on [Islamic programs for tweens](/blog/islamic-programs-for-tweens) explains what that age group needs.

## School-based groups: MSAs and prayer spaces

A Muslim Students' Association (MSA) is a student-run club for Muslim students. They are common at universities, including the University of Ottawa and Carleton, and many high schools have one too. A school MSA might organize Friday prayer, Ramadan events or Islamic History Month activities.

If your child's high school does not have one, students can usually start a club the same way as any other, with a staff advisor. Ontario's human rights framework expects schools to accommodate religious practice, including space to pray; the [Ontario Human Rights Commission's policy statement on religious accommodation in schools](https://www.ohrc.on.ca/en/policy-statement-religious-accommodation-schools) is a good reference. Our post on [Muslim kids in public school](/blog/muslim-kids-in-public-school) covers prayer, Ramadan and accommodation requests in more depth.

## How to find current programs

Ottawa's Muslim youth scene changes quickly. Volunteers move on, new programs launch each September, and a lot is announced only on Instagram or WhatsApp. These are the most reliable ways to stay current.

1. **Check Muslim Link.** [Muslim Link](https://muslimlink.ca) is Ottawa's Muslim community newspaper and events listing, with camps, schools and youth events posted by local organizations.
2. **Follow your nearest masjid.** Most mosques announce youth nights and registrations on social media first. Ask at the front desk for the youth coordinator's contact.
3. **Ask other parents.** Parent WhatsApp groups at weekend schools and full-time Islamic schools often hear about programs before they are posted.
4. **Watch the September and May windows.** Fall programs and summer camps open registration around these months and often fill quickly.
5. **Check the [MAC Ottawa chapter page](https://chapters.macnet.ca/ottawa/)** for MAC Youth camps and its centres' programs.

## How to choose the right youth program

A youth program is only as good as the adults running it. Before you register, ask these questions.

- **Who supervises, and how many adults are there?** Ask for the coordinator's name and how many volunteers attend each session.
- **Are volunteers screened?** Many organizations ask volunteers who work with children for a police record check. It is reasonable to ask whether they do.
- **How are ages and genders grouped?** A 9-year-old and a 16-year-old need very different programs. Many Ottawa masjids run separate boys' and girls' programs for tweens and teens.
- **What happens at pick-up?** Clear sign-in and sign-out matters, especially for evening programs.
- **Is there a learning goal?** For weekly classes, ask how progress is tracked and shared with parents.
- **What does it cost, and is help available?** Many mosque programs are free or low-cost, and camps often offer subsidies or sibling discounts.

In our experience, the best sign is simple: your child wants to go back next week. If they come home with a new friend and a new surah, you have found a good fit.
