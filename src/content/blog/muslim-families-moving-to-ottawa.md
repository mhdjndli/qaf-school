Moving to Ottawa as a Muslim family goes more smoothly if you settle three things early: a neighbourhood near a masjid and the school you want, health coverage and benefits, and a school plan for each child. Ottawa has masjids in every part of the city, about a dozen full-time Islamic schools, many weekend Quran and Arabic programs, and free public schools with full-day kindergarten. This guide walks you through each decision in the order most families face them.

> **Key takeaways**
> - Pick your neighbourhood around two anchors: the masjid you will attend and the school your children will attend. Commutes across Ottawa can be long.
> - OHIP has no waiting period in Ontario. Apply in person at ServiceOntario with three original documents.
> - Ontario children can start Junior Kindergarten in September of the year they turn 4, and board kindergarten is free.
> - Ottawa's four school boards assign schools by address, so check the locators before you sign a lease.
> - Free newcomer settlement help is available from organizations such as OCISO and CCI Ottawa.

## Moving to Ottawa as a Muslim family: your first-month checklist

Most families find it easiest to tackle these in order.

**Step 1: Apply for OHIP.** Ontario's health insurance has [no waiting period](https://www.ontario.ca/page/apply-ohip-and-get-health-card) for eligible residents. Apply in person at a ServiceOntario centre with three original documents: one proving citizenship or eligible immigration status, one proving Ontario residency, and one proving identity.

**Step 2: Apply for the Canada Child Benefit.** The CCB is a monthly, non-taxable payment from the Canada Revenue Agency to help eligible families with the cost of raising children under 18. Eligibility and amounts depend on your status and income, so start with the [CRA's overview](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/child-family-benefits/canada-child-benefit-overview.html).

**Step 3: Find your designated schools.** Enter your address in the [OCDSB school locator](https://www.ocdsb.ca/our_schools/school_locator) and the OCSB's school locator. If you are a French-language rights holder, check CEPEO and CECCE as well.

**Step 4: Visit the closest masjid.** Introduce yourself after Jumu'ah or Maghrib. Masjids are often the fastest way to find halal butchers, Quran teachers, carpools and local recommendations.

**Step 5: Connect with a settlement agency.** Free settlement services can help with housing, jobs, language classes and school registration (see below).

**Step 6: Get ready for winter.** If you arrive between October and March, buy snowsuits, insulated boots and mittens before the first cold snap. Ontario schools usually send children outside at recess unless it is extremely cold.

## How school works in Ottawa

If you are new to Ontario, here are the basics.

- **Kindergarten:** two years, Junior Kindergarten (JK) and Senior Kindergarten (SK). A child can start JK in September of the calendar year they turn 4. Kindergarten is optional but free in board schools, and the English boards teach it about half in French.
- **Compulsory school:** begins at age 6.
- **Four public boards:** OCDSB (English, secular), OCSB (English, Catholic, welcomes all faiths), CEPEO (French, secular) and CECCE (French, Catholic).
- **Private Islamic schools:** charge tuition and set their own admission rules and timelines.
- **Weekend programs:** many families combine a board school with a weekend Quran and Arabic program. See our list of [weekend Islamic schools in Ottawa](/blog/weekend-islamic-schools-ottawa).

Ottawa's boards also run International Languages classes, including Arabic, on Saturday mornings at schools across the city.

For a deeper walkthrough of documents, ESL support and registration, see our [newcomer guide to Ottawa schools](/blog/newcomer-guide-ottawa-schools).

## Masjids and community life

Ottawa's Muslim community is long-established. The Ottawa Muslim Association traces its roots to the 1960s, when a few families began meeting to teach their children Quran, Islamic studies and Arabic. Its Ottawa Main Mosque on Northwestern Ave was built in the 1970s. Today the [OMA](https://ottawamosque.ca) lists services ranging from education and a Tarteel Quran program to marriage, funerals, zakat and a newcomers page.

Since then, masjids and Islamic centres have opened across the city, from the Centretown Mosque downtown to Masjid ar-Rahmah in the south, SNMC in Barrhaven, the Kanata Muslim Association in the west and Masjid Bilal in Orléans. Each has its own character, language mix and programs. It is worth visiting two or three before deciding where your family will settle in.

What masjids typically help newcomers with:

- **Children's Quran and Islamic Studies classes**, often on weekends or weekday evenings
- **Funeral and marriage services**
- **Zakat and emergency support** for families in need
- **Informal networks** for housing, jobs, carpools and recommendations

## Halal food and daily life

Finding halal food in Ottawa is not hard. Halal butchers, grocers and restaurants are spread across the city, especially along major commercial streets. An online directory such as Zabihah is a useful starting point. Your masjid's notice board and WhatsApp groups are often more current.

Two habits help newcomer families:

- **Ask how "halal" is certified.** Standards vary, so ask the shop or restaurant which certifier or supplier they use.
- **Plan school lunches early.** Many Ottawa schools and child care centres are nut-aware or nut-free, and children usually bring a packed lunch. At our school, all food must be halal and nut-free, with a packed lunch and two snacks.

## Health care, benefits and child care

Beyond OHIP and the Canada Child Benefit, a few things are worth knowing:

- **Family doctors:** finding one can take time. Ask at your masjid and at your settlement agency, and use walk-in clinics in the meantime.
- **Child care:** many licensed programs are enrolled in the Canada-wide (CWELCC) fee-reduction system, and the City of Ottawa offers an income-tested fee subsidy. You register on the city's child care waitlist first, so start early. Our [Ottawa child care subsidy guide](/blog/ottawa-child-care-subsidy-guide) walks through it.
- **Children's dental and vision:** ask your settlement worker which public programs your family qualifies for, since eligibility depends on income and status.

## Newcomer services in Ottawa

Free settlement help is one of the best-kept secrets for new families. Two long-standing agencies:

- **OCISO (Ottawa Community Immigrant Services Organization):** settlement services for newcomers and their families, a School Supports program, and YOCISO programs for immigrant youth aged 13 to 24. Locations include 959 Wellington St W, 275 Bay St and 1800 Bank St. Details are on the [OCISO website](https://ociso.org).
- **CCI Ottawa (Catholic Centre for Immigrants):** settlement, housing, employment and language support for permanent residents, refugees and refugee claimants, with free workshops on topics like winter, renting and taxes. It is at 219 Argyle Ave and serves Ottawa residents only. Visit [CCI Ottawa](https://www.cciottawa.ca).

## Getting Islamic education right from the start

The first year after a move is when routines are easiest to set. A few choices make a big difference:

- **Ages 4–5:** a full-day Islamic kindergarten gives children Quran, Arabic and Islamic manners every day while they adjust to a new country. [Our Islamic JK/SK program](/program) runs 9:00 AM to 3:00 PM, Monday to Friday, at 900 Dynes Rd. It is Montessori-inspired, aligned with Ontario's kindergarten curriculum and includes daily Arabic immersion. Tuition is $690 per month for 10 months plus a $190 annual resource fee, with a sibling discount.
- **Ages 8–15:** [QAF Sunday Camp](/sunday-camp) runs Sundays from 11 AM to 3 PM with Quran, Arabic reading and writing, math and STEM, and soccer, for $190 a month from September to June.
- **Everyone:** keep the home language strong. Children who arrive speaking Arabic, Somali, Urdu or another language benefit from keeping it while they add English and French.
