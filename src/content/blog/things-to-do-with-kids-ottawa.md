The best things to do with kids in Ottawa include hands-on national museums (several with free admission windows), the Rideau Canal Skateway in winter, Gatineau Park in every season, farm days in the fall, and free library programs when the weather turns. This guide groups family activities by season, with free-admission times, ages, and practical notes for Muslim families on prayer timing and halal food nearby.

> **Key takeaways**
> - Three Ingenium museums (Science and Technology, Aviation and Space, Agriculture and Food) offer free admission daily from 4 to 5 PM, and the Canadian Museum of Nature is free on Thursdays from 5 to 8 PM. Book a free ticket online.
> - The Ottawa Public Library lends museum Access Passes for free family admission.
> - Winter is short-day season: Dhuhr, Asr and Maghrib can all fall during a museum visit, so plan prayer breaks and bring a travel mat.
> - Bank Street, Centretown and the Rideau Street area all have halal options, and Ottawa Tourism keeps a halal food guide.

## Ottawa's best museums for kids

Ottawa's national museums are world-class and very child-friendly. All four below are free for children aged 2 and under.

### 1. Canada Science and Technology Museum

**What it is:** A hands-on museum at 1867 St. Laurent Blvd with interactive exhibits on technology, trains and everyday science.
**Best for:** Curious kids who like to press buttons, build and experiment.
**Ages:** About 3–14.
**Cost:** Paid admission, with free entry daily from 4 to 5 PM and for children 2 and under ([Ingenium visitor info](https://ingenium.ca/scitech/en/visit)).
**Our take:** One of the best rainy-day outings in the city. It is closed on Mondays, and the free hour is short, so treat it as a taster or a quick return visit.

### 2. Canadian Museum of Nature

**What it is:** A national natural history museum at 240 McLeod Street, with dinosaurs, mammals, minerals, live insects and a castle-like building kids love.
**Best for:** Dinosaur fans and animal lovers.
**Ages:** About 2–12.
**Cost:** Paid daytime admission; free general admission on Thursdays from 5 to 8 PM, and free for tiny tots aged 0–2 ([Museum of Nature admission](https://nature.ca/en/visit-us/plan-your-visit/admission-fees/)). Every visitor needs a ticket, even free ones.
**Our take:** Thursday evenings are perfect for families with school-age kids. Note that the museum is closed on Tuesdays during its winter season.

### 3. Canada Aviation and Space Museum

**What it is:** A large museum at 11 Aviation Parkway with real aircraft, from early planes to jets and space artifacts.
**Best for:** Kids who love planes, rockets and big machines.
**Ages:** About 4–14.
**Cost:** Paid admission, free daily from 4 to 5 PM and for children 2 and under. It is closed on Tuesdays.
**Our take:** The sheer size of the aircraft impresses even toddlers, but older kids get the most out of it.

### 4. Canada Agriculture and Food Museum

**What it is:** A working farm museum at 901 Prince of Wales Drive, on the Central Experimental Farm, with barns, animals and food exhibits.
**Best for:** Toddlers and young children who love animals.
**Ages:** About 2–10.
**Cost:** Paid admission, free daily from 4 to 5 PM and for children 2 and under. It is closed Mondays and Tuesdays.
**Our take:** A gentle, low-stress outing for little ones, and close to the south and west ends of the city.

## How to visit Ottawa museums for free

You do not need to pay full price to enjoy Ottawa's museums. Here are the main ways families visit for free.

| Museum | Free admission time | Free for young children |
|---|---|---|
| Canada Science and Technology Museum | Daily, 4–5 PM | Ages 2 and under |
| Canada Aviation and Space Museum | Daily, 4–5 PM | Ages 2 and under |
| Canada Agriculture and Food Museum | Daily, 4–5 PM | Ages 2 and under |
| Canadian Museum of Nature | Thursdays, 5–8 PM | Ages 0–2 |

These policies were current when we checked in October 2026. Each museum asks visitors to book tickets in advance, including free ones.

- **Library Access Passes:** The Ottawa Public Library lends [Access Passes](https://collections.biblioottawalibrary.ca/en/access-passes-including-museum-passes) for free family admission to partner museums, including the Ingenium museums and the Museum of Nature. Passes are borrowed in person at a branch and cannot be placed on hold.
- **Newcomer families:** Recent permanent residents and new citizens can get free or discounted admission through the [Canoo app](https://www.canoo.ca/) from the Institute for Canadian Citizenship. Ingenium and the Museum of Nature both take part.
- **Free days:** The Ingenium museums and the Museum of Nature are free on Canada Day (July 1) and the National Day for Truth and Reconciliation (September 30).

## Winter things to do with kids in Ottawa

Ottawa winters are long, but they come with some of the city's best family activities.

- **Rideau Canal Skateway:** The NCC's [Rideau Canal Skateway](https://ncc-ccn.gc.ca/places/rideau-canal-skateway) is 7.8 km long and free to use. It usually opens between January and early March, but the season depends entirely on the weather, so check ice conditions before you go. Bring a sled to pull toddlers.
- **Winterlude:** The federal Winterlude festival runs in February, with snow sculptures, ice slides and family activities across Ottawa and Gatineau.
- **Tobogganing:** The City of Ottawa maintains designated toboggan hills. Check ottawa.ca for the list and safety rules, and always use a helmet.
- **Snowshoeing in Gatineau Park:** Many trails in Gatineau Park are open for winter walking and snowshoeing.
- **Indoor museums:** Winter is peak museum season. Plan for the free afternoon hour, then head home before dinner.

> **Tip:** In December and January, sunset in Ottawa comes as early as about 4:20 PM. If you arrive for the 4 PM free hour at a museum, Asr may already be in, and Maghrib may come before you leave.

## Spring and summer things to do with kids

When the snow melts, Ottawa turns into a city of parks, water and festivals.

**Spring (April to June)**

- **Canadian Tulip Festival:** Held every May, mainly around Commissioners Park at Dow's Lake. Check the festival's website for dates. Eid al-Adha 2027 is expected around May 16, so a tulip walk could make a lovely Eid outing.
- **Hog's Back Falls and Mud Lake:** Short, stroller-friendly nature walks. Mud Lake in the west end is popular for bird-watching.
- **Farm visits:** The Canada Agriculture and Food Museum and local farms are great in spring.

**Summer (July and August)**

- **Splash pads and wading pools:** The City of Ottawa runs free splash pads in parks across the city. Check ottawa.ca for locations and opening dates.
- **City beaches:** Mooney's Bay, Britannia and Petrie Island have supervised swimming in summer. Water quality is tested daily, so check the City's beaches page before you go.
- **Gatineau Park lakes and trails:** Family hikes and swimming beaches, about 20–30 minutes from downtown.
- **Summer farm days:** Saunders Farm runs summer maze days and a corn maze festival. Check the farm's website for dates.

Summer days are long. In late June, Maghrib in Ottawa is close to 9 PM (daylight time), so families can fit a full day out between prayers more easily than in winter.

## Fall things to do with kids in Ottawa

Fall is a beautiful season in Ottawa, with crisp air and bright colours.

- **Fall colours in Gatineau Park:** Late September to mid-October is usually the best time for colours. Weekends are very busy, so arrive early.
- **Pumpkin and apple picking:** Farms around Ottawa offer pick-your-own pumpkins and apples. Check each farm's website for the season.
- **Saunders Farm:** Its fall season includes daytime family activities such as mazes, play areas and pumpkins. The evening events are horror-themed and aimed at older visitors, and some daytime attractions also have Halloween themes. Families who prefer to avoid Halloween content can check the farm's website for what is on each day, or visit in summer instead.
- **Back to the museums:** As days get shorter, the free museum hours become a good after-school treat.

## Rainy days and indoor play

Not every day is a good outdoor day. These indoor options are reliable year-round.

- **Ottawa Public Library:** Branches across the city run free storytimes and children's programs. Browse the library's website for events, and borrow books while you are there. Our list of the [best Islamic books for kids](/blog/best-islamic-books-for-kids) is a good starting point.
- **Community centres:** City recreation centres offer drop-in programs, swimming and skating. Check ottawa.ca for your local schedule.
- **Indoor playgrounds and trampoline parks:** Ottawa has several privately run indoor play centres. Check age rules, sock policies and whether you need to book ahead.
- **Museums:** All four museums above are indoors and work well on rainy or very cold days.

## Tips for Muslim families exploring Ottawa

A little planning makes outings smoother when you are fitting in prayers and looking for halal food.

**Plan around prayer times**

1. **Step 1: Check prayer times** for the day using your mosque's timetable or a prayer app.
2. **Step 2: Choose your outing window.** In winter, Dhuhr is around noon and sunset is early, so a morning outing often fits best. In summer, the long afternoon gives more room.
3. **Step 3: Pack a travel prayer kit:** a compact mat, a compass or app for qibla, and a water bottle for wudu.
4. **Step 4: Find a quiet spot.** Most museums do not have dedicated prayer rooms, so families often pray in a quiet corner, a parked car or a park. Staff are usually helpful if you ask.
5. **Step 5: Combine with a mosque visit.** The Ottawa Main Mosque at 251 Northwestern Avenue in the west end is one of the city's oldest, and there are many other mosques and musallas across the city.

**Finding halal food nearby**

- **Bank Street** has many halal shawarma, grill and burger spots, from Centretown down to the south end near Hunt Club.
- **Centretown and Elgin Street** are close to the Museum of Nature and have several halal options.
- **Near the Canada Science and Technology Museum,** Ottawa Tourism lists a halal option in Elmvale Acres.
- Ottawa Tourism's [halal food guide](https://ottawatourism.ca/en/ottawa-insider/halal-food-options-ottawa) is a helpful starting point. Halal status can change, so confirm with each restaurant.

**Plan Eid outings early.** Eid al-Fitr 2027 is expected in early March and Eid al-Adha around mid-May (depending on moon sighting). Popular spots get busy on weekends, so book museum tickets ahead.
