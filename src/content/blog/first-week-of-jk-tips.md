The best first day of kindergarten tips are simple: prepare your child a week ahead, keep the morning calm, say one short and loving goodbye, and plan a quiet afternoon. Most of what makes the first day go well happens before you reach the classroom door. This guide walks you through the week before, the morning itself, drop-off, pick-up and the rest of the first week of JK, with tips for Muslim families along the way.

> **Key takeaways**
> - Start earlier bedtimes and a school-day routine one to two weeks before the first day.
> - Pack the night before and lay out clothes your child can manage alone.
> - Keep drop-off short and warm. Tell your child who will pick them up, then leave.
> - Expect tiredness and big feelings after school in the first weeks. It is normal.
> - Share key information with the teacher early: allergies, toileting, home language and anything that comforts your child.

## First day of kindergarten tips at a glance

| When | What to do | Why it helps |
|---|---|---|
| 1–2 weeks before | Shift bedtime and wake-up time earlier | Rested children cope better with a long day |
| 1 week before | Visit the school or walk past it | The building and route become familiar |
| A few days before | Practise opening lunch containers and putting on shoes | Independence reduces stress at lunch and recess |
| The night before | Pack the bag, label everything, lay out clothes | Mornings stay calm |
| Morning of | Breakfast, early start, leaving-home dua | A calm start sets the tone |
| Drop-off | One warm goodbye, then leave | Long goodbyes make separation harder |
| After school | Snack, quiet time, early bedtime | Children often release feelings at home |

## Before the first day

### One to two weeks before

**Step 1: Reset sleep.** Move bedtime and wake-up time earlier by 15 minutes every few days. The Canadian 24-Hour Movement Guidelines recommend [10 to 13 hours of good-quality sleep](https://csepguidelines.ca/guidelines/early-years/) for children aged 3 to 4, which can include a nap. The American Academy of Pediatrics also suggests [shifting to earlier bedtimes](https://www.healthychildren.org/English/ages-stages/gradeschool/school/Pages/Back-to-School-Tips.aspx) a week or two before school starts.

**Step 2: Visit the school.** Walk or drive the route at the same time of day you will travel in September. Play in the school yard if it is open, and point out the door your child will use.

**Step 3: Talk about school simply and positively.** The Ontario government's [kindergarten preparation tips](https://www.ontario.ca/page/preparing-kindergarten) suggest talking about the new friends your child will meet and the things they will learn. Avoid "Don't be scared." Try "Lots of children feel excited and a little nervous. Your teacher will help you."

**Step 4: Practise self-help skills.** Let your child practise opening their lunch bag and containers, putting on and taking off shoes, using the toilet and washing hands, and zipping a jacket. These are the moments when four-year-olds most often need help.

**Step 5: Read about kindergarten.** Picture books about starting school give your child words for their feelings and show what a school day looks like.

### The night before

- Pack the backpack together and let your child put in their water bottle.
- Label everything: lunch bag, containers, water bottle, spare clothes, jacket and shoes.
- Lay out clothes and shoes your child can put on alone. Velcro or slip-on shoes save time.
- Prepare as much of lunch and the two snacks as you can.
- Keep the evening calm, with an early bath, a story, bedtime duas and lights out on time.

## The morning of the first day

**Wake up early enough that nobody rushes.** A rushed morning passes stress straight to your child.

**Offer a simple breakfast your child already likes.** The first day is not the time for new foods. If your child is too nervous to eat much, pack an extra snack.

**Say the leaving-home dua together.** It turns a nervous moment into a familiar one:

بِسْمِ اللَّهِ تَوَكَّلْتُ عَلَى اللَّهِ لاَ حَوْلَ وَلاَ قُوَّةَ إِلاَّ بِاللَّهِ

*Bismillah, tawakkaltu 'alallah, la hawla wa la quwwata illa billah.* "In the name of Allah, I place my trust in Allah; there is no might and no power except with Allah." (Sunan Abi Dawud 5095). More short duas for school days are in our guide to [daily duas for kids](/blog/daily-duas-for-kids).

**Arrive a little early, but not too early.** The Ontario government suggests arriving early to explore the school and playground. Ten minutes is plenty. Waiting a long time at the door can build anxiety.

**Take your photo before you leave home.** Photos at the classroom door stretch out the goodbye.

## At drop-off: the goodbye

The goodbye is the moment most parents dread. A clear, short routine makes it easier for everyone.

1. Walk your child to the meeting spot or classroom door.
2. Get down to their level, give a hug, and say one warm, confident goodbye.
3. Tell them exactly who will pick them up and when, in terms they understand: "After lunch and outside play, Baba will pick you up."
4. Hand your child to the teacher and leave, even if there are tears.
5. Do not sneak away, and do not come back for "one more hug."

Crying at drop-off is common in the first weeks, and many children settle soon after the parent leaves. If tears continue for weeks, or your child is very distressed, read our step-by-step plan for [kindergarten separation anxiety](/blog/kindergarten-separation-anxiety).

> **Tip:** Agree on a goodbye ritual you can repeat every day, such as a hug, a high-five and "Assalamu alaykum, see you after school." Predictable goodbyes feel safe.

## Pick-up and the first afternoon

Be on time, or a few minutes early, for the first weeks. Seeing you where you promised builds trust quickly.

Many children say very little about their day. "How was school?" often gets "Fine" or "I don't know." Try specific questions instead:

- "What did you eat first at lunch?"
- "Who did you play with outside?"
- "What was the funniest thing that happened?"
- "Did anything make you feel sad or worried?"
- "What do you want to do again tomorrow?"

Expect your child to be tired, hungry and maybe grumpy. Children often hold it together all day and release their feelings at home. Offer a snack, a cuddle and quiet play before any errands or activities. Keep bedtime early all week.

## What a normal first week of JK looks like

Every child is different, but many families see a pattern in the first week:

| Day | What often happens |
|---|---|
| Day 1 | Excitement, or nervous clinging, or both. Very tired by evening. |
| Day 2 | Some children who were fine on day 1 cry, because they now know you will leave. |
| Days 3–4 | Routines start to feel familiar. Tiredness builds. |
| Day 5 | Many children are exhausted by Friday. A quiet weekend helps. |
| Weeks 2–4 | Tears at drop-off usually shrink. New friendships start to appear. |

Signs your child is settling well include talking about a teacher or classmate by name, telling you about an activity, and a quicker recovery after drop-off. It is also normal to see some regression at home in the first weeks, such as more tantrums or bedtime struggles.

When should you be concerned? The Canadian Paediatric Society suggests talking to your child's health care provider if [fears start to interfere with normal daily activities](https://caringforkids.cps.ca/handouts/mentalhealth/anxiety-and-anxiety-disorders), or if your child often has physical symptoms like stomach aches when separation is expected.

## Lunch and snacks in the first week

Lunch is one of the hardest parts of the day for a new JK child. There are new containers, a time limit, and lots of noise.

- **Pack familiar foods.** Save new recipes for later in the year.
- **Choose containers your child can open alone.** Test them at home first.
- **Keep portions small and easy to eat.** Cut fruit and sandwiches into small pieces.
- **Check the school's food rules.** Many schools are nut-free. At QAF, the school is nut-free, all food must be halal, and families pack a lunch plus two snacks.
- **Add a small note or sticker** if your child can recognize it.

If your child comes home with a full lunch bag in the first week, that is common. They are often too busy or distracted to eat. Our [halal lunch ideas for kids](/blog/halal-lunch-ideas-for-kids) include easy options for picky eaters.

## Working with your child's teacher

Your child's teacher wants a good start as much as you do. Share useful information early, in a short note or message:

- Allergies, medical needs and anything about toileting.
- What your child likes to be called.
- The languages spoken at home, and words your child uses for the bathroom or for feeling sick.
- What comforts your child when upset.
- Any big changes at home, such as a new baby or a recent move.

Ask how the school shares updates. At QAF, parents use ClassDojo to see daily photos and videos, read announcements and message the teacher, which many families find reassuring in the first weeks. Our [ClassDojo parent guide](/blog/classdojo-parent-guide) explains how to set it up.

## The bottom line

The first day of kindergarten is a big step, but it does not have to be stressful. Prepare a week ahead, keep the morning calm, say one confident goodbye, and plan a quiet afternoon. Expect a few wobbly days, stay in touch with the teacher, and trust that many children settle into new routines within a few weeks.
