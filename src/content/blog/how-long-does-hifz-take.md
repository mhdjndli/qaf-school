How long does it take to memorize the Quran? For a focused full-time student, often about two to three years; for a child doing part-time hifz alongside school, more commonly five years or longer. The real answer comes down to simple maths: how many pages your child memorizes each week, how many weeks a year they keep going, and how much time revision needs. This guide shows realistic timelines by pace, what real Ottawa huffaz experienced, and how to estimate your own child’s timeline.

> **Key takeaways**
> - The standard mushaf has 604 pages of 15 lines, in 30 juz of roughly 20 pages each.
> - At one page a day, five days a week, the memorization alone takes about three years at a realistic 40 active weeks a year. At half a page a day, about six years.
> - Revision, consolidation weeks, exams and holidays always add time. Plan for them rather than fighting them.
> - Speed is not the goal. Strong, accurate retention is, and the reward is in the effort as well as the finish.

## How long does it take to memorize the Quran? The short answer

**A realistic estimate for most students is two to six years for the whole Quran, depending mainly on their daily pace and consistency.** Full-time students in a dedicated hifz program sit at the faster end. Part-time students who attend regular school usually sit at the slower end, and that is perfectly normal.

| Commitment | Typical new memorization | Realistic timeline for the whole Quran |
|---|---|---|
| Full-time hifz school | About 1 page or more a day | Often about 2–3 years |
| Strong part-time (evening classes + daily home work) | About half a page to 1 page a day | About 3–6 years |
| Light part-time (weekend class + some home revision) | A few lines a day or 1–2 pages a week | Often 7 years or more |

These ranges are estimates built from the page maths below, not guarantees. A child who revises well and rarely misses a day will beat the table. A child who keeps restarting will fall behind it.

## The basic maths of hifz

The most widely used mushaf, the one shown on [Quran.com’s page view](https://quran.com/page/604), has **604 pages, 15 lines per page**. The Quran contains **114 surahs** and, in the common count, **6,236 ayat**, divided into **30 juz**.

Most juz are about 20 pages. A few useful benchmarks from that mushaf:

| Portion | Pages | Approximate length |
|---|---|---|
| Juz Amma (Juz 30) | Pages 582–604 | 23 pages, but many short surahs |
| Juz Tabarak (Juz 29) | Pages 562–581 | 20 pages |
| Surah Al-Baqarah | Pages 2–49 | 48 pages |
| The whole Quran | Pages 1–604 | 604 pages |

So the question “how long does it take to memorize the Quran” is really “how many pages a week, for how many weeks?”

## Timelines by daily pace

The table below assumes new memorization **five days a week** and **40 active weeks a year**. Forty weeks is deliberately conservative: it leaves room for holidays, illness, exams, Ramadan adjustments and the revision-only weeks every hifz student needs.

| Daily new lesson | Pages per week | Years for the whole Quran |
|---|---|---|
| 3 lines a day | 1 | About 15 years |
| 5 lines (a third of a page) a day | About 1.7 | About 9 years |
| Half a page a day | 2.5 | About 6 years |
| 1 page a day | 5 | About 3 years |
| 2 pages a day | 10 | About 1.5 years |

Two things stand out. First, small increases in daily pace change the timeline dramatically: moving from three lines to half a page cuts the time by more than half. Second, even three lines a day is real progress. Fifteen years sounds long, but a child who starts at eight with three lines a day and slowly increases the portion as they mature will often finish far sooner.

### Why revision makes the real timeline longer

The table only counts new pages. In practice, students pause new lessons to strengthen what they know: after every juz, before tests, and whenever older portions weaken. Many teachers build in these consolidation periods on purpose.

The Prophet ﷺ said: “The example of the person who knows the Qur’an by heart is like the owner of tied camels. If he keeps them tied, he will control them, but if he releases them, they will run away” ([Sahih al-Bukhari 5031](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:5031)). Revision is the rope. A student who memorizes fast but revises poorly may have to re-memorize whole juz, which costs far more time than steady revision would have. Our [hifz revision schedule](/blog/hifz-revision-schedule) shows how to balance the two.

## What real timelines look like

In 2023, [CBC Ottawa spoke with three local huffaz](https://www.cbc.ca/news/canada/ottawa/memorizing-qur-an-ottawa-hafiz-1.6799786) whose paths show how wide the range is:

- **Starting young and building up.** One boy began at about age six with ten verses a day. When that became easy he moved to whole pages, and at his peak he was memorizing five pages a day. He had the whole Quran by heart by age ten, with his mother helping him review most of the time.
- **A three-year full-time program.** A girl joined a dedicated program at Al-Furqan School in Ottawa at 11, started each day with a new page, and became a hafizah at 14. She described the first year as the hardest.
- **A decade alongside life.** The boy’s teacher, an Ottawa imam, started at 18 and took about ten years while juggling school, work and family.

All three finished. None followed the same timeline. That is the most honest lesson for parents.

## Factors that change the timeline

**Reading fluency.** A child who reads the mushaf smoothly memorizes faster and makes fewer mistakes. If reading is still slow, a few months on reading first often saves time overall. See [how to teach a child to read Quran](/blog/quran-reading-for-beginners-kids).

**Age and maturity.** Young children memorize by ear quickly but need short sessions. Older children can handle longer lessons and manage their own revision. Our guide to [the best age to start hifz](/blog/best-age-to-start-hifz) covers readiness by age.

**Consistency.** Five short sessions a week beat one long one. Missing days is the biggest hidden cause of slow progress.

**Teacher quality.** A qualified teacher who hears every lesson prevents mistakes from becoming habits, which avoids painful re-memorization later.

**Similar verses.** Some passages repeat with small differences (mutashabihat). Students often slow down in these sections, and that is expected.

**Family support.** Someone at home who knows what to revise each day makes an enormous difference, as the CBC example shows.

**Format.** Full-time students have more hours. Part-time students have more years. Our guide to [part time hifz](/blog/part-time-hifz) explains how to make the slower route work.

## Can a child memorize the Quran in one year?

It happens, but rarely, and usually with a full-time student who already reads fluently, has years of short-surah memorization behind them, and spends most of each day on hifz. Two pages a day for 40 weeks is about 400 pages, so a one-year finish requires closer to three pages a day with very few breaks.

For most children, a one-year target creates more risk than reward: shaky revision, burnout and, too often, a child who finishes but cannot hold it. A steady pace that a child can sustain for years is the safer goal.

There is comfort here for the slower child. The Prophet ﷺ said that the one who recites the Quran and memorizes it will be with the noble, righteous scribes, and the one who works hard at it while finding it difficult will have a double reward ([Sahih al-Bukhari 4937](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:4937)).

## How to estimate your child’s timeline

**Step 1: Count the pages remaining.** Subtract what your child already knows solidly. If they know Juz Amma, about 581 pages remain.

**Step 2: Measure a realistic weekly pace.** Track four normal weeks of new lessons that your child can recite cleanly the next day. Average them.

**Step 3: Choose your active weeks.** Use 40 for a typical school-year family, fewer if you expect long breaks.

**Step 4: Divide.** Pages remaining ÷ pages per week ÷ active weeks = years.

**Step 5: Add a buffer.** Add about 15–25% for consolidation periods and revision catch-up.

**Example:** A 10-year-old knows Juz Amma and averages 2.5 clean pages a week. 581 ÷ 2.5 ÷ 40 is about 5.8 years; with a 20% buffer, about 7 years. If they move up to five pages a week at 12, the total drops sharply. Recalculate every term.
