Private kindergarten in Ottawa is a tuition-based JK/SK program run by an independent school instead of a public school board. Families choose it for a specific approach (Montessori, faith-based, bilingual, IB-style), smaller groups, or a particular culture. Tuition ranges from a few thousand dollars a year to well over $20,000, and what you get for that money varies a lot. This guide explains how private JK/SK works in Ontario, what tuition usually covers, and how to compare schools fairly.

> **Key takeaways**
> - Public full-day kindergarten is free in Ontario. Private kindergarten in Ottawa charges tuition, and the province does not fund, license or inspect private elementary schools.
> - Private schools do not have to follow the Ontario curriculum or hire certified teachers, so you need to ask each school directly.
> - Compare the full annual cost (tuition, registration, resource and extra fees), not the headline monthly price.
> - The best private kindergarten is the one whose approach, hours, location and values match your family, not the most expensive one.

## What is private kindergarten in Ottawa?

In Ontario, kindergarten has two years: Junior Kindergarten (JK) for children who turn 4 by December 31 of the school year, and Senior Kindergarten (SK) for children who turn 5. Every school board in Ottawa offers free full-day kindergarten, and the province notes that [kindergarten is optional](https://www.ontario.ca/page/kindergarten). Compulsory schooling starts at age 6.

A private kindergarten is the same JK/SK stage, offered by a school that operates independently. According to the Ontario government's [private schools page](https://www.ontario.ca/page/private-schools), private schools are businesses or non-profit organizations that must submit a Notice of Intention to Operate each year. The Ministry of Education does not fund, regulate, license or accredit them, and it does not inspect private elementary schools.

That independence is the whole point for many families. It lets a school build its own day around a philosophy, a language or a faith. It also means the quality checks that come with public school are not automatic, so the research falls on you.

## Types of private kindergarten in Ottawa

Ottawa's private schools cluster into a few broad types. Many combine two or more.

### Montessori kindergartens

Montessori schools use mixed-age classrooms, hands-on materials and a long, uninterrupted work period. Some run a three-year "Casa" cycle that blends preschool, JK and SK. The word "Montessori" is not protected, so ask about teacher training and how closely the school follows the method.

### Faith-based kindergartens

These include Islamic, Christian, Jewish and other religious schools. They teach the academic basics alongside faith, prayer, scripture and values. In Ottawa, Muslim families can choose from several full-time options, which we list in our guide to [Islamic schools in Ottawa](/blog/islamic-schools-in-ottawa-guide).

### Bilingual and language-focused kindergartens

Some private schools teach mostly in French, some offer English-French programs, and a few teach a third language every day, such as Arabic or Mandarin. If language is your main goal, look at how many minutes a day children actually hear and use it.

### Independent and IB-style schools

Larger independent schools often run JK through Grade 8 or Grade 12, sometimes with an International Baccalaureate Primary Years Programme. They tend to sit at the higher end of the tuition range and may offer specialist teachers, larger campuses and extended-day care.

## How much does private kindergarten in Ottawa cost?

There is no single price. Directory listings for Ottawa private elementary schools show yearly tuition from about $3,000–$5,000 at some smaller Montessori and part-day programs to more than $20,000 at some independent and IB schools. Many full-day faith-based and Montessori kindergartens fall somewhere in between. Prices change every year, so always confirm directly with the school.

The bigger trap is comparing headline numbers. A school quoting a low monthly price may bill for 12 months, while another bills for 10. One may include supplies and field trips, while another charges for everything separately.

### Fees to ask about

- **Application or registration fee**, often non-refundable
- **Deposit** to hold a space, and whether it comes off tuition
- **Resource, supply or technology fee**
- **Uniform**, if required
- **Before- and after-school care**, and whether it costs extra
- **Field trips, events and special programs**
- **Sibling discounts and payment plans**

### Is private kindergarten tuition tax-deductible?

Sometimes, in part, and it depends on the facts. The CRA does not allow tuition for an educational program as a child care expense, but its guidance says payments to a school for a child under compulsory school age (6 in Ontario) are generally treated as child care unless the facts indicate otherwise. Separately billed before- and after-school care is the clearest claim. We walk through the CRA rules, and what receipts to ask for, in our guide to the [child care expense deduction for kindergarten](/blog/kindergarten-tax-deductions-ontario). Always confirm with the CRA or a tax professional.

## What you get for private kindergarten tuition

When you pay tuition, you are paying for choices a public school cannot always make. The most common ones are below. Not every private school offers all of them.

**A specific approach.** A Montessori prepared environment, a faith-centred day, an immersion language, or a particular discipline philosophy.

**Smaller groups.** Many private schools keep classes smaller than the public average. Ask for the actual number of children per class and per adult, not a marketing range.

**Values and community.** For many Muslim families, the biggest value is a day where salah, duas, halal food and Islamic manners are normal, not exceptions.

**Extra subjects.** Daily second or third language, Quran, music, or outdoor education built into the day.

**Continuity.** Some schools run from JK to Grade 8 or beyond, so you avoid a switch after SK. Others, like QAF, specialize only in kindergarten and focus everything on ages 4 and 5.

What you do **not** automatically get is government oversight. The province is clear that it does not inspect private elementary schools for curriculum, health and safety, or staffing. That makes your own visit and questions essential.

## How to check a private kindergarten's quality

Because private elementary schools are not inspected, you have to do the checking. These questions are drawn from the Ontario government's advice to parents and from what we see families ask on visits.

**Step 1: Confirm the school is on Ontario's list.** The province publishes [private school contact information](https://data.ontario.ca/dataset/private-school-contact-information) for every school that submitted a Notice of Intention to Operate. The ministry notes the list is not an endorsement.

**Step 2: Ask about the curriculum.** Does the school align with the [Ontario kindergarten curriculum](https://www.dcp.edu.gov.on.ca/en/curriculum/kindergarten), and how? Ontario introduced a new kindergarten curriculum in September 2026. A school that aligns makes the move to Grade 1 anywhere smoother.

**Step 3: Ask who teaches.** Private school teachers do not need Ontario College of Teachers certification. Ask about qualifications, early-years training and experience. You can search a certified teacher's name on the College of Teachers' public register.

**Step 4: Ask about safety.** Ask whether all staff have police vulnerable sector checks, whether the school has liability insurance, and what its emergency, allergy and pick-up procedures are.

**Step 5: Read the contract.** Check refund rules, withdrawal notice periods, what happens if a class is cancelled, and how fee increases work.

**Step 6: Visit during a real school day.** Watch how adults speak to children, how transitions work, and whether children look busy and calm. Our [questions to ask on a school tour](/blog/questions-to-ask-islamic-school-tour) list gives you 40 to choose from.

> **Tip:** Ask every school the same five questions and write down the answers. It is the fastest way to compare fairly, and you will notice which schools answer clearly.

## Questions parents ask about timing and admissions

### Can my child start private JK earlier than public JK?

Public JK follows the December 31 age rule. Private schools set their own admission ages. Some take children who turn 4 later, and Montessori schools may enrol 3-year-olds into a preschool year. If your child has a late birthday, ask how the school decides between JK and a preschool year.

### When should I apply?

Many Ottawa private schools open applications in the fall or winter for the following September, and popular programs can fill early. Book visits by late winter if you want options.

### Can I switch from private to public later?

Yes. Your child keeps the right to attend your local public school. If the private school aligns with the Ontario curriculum, the transition to Grade 1 is usually smooth.
