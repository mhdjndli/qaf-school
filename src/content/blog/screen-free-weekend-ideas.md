The best screen-free weekend activities for kids are simple: time outdoors, hands-on making, cooking together, books, board games and small acts of service. Below are 40 ideas grouped by type, with ages and cost, plus a step-by-step plan to make a screen-free weekend actually stick and Ottawa-specific outings for every season.

> **Key takeaways**
> - Canada's 24-Hour Movement Guidelines recommend no more than 2 hours a day of recreational screen time for ages 5 to 17; for ages 2 to 5, the Canadian Paediatric Society suggests about an hour or less.
> - Plan the weekend before it starts: children resist "no screens" far less when they already know what they will do instead.
> - Mix one outing, one making project and one family ritual each day.
> - Most ideas here are free or nearly free.
> - Parents set the tone: phones away at meals and outings makes the biggest difference.

## Why a screen-free weekend matters

A screen-free weekend is a planned stretch, a day, an afternoon or the whole weekend, when the family puts away tablets, TV, gaming and phones and fills the time with play, people and the outdoors.

The goal is not to make screens the enemy. It is to protect what screens crowd out. The [Canadian Paediatric Society](https://caringforkids.cps.ca/handouts/behavior-and-development/screen-time-and-young-children) notes that young children learn best from face-to-face interaction and that screen time often replaces outdoor play, creating and family downtime. Canada's [24-Hour Movement Guidelines](https://csepguidelines.ca/guidelines/children-youth/) recommend that children and youth aged 5 to 17 get at least 60 minutes of moderate to vigorous activity a day and no more than 2 hours of recreational screen time.

Weekends are usually where screen time spikes, which makes them the easiest place to win it back.

## 40 screen-free weekend activities for kids

Each idea lists the ages it suits best. Pick two or three per day and mix active, creative and quiet options.

### Outdoor ideas

1. **Nature scavenger hunt.** Make a list (pine cone, red leaf, smooth stone, bird) and head to a local park. Ages 3+. Free.
2. **Reflect on creation walk.** On a walk, pause to notice the sky, trees and animals, and share a short verse about reflecting on creation (see the faith section below). Ages 4+. Free.
3. **Bike or scooter loop.** Pick a new path each weekend and let the kids choose the route on a paper map. Ages 4+. Free.
4. **Backyard or park picnic.** Kids pack the basket and plan the menu. Ages 3+. Low cost.
5. **Tobogganing or snow fort building.** Ottawa winters make this an easy weekly ritual. Ages 3+. Free.
6. **Puddle walk.** Rain boots and rain jackets turn a grey Saturday into an adventure. Ages 2+. Free.
7. **Bird counting.** Count birds at a feeder or park for 15 minutes and keep a tally sheet. Ages 5+. Free.
8. **Sidewalk chalk art and games.** Hopscotch, mazes, murals. Ages 3+. Low cost.

### Making and building ideas

9. **LEGO challenge cards.** Write challenges on cards: build a bridge that holds a book, a tower taller than your knee, a car that rolls down a ramp. Ages 4+.
10. **Cardboard city.** Save boxes all week and build a city, castle or masjid on Saturday. Ages 4+. Free.
11. **Paper plane contest.** Fold three designs, test which flies furthest and talk about why. Ages 6+. Free.
12. **Calligraphy and colouring.** Older kids trace Arabic letters or simple words; younger ones colour geometric patterns. Ages 5+. Low cost.
13. **Homemade playdough.** Mix, colour and sculpt. Ages 2+. Low cost.
14. **Family art gallery.** Everyone makes one piece, then hang them and give a "gallery tour" to grandparents. Ages 3+.

### Kitchen ideas

15. **Bake bread or flatbread together.** Kneading is great for little hands and patience. Ages 3+.
16. **Pizza night with a kid-made menu.** Children choose toppings, write the menu and serve. Ages 4+.
17. **Plan and prep school lunches.** Let kids pick and pack Monday's lunch, with halal, nut-free options. Ages 4+.
18. **Make a dessert for a neighbour.** Combine cooking with kindness. Ages 4+.

### Books, games and quiet-time ideas

19. **Library trip with a mission.** Each child picks one book on a new topic, one story and one book to read to a sibling. The [Ottawa Public Library](https://biblioottawalibrary.ca/en) is free, and branches run weekend programs. All ages.
20. **Family read-aloud.** One chapter after Maghrib or before bed. Our list of [the best Islamic books for kids](/blog/best-islamic-books-for-kids) is a good starting point. All ages.
21. **Board game tournament.** Pick three games and keep a family scoreboard. Ages 4+.
22. **Puzzle on the table all weekend.** Anyone who walks by adds a piece. Ages 4+.
23. **Story cubes or "make up a story" circle.** Each person adds one sentence. Ages 4+. Free.
24. **Letter writing.** Write and post a letter or card to grandparents or a relative abroad. Ages 5+.

### Faith and family ideas

25. **Weekend family halaqa.** Fifteen minutes: one short surah, one hadith, one story of a prophet, one question for discussion. All ages.
26. **Quran revision game.** Take turns starting a verse from a short surah and see who can continue it. Ages 5+.
27. **Pray together at the masjid.** Attend a congregational prayer and stay for a few minutes to meet people. All ages.
28. **Visit family or elders.** Keeping ties of kinship is part of faith, and children remember these visits. All ages.
29. **Dua jar.** Write duas on slips of paper and pick one each night. Ages 4+.
30. **Reflection walk.** The Quran invites us to look at the world around us:

أَفَلَا يَنظُرُونَ إِلَى ٱلْإِبِلِ كَيْفَ خُلِقَتْ

“Then do they not look at the camels – how they are created?” (Surah Al-Ghashiyah 88:17, [Saheeh International](https://quran.com/88/17)). Ask children what they notice and how it shows Allah's design. Ages 4+.

### Service and kindness ideas

31. **Sadaqah jar.** Kids add coins all week and choose a cause together on the weekend. Ages 3+.
32. **Food bank sorting box.** Collect non-perishables as a family and drop them off. Ages 4+.
33. **Help a neighbour.** Shovel a walk, rake leaves or carry groceries. Ages 6+.
34. **Declutter and donate.** Each child picks toys and clothes to give away. Ages 4+.

### Learning-without-screens ideas

35. **Kitchen science.** Baking soda volcanoes, density towers, ice melting races. Ages 4+.
36. **Indoor or windowsill garden.** Plant herbs or beans and measure growth weekly. Ages 3+.
37. **Learn a new skill together.** Knitting, a card game, simple woodworking, or a few Arabic words around the house. Ages 5+.
38. **Map making.** Draw a map of your home, street or neighbourhood with a legend. Ages 6+.
39. **Family "museum" day.** Kids research one topic in library books and present it with drawings. Ages 6+.
40. **Weekend journal.** A notebook where each child draws or writes one thing they did and one thing they are grateful for. Ages 4+.

## How to plan a screen-free weekend that sticks

**Step 1: Agree on the rules together.** Decide what counts (tablets, TV, gaming, phones), when it applies (all weekend, or until dinner), and any exceptions, like a video call with grandparents.

**Step 2: Make a visible plan.** On Friday, write two or three activities per day on paper and stick it on the fridge. Children cope better with "no screens" when they know what comes next.

**Step 3: Prepare the materials.** Boxes, paper, a library bag, ingredients, a charged bike pump. Friction kills good plans.

**Step 4: Park the devices.** Put tablets and controllers in a basket or drawer out of sight. Parents' phones go there too during meals and outings.

**Step 5: Expect boredom, and allow it.** The first hour is the hardest. Boredom often turns into the most creative play if adults don't rush to fill it.

**Step 6: Review on Sunday night.** Ask: What was the best part? What should we do next weekend? Let children own the next plan.

## Screen-free weekends in Ottawa

Ottawa is full of free and low-cost places to go without a screen:

- **Gatineau Park:** trails, lookouts and snowshoeing, managed by the [National Capital Commission](https://ncc-ccn.gc.ca/places/gatineau-park). Check parking fees and trail conditions before you go.
- **Ottawa Public Library branches:** free books, puzzles and weekend programs in every neighbourhood.
- **Winter tobogganing hills and outdoor rinks** across the city.
- **Museums with free admission times**, such as the Ingenium museums, which are free daily from 4 to 5 PM.

For a full seasonal list, see our guide to [things to do with kids in Ottawa](/blog/things-to-do-with-kids-ottawa), and for structured options see [kids soccer in Ottawa](/blog/kids-soccer-ottawa-weekend).

## The bottom line

Screen-free weekends do not need to be elaborate. Plan a few simple activities, prepare what you need, put the devices away, and let your children be a little bored. With a few weekends of practice, most families find the house calmer and the kids more creative.
