The best Eid al-Adha activities for kids connect the fun of Eid to its story: telling the story of Prophet Ibrahim, practising the takbir, making a Kaaba or sheep craft, and helping share food with others. Eid al-Adha 2027 is expected on **Sunday, May 16, 2027** (or Monday, May 17), depending on moon sighting. This guide explains Eid al-Adha for kids in simple words, retells the story from the Quran, and gives 16 Eid al-Adha activities for kids for the ten days before Eid and Eid day itself.

> **Key takeaways**
> - Eid al-Adha, the “Festival of Sacrifice,” falls on 10 Dhul Hijjah, the day after the Day of Arafah during Hajj.
> - In 2027 it is expected on Sunday, May 16, with the Day of Arafah on Saturday, May 15 (depending on moon sighting; confirm with your mosque).
> - The heart of the story is Prophet Ibrahim’s and his son’s trust in Allah, and the lesson that “what reaches Him is piety from you” (Surah Al-Hajj 22:37).
> - The first ten days of Dhul Hijjah are among the best days for good deeds, so start your activities early in May.
> - Explain the sacrifice (qurbani) honestly but gently, focusing on gratitude and sharing.

## When is Eid al-Adha 2027?

Eid al-Adha 1448 AH is expected on **Sunday, May 16, 2027**. That is the date in the Saudi Umm al-Qura calendar used for the projection at [moonsighting.com](https://moonsighting.com/ramadan-eid.html). The UAE Astronomy Centre’s estimates, reported by [Khaleej Times](https://www.khaleejtimes.com/uae/ramadan-eid-al-fitr-eid-al-adha-2027-likely-dates), give May 16 or 17. Many North American communities follow the Day of Arafah announced in Makkah, so final dates are confirmed only a few days before.

| Milestone | Islamic date | Expected date (2027) |
|---|---|---|
| Dhul Hijjah begins (first ten days start) | 1 Dhul Hijjah | Around Friday, May 7 |
| Day of Arafah | 9 Dhul Hijjah | Around Saturday, May 15 |

*All dates are expected, depending on moon sighting.*

A Sunday Eid is good news for families with children in school. If Eid moves to Monday, Ontario school boards accommodate absences for religious holy days. The Ottawa-Carleton District School Board, for example, publishes a [calendar of religious and creed-based days of observance](https://www.ocdsb.ca/news-events/calendar/religious-creed-based-days-of-observance) that includes Eid al-Adha.

## What is Eid al-Adha? Explaining it to kids

Eid al-Adha is one of the two Eids in Islam. It is celebrated on the 10th day of Dhul Hijjah, the twelfth month of the Islamic calendar, while millions of pilgrims are completing Hajj in Makkah. Muslims around the world pray the Eid prayer, say the takbir, wear their best clothes, visit family and share meat from a sacrificed animal with family, friends and people in need.

For a young child, you can say: *“Eid al-Adha is the big Eid when we remember how Prophet Ibrahim loved Allah so much that he was ready to give up anything for Him. Allah was so pleased that He gave him a ram instead. So on Eid we thank Allah and share food with everyone.”*

## The story of Prophet Ibrahim for kids

Tell the story in short scenes, pausing to ask questions. It is told in the Quran in Surah As-Saffat, verses 99–111, which you can read with translation on [Quran.com](https://quran.com/37/99-111).

**Scene 1: A prayer for a son.** Prophet Ibrahim (peace be upon him) asked Allah for a righteous child, and Allah gave him the good news of a gentle, patient son. Most scholars understand this son to be Ismail.

**Scene 2: A hard dream.** When the boy was old enough to work alongside his father, Ibrahim saw in a dream that he was sacrificing his son. The dreams of prophets are true, so he knew it was a command from Allah. He did not force his son. He asked him what he thought:

فَلَمَّا بَلَغَ مَعَهُ ٱلسَّعْىَ قَالَ يَـٰبُنَىَّ إِنِّىٓ أَرَىٰ فِى ٱلْمَنَامِ أَنِّىٓ أَذْبَحُكَ فَٱنظُرْ مَاذَا تَرَىٰ ۚ قَالَ يَـٰٓأَبَتِ ٱفْعَلْ مَا تُؤْمَرُ ۖ سَتَجِدُنِىٓ إِن شَآءَ ٱللَّهُ مِنَ ٱلصَّـٰبِرِينَ

“O my son, indeed I have seen in a dream that I [must] sacrifice you, so see what you think.” He said, “O my father, do as you are commanded. You will find me, if Allah wills, of the steadfast” (Surah As-Saffat 37:102).

**Scene 3: Both trusted Allah.** Father and son both submitted to Allah’s command. At that moment Allah called out to Ibrahim that he had fulfilled the vision (37:104–105).

**Scene 4: A great ransom.** Allah ransomed the boy “with a great sacrifice” (37:107), a ram. No one was harmed. Allah had tested their hearts, and they had passed.

**Questions to ask your child:**

- How do you think Ismail felt when his father asked him?
- Why did Ibrahim ask his son instead of just telling him?
- What is something hard you can do because Allah asked us to (praying on time, telling the truth, sharing)?

## The first ten days of Dhul Hijjah with kids

The countdown to Eid is worship too. The Prophet (peace be upon him) said that no good deeds done on other days are better than those done in these ten days ([Sahih al-Bukhari 969](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:969)). Turn that into a ten-day plan:

| Day | Expected date (2027) | Kids’ good deed |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Fri, May 7 | Learn the Eid takbir |
| 2 | Sat, May 8 | Help a neighbour or grandparent |
| 3 | Sun, May 9 | Read the Ibrahim story together |
| 4 | Mon, May 10 | Give sadaqah from pocket money |
| 5 | Tue, May 11 | Pray one prayer on time with a parent |
| 6 | Wed, May 12 | Make Eid cards for teachers |
| 7 | Thu, May 13 | Learn about Hajj: the Kaaba and Safa–Marwa |
| 8 | Fri, May 14 | Tidy and donate toys |
| 9 | Sat, May 15 (Arafah) | Make a long dua list together |
| 10 | Sun, May 16 (Eid) | Takbir, Eid prayer, sharing |

Adults are encouraged to fast on the Day of Arafah; the Prophet said it expiates the sins of the previous and coming year (Sahih Muslim 1162b). Children are not required to fast. If an older child wants to try, keep it short and follow the same gentle, step-by-step approach families use for a first Ramadan fast.

## 16 Eid al-Adha activities for kids

The ideas are grouped into crafts (1–4), learning and worship (5–8), Eid day (9–12) and sharing (13–16). Pick one or two from each group.

### 1. Paper plate sheep

Glue cotton balls on a paper plate and add a black paper face and legs. Use it to retell the story of the ram. **Best for:** ages 3–7.

### 2. Kaaba box model

Cover a small square box in black paper with a gold strip near the top. Children can walk a toy figure around it seven times to learn about tawaf. **Best for:** ages 4–9.

### 3. Eid bunting

Cut triangles, write one letter of “EID MUBARAK” on each and hang them across the living room. **Best for:** ages 4–10.

### 4. Eid cards for neighbours

Make simple cards explaining Eid al-Adha in one sentence and deliver them with a treat. **Best for:** ages 5–12.

### 5. Takbir practice

Practise a widely used form of the Eid takbir in the days before Eid:

اللَّهُ أَكْبَرُ اللَّهُ أَكْبَرُ، لَا إِلَٰهَ إِلَّا اللَّهُ، وَاللَّهُ أَكْبَرُ، اللَّهُ أَكْبَرُ، وَلِلَّهِ الْحَمْدُ

*Allāhu akbar, Allāhu akbar, lā ilāha illallāh, wallāhu akbar, Allāhu akbar, wa lillāhil-ḥamd*: “Allah is the Greatest, Allah is the Greatest, there is no god but Allah, Allah is the Greatest, Allah is the Greatest, and to Allah belongs all praise.” Allah links the sacrifice to glorifying Him in Surah Al-Hajj 22:37. **Best for:** ages 3+.

### 6. Story puppet show

Make paper-bag puppets of Ibrahim, Ismail and the ram, and let children act out the four scenes above. **Best for:** ages 4–9.

### 7. Arafah dua jar

On the Day of Arafah, everyone writes duas on slips of paper and puts them in a jar. Read them together before Maghrib. **Best for:** ages 5+.

### 8. Learn Surah Al-Kawthar

This three-verse surah includes the command “So pray to your Lord and offer sacrifice” (108:2), a perfect link to Eid al-Adha. **Best for:** ages 4–8.

### 9. Eid morning checklist

Bath, best clothes, a little perfume for older children, then takbir in the car. A picture checklist helps young children get ready without rushing. **Best for:** ages 3–8.

### 10. Eat after the prayer

The Prophet would not eat on Eid al-Adha until after he had prayed (Jami’ at-Tirmidhi 542). Plan a special family breakfast or brunch for after the prayer. Young children can of course have a snack if they need one. **Best for:** all ages.

### 11. The different-way-home game

The Prophet used to return from the Eid prayer by a different route from the one he took there (Sahih al-Bukhari 986). Let children choose the new route and spot five things they have never noticed before. **Best for:** ages 4–12.

### 12. Eid gift exchange

Small, thoughtful gifts make the day memorable. Our [Eid gift ideas for kids](/blog/eid-gift-ideas-for-kids) list ideas by age and budget. **Best for:** all ages.

### 13. Sharing bags

After the qurbani meat arrives, children help pack portions for relatives, neighbours and families in need. **Best for:** ages 5–12.

### 14. Food drive basket

Many families cannot do their own qurbani locally. Let children fill a basket of non-perishables for a food bank or mosque drive in the week of Eid. **Best for:** ages 4+.

### 15. “What will I give for Allah?” reflection

Older children write one thing they will give up or give away for Allah’s sake, like screen time on one evening or a favourite toy for donation. **Best for:** ages 8–15.

### 16. Eid picnic in the park

Mid-May is usually mild in Ottawa. Pack a picnic and invite another family. Our guide to [things to do with kids in Ottawa](/blog/things-to-do-with-kids-ottawa) suggests parks and spring outings. **Best for:** all ages.

## Explaining qurbani (the sacrifice) gently

Some children worry when they hear that animals are sacrificed. Be honest and calm. The Prophet (peace be upon him) himself sacrificed two rams, saying Allah’s name and the takbir (Sahih al-Bukhari 5558), and Muslims who can afford it follow this Sunnah.

Allah explains the purpose of the sacrifice in Surah Al-Hajj:

لَن يَنَالَ ٱللَّهَ لُحُومُهَا وَلَا دِمَآؤُهَا وَلَـٰكِن يَنَالُهُ ٱلتَّقْوَىٰ مِنكُمْ ۚ كَذَٰلِكَ سَخَّرَهَا لَكُمْ لِتُكَبِّرُوا۟ ٱللَّهَ عَلَىٰ مَا هَدَىٰكُمْ ۗ وَبَشِّرِ ٱلْمُحْسِنِينَ

Focus on three ideas children can understand:

- **It is about the heart, not the meat.** Allah says: “Their meat will not reach Allah, nor will their blood, but what reaches Him is piety from you” (Surah Al-Hajj 22:37).
- **Animals must be treated kindly.** In Islam an animal for sacrifice must be healthy, well cared for and slaughtered quickly and with mercy.
- **Sharing is the point.** The Prophet told the companions to keep some of the meat and give the rest in charity (Sahih Muslim 1971). Many families divide it between their household, relatives and friends, and people in need.

In Canada, most families arrange their qurbani through a halal butcher or a charity, sometimes overseas. Young children do not need to watch. Hearing the story and helping to share the meat is enough.

## Eid al-Adha in Ottawa

Ottawa mosques and community organisations hold Eid prayers on the morning of Eid, often in larger venues to fit the crowds. Times and locations are usually announced a week or two ahead, so follow your mosque’s website or social media in early May 2027. Arrive early with children, bring a prayer mat and plan for parking.

With Eid expected on a Sunday, many families will spend the afternoon visiting relatives or at a park. If Eid falls on Monday, write to your child’s teacher in advance to let them know your child will be absent for Eid.
