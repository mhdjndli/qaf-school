Yes, you can switch schools in the middle of the year in Ontario, and for kindergarten children it is usually smoother than parents expect. Switching schools mid year in Ontario comes down to three things: securing a spot at the new school, giving notice to the old one, and making sure your child's Ontario Student Record (OSR) follows them. This guide walks through each step, with extra detail for families moving from a public board to an Islamic or private school.

> **Key takeaways**
> - Ontario has no rule against changing schools mid-year. Public boards register students year-round, and private schools admit students whenever they have space.
> - Your child's **OSR** transfers to the new school. For private schools, the old principal needs a written request from the new school and a signed parent consent.
> - Children are not legally required to attend school until **age six**, so JK and SK moves carry less paperwork pressure.
> - The best windows are natural breaks: after winter break, after March Break, or right after a reporting period.
> - Secure the new spot in writing **before** you withdraw, and plan a short, warm transition for your child.

## Switching schools mid year in Ontario: the rules

There is no provincial "transfer window." A family can move a child between Ontario schools at any point in the school year. What changes is who does the paperwork.

**Public to public (same or different board).** You register at the new school (in Ottawa, through the board's online registration and [OCDSB School Locator](https://www.ocdsb.ca/our_schools/school_locator) or the Catholic board's equivalent). The new school then requests the OSR. If you want a school other than your designated one, expect a cross-boundary application, which is not always approved. Our [comparison of Ottawa's school boards](/blog/ottawa-school-boards-compared) covers the four boards and how each works.

**Public to private (including Islamic schools).** You apply directly to the private school. Once accepted, the private school sends a written request for the OSR, and you sign a consent to transfer. Each private school sets its own admissions process, timeline and fees.

**Private to public.** You register with the public board like any new family. The principal places your child in a grade based on age and records, then explains the placement to you.

### What the Ministry says about placement

Ontario's [policy on student records and transfers](https://www.ontario.ca/document/ontario-schools-kindergarten-grade-12-policy-and-program-requirements-2024/student-records) says that when an elementary student moves between Ontario schools, the receiving principal uses the report card and OSR to place the child in the right grade. For students arriving from a private school, home schooling or outside Ontario, the principal places the child in an age-appropriate grade after reviewing records and talking with the family.

For JK and SK, placement is simple: it follows your child's birth year.

## Is it bad to switch schools mid-year?

Changing schools mid-year is not harmful in itself. Much of the research on school moves looks at changes forced by upheaval, such as evictions, job losses or family breakdown. A move you choose deliberately, to a better fit, is a different situation.

That said, every change has a cost. Your child loses familiar friends, routines and a teacher they know. Older children may meet small gaps where the new school teaches topics in a different order. The question is whether the benefit outweighs that cost.

**Reasons that usually justify a mid-year move:**

- Persistent unhappiness or anxiety that has not improved after talking with the teacher and principal
- Bullying or safety concerns that the school has not resolved
- A program mismatch you cannot fix, such as wanting daily Arabic, Quran and an Islamic environment that your current school does not offer
- A family move that makes the daily commute unworkable
- A spot opening up at a school you had already chosen but were waitlisted for

**Reasons that are often worth waiting out:**

- A rough first few weeks of JK (separation tears are normal; see our guide to [kindergarten separation anxiety](/blog/kindergarten-separation-anxiety))
- A single conflict with one classmate
- Small differences in reputation between two similar schools

If you are weighing public school against an Islamic school more generally, our [Islamic school vs public school comparison](/blog/islamic-school-vs-public-school-ontario) sets out the trade-offs honestly.

## Kindergarten moves are the easiest

Kindergarten is the gentlest time to change schools. Ontario's policy is clear that parents are [not required to send a child to school until age six](https://www.ontario.ca/document/ontario-schools-kindergarten-grade-12-policy-and-program-requirements-2024/student-records). JK and SK are optional, play-based years, so there are no credits, exams or graded subjects to "lose."

Ontario's kindergarten reporting also lends itself to a mid-year move. Public kindergarten students receive an [Initial Observations report in the fall](https://www.ontario.ca/page/kindergarten-communication-learning), followed by two Communication of Learning reports later in the year. Bring copies of whatever your child has received to the new school. They help the new educators see where your child is, though no new kindergarten teacher expects a four-year-old to arrive "on track" with a fixed syllabus.

From the classroom side, we see four- and five-year-olds settle into a new group quickly when the routine is predictable and the adults are warm. Children this age form friendships through play, not shared history.

## Best time to switch schools during the year

You can move any week, but some windows are easier:

| Timing | Why it works | Watch out for |
|---|---|---|
| After winter break (January) | Everyone is "restarting" after two weeks off | Fall report may arrive only days before you leave |
| After March Break | Fresh start for spring; weather is better for outdoor play | Fewer months left to settle before summer |
| Right after a reporting period | You leave with an up-to-date record | Report dates vary by board |
| Late fall (after Thanksgiving) | Early enough to build friendships for the year | Initial observations may still be in progress |
| May or June | Rarely worth it unless you are moving home | Very little time to settle; consider waiting for September |

If your concern is the program rather than an urgent problem, starting at a break gives your child the cleanest beginning.

## Step-by-step: how to transfer schools mid-year

### Step 1: Secure the new spot in writing

Never withdraw first. Confirm that the new school has space, that your child has been accepted, and the exact start date. For private schools, ask for the enrolment agreement and fee details in writing. Ontario's guidance on [private schools](https://www.ontario.ca/page/private-schools) recommends asking whether the school has written contracts covering fees and refunds, and what its policy is on access to student records.

### Step 2: Visit with your child

A visit turns an abstract change into something concrete. Your child sees the classroom, the cubbies, the washrooms and the teacher's face. Our guide to [what to expect at a school visit](/blog/visit-islamic-school-what-to-expect) explains what happens, and our list of [questions to ask on a school tour](/blog/questions-to-ask-islamic-school-tour) helps you use the time well.

### Step 3: Tell the current school

Inform the teacher and the office of your child's last day, in writing. A short, polite email is enough. If your child receives special education support or has an Individual Education Plan (IEP), ask the school to include relevant documents in the record.

### Step 4: Arrange the OSR transfer

Under the [Ontario Student Record guideline](https://www.ontario.ca/page/ontario-student-record-osr-guideline), the original OSR can only move between schools in Ontario. Before sending it to a private school, the principal must receive a written request from the receiving school, plus a written statement of consent signed by the parent. The new school normally starts this; your job is to sign the consent and confirm with both offices that the file has moved.

### Step 5: Gather the practical paperwork

Most schools ask for proof of age (birth certificate or passport), proof of address, immunization records, custody documents if relevant, and recent report cards. Have digital copies ready so the new school can finish its file quickly.

### Step 6: Sort out the logistics

Cancel or adjust school bus transportation, before- and after-care, and any lunch programs tied to the old school. Plan the new commute and do a practice drive at drop-off time. If you are moving into a fee-paying school, review the cost in full; our guide to [Ottawa Islamic school fees](/blog/ottawa-islamic-school-fees-explained) explains how tuition and extra costs typically work.

### Step 7: Prepare your child for day one

Tell your child about a week ahead (sooner for older children). Keep it simple and positive: "Your new school has a big carpet for stories and you'll learn Arabic songs." Let them help choose the lunch bag, label their things, and say a proper goodbye to friends at the old school.

## Helping your child settle in the first month

The first two to four weeks matter most. A few habits make a real difference:

- **Keep goodbyes short and consistent.** A hug, a dua, and a clear "I'll pick you up after snack time" works better than lingering.
- **Protect sleep and mornings.** Tired children find change harder. Pack the bag the night before.
- **Ask the teacher about a buddy.** Pairing a new child with a friendly classmate helps them find their feet.
- **Talk about specifics, not "How was school?"** Ask who they played with, what they ate, what song they sang.
- **Keep one familiar anchor.** A favourite water bottle or a family photo in the cubby can help a young child feel at home.
- **Stay in touch with the teacher.** Many schools share daily updates; at QAF, parents use ClassDojo for photos, announcements and messages to the teacher.

Expect some regression: extra clinginess, tiredness or tears at bedtime. That is normal and usually fades as the new routine becomes familiar.

## Moving to an Islamic school mid-year in Ottawa

Many Ottawa families decide partway through JK or SK that they want a school where faith is part of the day, not an add-on on weekends. Islamic schools differ a lot in size, schedule, grades offered and approach, so compare a few before deciding. Ask each school specifically about mid-year entry. Some keep a waitlist and fill spots as they open; others admit only in September. Ask how the first week is handled, whether tuition starts from your first month, and whether your child can visit before starting.
