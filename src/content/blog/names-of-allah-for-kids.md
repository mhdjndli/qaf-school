The best way to teach the 99 names of Allah for kids is not to start with 99. Start with about 20 names a child can feel, such as Ar-Rahman (the Most Merciful), Ar-Razzaq (the Provider) and As-Sami' (the All-Hearing), and attach each one to a moment in their day. This guide gives you those 20 names with Arabic, child-friendly meanings and Quran references, plus activities and a simple week-by-week plan.

> **Key takeaways**
> - The Prophet ﷺ said Allah has 99 names and "whoever knows them will go to Paradise" (Sahih al-Bukhari 2736). Knowing them means understanding and living by them, not only reciting a list.
> - Begin with 20 names drawn directly from the Quran, one or two a week.
> - Link each name to an everyday moment: Ar-Razzaq at meals, As-Sami' at dua time, Al-Ghafur after a mistake.
> - Use hands-on activities (name cards, a "name of the week", nature walks, a names jar) rather than drilling.
> - Never use Allah's names to frighten a child. Lead with mercy, love and closeness.

## What are the 99 names of Allah?

The names of Allah (*al-asma' al-husna*, "the most beautiful names") describe who Allah is: merciful, loving, all-hearing, generous. The Quran says:

وَلِلَّهِ ٱلْأَسْمَآءُ ٱلْحُسْنَىٰ فَٱدْعُوهُ بِهَا

"And to Allah belong the best names, so invoke Him by them" ([Surah Al-A'raf 7:180](https://quran.com/7/180)). The last three verses of Surah Al-Hashr ([59:22–24](https://quran.com/59/22-24)) list more than a dozen names in a row and are a lovely passage to read with older children.

The Prophet ﷺ said:

إِنَّ لِلَّهِ تِسْعَةً وَتِسْعِينَ اسْمًا مِائَةً إِلاَّ وَاحِدًا مَنْ أَحْصَاهَا دَخَلَ الْجَنَّةَ

"Allah has ninety-nine names, one hundred minus one, and whoever knows them will go to Paradise" ([Sahih al-Bukhari 2736](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:2736) and 7392; also Sahih Muslim 2677). Scholars explain that *ahsaha* ("knows them") includes memorising the names, understanding them and living by what they mean.

**A note on the popular list.** The familiar list of 99 names on posters comes from a longer narration in [Jami' at-Tirmidhi 3507](https://sunnah.com/tirmidhi:3507), which sunnah.com shows as graded da'if (weak) by Darussalam. Many hadith scholars hold that the list itself was compiled by a narrator, which is why lists differ slightly from book to book. That is no reason to avoid the posters, but it is why every name in this guide comes straight from a Quran verse.

## The 20 names in five groups

Grouping names by theme helps children see the bigger picture: Allah made us, gives to us, loves us, knows us and looks after us.

### Allah who made everything

**Al-Ahad, Al-Khaliq, Al-Musawwir, Al-Malik.** Use these on walks, at the window or in the bath. "Who made the snow? Al-Khaliq. Who shaped every snowflake differently? Al-Musawwir." Al-Ahad belongs with Surah Al-Ikhlas, which most children learn first; our [short surahs for kids](/blog/short-surahs-for-kids) guide covers it.

### Allah who gives

**Ar-Razzaq, Al-Wahhab, Al-Karim.** Mealtimes and gifts are the natural moments. Before eating: "Bismillah. Thank you, Ar-Razzaq." When your child gets a present: "Who really gave you this? Al-Wahhab gave it through Grandma."

### Allah who loves and forgives

**Ar-Rahman, Ar-Rahim, Al-Wadud, Al-Ghafur, At-Tawwab.** These are the most important names for young children. The Prophet ﷺ pointed to a mother hugging her baby and said, "Allah is more merciful to His slaves than this lady to her son" ([Sahih al-Bukhari 5999](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:5999)). After a mistake, skip the lecture and say: "Allah is At-Tawwab. He loves it when we say sorry."

### Allah who hears, sees and knows

**As-Sami', Al-Basir, Al-'Alim, Al-Latif.** Present these as comfort, not surveillance. "Al-Basir sees you when you share and nobody else notices" builds love. "Allah is watching you, so behave" builds fear and resentment. Al-Latif is perfect for ants, seeds and raindrops.

### Allah who takes care of us

**As-Salam, Al-Mujib, Al-Wakil, Al-Hayy.** These help with worries and bedtime. "Allah says, 'I am near. I respond to the call of the one who calls'" (2:186). At night: "Al-Hayy never sleeps, so He looks after us while we do." The Ayat al-Kursi verse (2:255), which says "neither drowsiness overtakes Him nor sleep", makes this name real.

## Activities for teaching the names of Allah

1. **Name of the week.** Write one name on a card on the fridge, use it whenever it fits, and ask at dinner, "Where did we see it today?"
2. **Name cards with pictures.** Draw a picture for each name (a loaf of bread for Ar-Razzaq, an ear for As-Sami'). Never draw Allah; draw what the name helps us notice.
3. **Matching game.** Lay out name cards and meaning cards face down and play memory.
4. **The names jar.** Each time your child uses a name in the right moment, add a pom-pom. Full jar, family treat.
5. **Nature walk hunt.** In an Ottawa park, look for one thing each for Al-Khaliq, Al-Musawwir and Al-Latif: a pine cone, a feather, a beetle.
6. **Dua with a name.** Teach children to start a dua with the matching name: "Ya Razzaq, please give us good food." "Ya Ghafur, forgive me."
7. **Gratitude circle.** Once a week, each family member shares one thing Al-Wahhab gave them.
8. **Story link.** Pair names with prophet stories: At-Tawwab with Adam, Al-Mujib with Yunus, Al-Wakil with Musa's mother. Our [prophet stories for kids](/blog/prophet-stories-for-kids) guide has the stories.
9. **Arabic letter spotting.** Children learning the alphabet can find the letters in each name. This doubles as reading practice; see [teaching the Arabic alphabet to kids](/blog/teach-arabic-alphabet-to-kids).
10. **Listen to a nasheed.** Choose a children's names-of-Allah nasheed without musical instruments if your family prefers, and sing a few lines at a time.

## A simple week-by-week order

Two names a week takes ten weeks. Repeat the cycle and add new names as your child grows.

| Week | Names | Daily moment |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Ar-Rahman, Ar-Rahim | Every Bismillah |
| 2 | Al-Khaliq, Al-Musawwir | Outdoor time, mirror |
| 3 | Ar-Razzaq, Al-Karim | Meals and snacks |
| 4 | As-Sami', Al-Basir | Dua time, bedtime |
| 5 | Al-Ghafur, At-Tawwab | After mistakes |
| 6 | Al-Wadud, Al-Wahhab | Hugs, presents |
| 7 | Al-Ahad, Al-Malik | Reciting Al-Ikhlas |
| 8 | Al-'Alim, Al-Latif | Questions, tiny creatures |
| 9 | Al-Mujib, Al-Wakil | Worries and wishes |
| 10 | As-Salam, Al-Hayy | Greetings, bedtime |

Children aged 2–3 will pick up the sound and a one-word meaning. Four- and five-year-olds can say the name, the meaning and use it in the right moment. From about seven, children can learn the Arabic root and read the verse where it appears. By ten, many enjoy learning all 99 with a nasheed.

## Mistakes to avoid

- **Racing through the list.** Memorising 99 names without meanings misses the point. Depth before breadth.
- **Using the names as threats.** "Allah will be angry" as a behaviour tool teaches children to fear Allah more than love Him. The Prophet ﷺ said, "Allah is kind and He loves kindness" ([Sahih Muslim 2593](https://sunnah.com/muslim:2593)).
- **Forgetting the adab.** Scholars teach that some names, such as Ar-Rahman, belong to Allah alone. Someone called Abdur-Rahman ("servant of the Most Merciful") should be called by his full name, not just "Rahman." Handle printed names respectfully too.
- **Making it a test.** Praise noticing ("You remembered Ar-Razzaq at lunch!") more than reciting.

## Bringing it all together

Teaching the 99 names of Allah to kids is a lifelong project that begins with one name and one moment. Start with Ar-Rahman at every Bismillah, add a new name each week, and use them in real life: at the table, on walks, after mistakes and at bedtime. Your child will learn that Allah is not a distant idea but the One who made them, feeds them, hears them and loves them.
