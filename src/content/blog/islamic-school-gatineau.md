Looking for an Islamic school in Gatineau, or an école islamique for your child in the Outaouais? On the Quebec side, the main options are part-time. The Centre islamique de l'Outaouais in Hull runs weekday and weekend Quran and Arabic programs, and the Mosque of Aylmer runs Al-Ihsane, a weekend Arabic and Quran school. We could not verify a full-time Islamic elementary school in Gatineau as of October 2026. Many Gatineau families therefore look across the river: QAF School's Islamic JK/SK at 900 Dynes Rd in Ottawa is about 10–15 km from Hull, the Plateau and Aylmer.

This guide covers both sides of the river: local weekend programs, the drive, and the Quebec rules worth understanding before you enrol in Ontario.

> **Key takeaways**
> - In Gatineau, the Centre islamique de l'Outaouais (4 rue Lois, Hull) and the Mosque of Aylmer (21 rue Park) both run children's Quran and Arabic programs.
> - QAF in Ottawa is about 17–30 minutes from Hull and 23–35 minutes from Aylmer outside rush hour, depending on the bridge.
> - Quebec uses a September 30 age cutoff; Ontario's JK uses December 31. That changes the year some children can start.
> - School is compulsory in Quebec from Grade 1 (age 6 by September 30), so kindergarten at 4 and 5 is a family choice.
> - If you may want a Quebec English-language school later, check the eligibility rules first.

## Is there a full-time Islamic school in Gatineau?

Not that we could confirm. The CIO lists an "école islamique" among its services but gives no details, so it is worth calling the centre (819-776-1831) to ask what is currently offered. Al-Ihsane describes itself as a weekend school with more than 130 students a year.

That leaves Gatineau families with three realistic paths:

1. **A Quebec school plus weekend Islamic learning** at the CIO or Al-Ihsane. This is the most common and the simplest logistically.
2. **A full-time Islamic school in Ottawa**, accepting a daily bridge crossing. Our comparison of [Islamic schools in Ottawa](/blog/islamic-schools-in-ottawa-guide) lists every option by area and grade.
3. **A mix by age:** a full-day Islamic kindergarten in Ottawa at 4 and 5, then a Quebec school from Grade 1 with weekend Quran and Arabic.

The third path works because kindergarten comes before compulsory schooling in Quebec, as explained below.

## Quebec rules that matter before you enrol in Ontario

Families on the Quebec side face a few rules that Ottawa families do not. This is general information, so check with the relevant ministry or school board for your situation.

### Different age cutoffs

Quebec counts a child's age on **September 30**. According to [Quebec's education system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Education_in_Quebec), kindergarten is for children who are 5 by September 30 and Grade 1 for children who are 6 by September 30. Ontario counts age on **December 31**, so a child starts JK in the year they turn 4.

Example: a child born in November 2023 can start Ontario JK in September 2027. Under Quebec's September 30 rule, the same child would usually wait a year for the matching Quebec program. Our [junior kindergarten guide](/blog/junior-kindergarten-ottawa-guide) explains Ontario's rules in full.

### Compulsory school starts at Grade 1

In Quebec, compulsory elementary school begins in Grade 1. Kindergarten at 4 and 5 is therefore a family choice, which is why a two-year Islamic kindergarten in Ottawa followed by a Quebec Grade 1 is a workable plan.

### Language of instruction

Under the Charter of the French Language, children in Quebec's publicly funded schools generally attend French-language schools unless they meet the eligibility rules for English instruction. Those rules depend largely on the parents' and siblings' schooling. If an English-language school in Quebec might matter to you later, confirm eligibility before you choose a kindergarten. Do not assume that schooling in Ontario changes the outcome.

### Religious symbols in Quebec public schools

Quebec's [secularism law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Act_respecting_the_laicity_of_the_State) (Bill 21, 2019) bars teachers and principals in public schools from wearing religious symbols at work. It was renewed in 2024 using the notwithstanding clause, and a challenge is before the Supreme Court of Canada. Rules can change, so check the current situation. Some Muslim families weigh this when choosing between Quebec and Ontario schools. Ontario has no equivalent law.

### Ontario public schools and non-residents

Ontario's school boards primarily serve Ontario residents. If you live in Quebec and are thinking about an Ottawa public school, ask the board about non-resident admission and fees. Private schools in Ontario, including Islamic schools, set their own admission policies.

## Weekend Islamic learning on the Gatineau side

For school-age children, the Quebec-side masjids are the natural base:

- **Centre islamique de l'Outaouais, Hull:** Ash-Shatibi is a structured Quran memorization program during the week. Al Fajr teaches Arabic on Saturdays, and Al Furqan focuses on Quran review on Sundays. The centre also offers math and French tutoring on Mondays, Wednesdays and Saturdays.
- **Mosque of Aylmer, Aylmer:** the Al-Ihsane Arabic School teaches Quran, Arabic and Islamic values on weekends. The mosque also runs evening classes and a program for children who do not speak Arabic. 2026–27 registration was first come, first served.

If your children's schooling is in French, these programs pair naturally with home and school. For Arabic options on the Ontario side, see our guide to [Arabic classes for kids in Ottawa](/blog/arabic-classes-for-kids-ottawa).
