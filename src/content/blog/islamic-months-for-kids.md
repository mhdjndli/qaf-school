They make up the Hijri calendar, a lunar calendar of 12 months that each begin with the new crescent moon. ## What is the Hijri calendar?

The Hijri calendar is the Islamic lunar calendar. Each month starts when the new crescent moon (the *hilal*) is sighted after sunset, so a month has either 29 or 30 days. Twelve months make a year of 354 or 355 days.

Allah tells us the moon is a clock for people. When people asked the Prophet about the new moons, Allah revealed:

يَسْـَٔلُونَكَ عَنِ ٱلْأَهِلَّةِ ۖ قُلْ هِىَ مَوَٰقِيتُ لِلنَّاسِ وَٱلْحَجِّ

“They ask you about the new moons. Say, ‘They are measurements of time for the people and for Hajj’” (Surah Al-Baqarah 2:189).

The calendar counts years from the Hijrah, the Prophet’s migration from Makkah to Madinah in 622 CE. The companions chose his arrival in Madinah as the starting point, rather than the start of his prophethood or the day of his death ([Sahih al-Bukhari 3934](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:3934)). The dating system was introduced around 17 AH (about 638 CE) under the caliph Umar ibn al-Khattab. That is why years are written with “AH,” from the Latin *Anno Hegirae*, “in the year of the Hijrah.”

Explain it to a young child like this: *“Our Islamic calendar follows the moon. When we see a tiny new moon, a new month begins. The years started when the Prophet moved to Madinah.”*

## The four sacred months

Allah says the number of months is twelve, and four of them are sacred ([Surah At-Tawbah 9:36](https://quran.com/9/36)):

إِنَّ عِدَّةَ ٱلشُّهُورِ عِندَ ٱللَّهِ ٱثْنَا عَشَرَ شَهْرًا فِى كِتَـٰبِ ٱللَّهِ يَوْمَ خَلَقَ ٱلسَّمَـٰوَٰتِ وَٱلْأَرْضَ مِنْهَآ أَرْبَعَةٌ حُرُمٌ

“Indeed, the number of months with Allah is twelve [lunar] months in the register of Allah [from] the day He created the heavens and the earth; of these, four are sacred.”

The Prophet named them: “Three of them are in succession: Dhul-Qa’da, Dhul-Hijja and Al-Muharram, and [the fourth is] Rajab… which stands between Jumada and Sha’ban” ([Sahih al-Bukhari 4662](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:4662)).

A memory trick for kids: **“Three in a row at the end and start of the year, and Rajab on its own in the middle.”**

## Special days in the Islamic months

Keep this list simple and stick to what is well established:

- **Muharram:** The Prophet called it “Allah’s month” and said the best fasting after Ramadan is in Muharram ([Sahih Muslim 1163a](https://sunnah.com/muslim:1163a)). The 10th is **Ashura**. The Prophet fasted it, remembering the day Allah saved Musa and the Children of Israel (Sahih al-Bukhari 2004), and said fasting it expiates the previous year (Sahih Muslim 1162b).
- **Rajab:** One of the four sacred months. Allah says of these months, “do not wrong yourselves during them” (9:36), so it is a good time to talk about avoiding wrongdoing.
- **Sha’ban:** Aisha said she never saw the Prophet fast more in any month, apart from Ramadan, than in Sha’ban (Sahih al-Bukhari 1969). It is a natural “warm-up” month for Ramadan practice.
- **Ramadan:** The month of fasting in which the Quran was revealed (Surah Al-Baqarah 2:185), with Laylatul Qadr in its last ten nights.
- **Shawwal:** Eid al-Fitr on the 1st.
- **Dhul Hijjah:** The first ten days are the best days for good deeds (Sahih al-Bukhari 969), followed by the Day of Arafah on the 9th and Eid al-Adha on the 10th.

## Why Ramadan moves every year

Children often ask why Ramadan was in March a couple of years ago and is in February now. The answer is in the numbers: a solar year has about 365 days and a Hijri year about 354, so Islamic dates arrive roughly 11 days earlier each year.

| Year | Ramadan begins (Umm al-Qura) |
|---|---|
| 2025 | March 1 |
| 2026 | February 18 |
| 2027 | February 8 (expected) |
| 2028 | January 28 (expected) |
| 2029 | January 16 (expected) |
| 2030 | January 5 and December 26 (expected) |

Point out the last row: in 2030, Ramadan is expected to begin twice in the same Gregorian year. Over about 33 years, Ramadan travels through every season. A child who is 5 today will fast Ramadan in summer as a young adult, when Ottawa days are much longer.

## How to teach the Islamic months to kids

1. **Step 1: Learn a chant.** Say the twelve names in four groups of three to a steady clap: *Muharram, Safar, Rabi’ al-Awwal / Rabi’ al-Akhir, Jumada, Jumada / Rajab, Sha’ban, Ramadan / Shawwal, Dhul Qa’dah, Dhul Hijjah.*
2. **Step 2: Start a moon journal.** Each clear evening, your child draws the moon’s shape. After a month they will see the cycle from thin crescent to full moon and back.
3. **Step 3: Go moon-spotting.** On the evening a new month is expected, look west just after sunset. A thin crescent is hard to see, which helps children understand why dates are “expected.”
4. **Step 4: Make a Hijri wall calendar.** Write both the Islamic and Gregorian dates on your family calendar, and let a child update the Islamic date each morning.
5. **Step 5: Find their Hijri birthday.** Use a reliable converter to find the Islamic date of your child’s birth. Children love having a “moon birthday,” and noticing that it moves.
6. **Step 6: Play month cards.** Write each month on a card with a picture (a lantern for Ramadan, a Kaaba for Dhul Hijjah). Shuffle and race to put them in order.
7. **Step 7: Count down to the next big month.** Use a paper chain to count the days to Rajab, Ramadan or Dhul Hijjah.

The month names are also excellent Arabic practice. Pair this lesson with our guide to the [days of the week in Arabic](/blog/days-of-the-week-in-arabic), and if your child wants more, see our overview of [Arabic classes for kids in Ottawa](/blog/arabic-classes-for-kids-ottawa).

> **Classroom tip:** In our classroom, we have found that young children remember the months best when each one is tied to a feeling or an event they know: “Ramadan is when we have iftar with Grandma,” “Dhul Hijjah is when people go to the Kaaba.”
