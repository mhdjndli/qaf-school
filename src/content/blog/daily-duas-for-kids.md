Daily duas for kids are short supplications from the Quran and Sunnah that children say at everyday moments: waking up, eating, leaving the house, sneezing and going to sleep. Below are 25 of them, each with the Arabic, an easy transliteration, the meaning and a verified source, plus practical tips for teaching them to children from toddlerhood up.

> **Key takeaways**
> - Start with the shortest duas (Bismillah, Alhamdulillah, Jazakallahu khayran) from age two or three, and add longer ones from four or five.
> - Tie each dua to a moment that already happens every day, such as meals, the car or bedtime, so it is repeated naturally.
> - Teach one new dua a week: say it, explain it, then let your child "catch" you saying it.
> - Every dua below is referenced to the Quran or an authenticated hadith so you can check it yourself on Quran.com or Sunnah.com.

## Morning duas for kids

### 1. Waking up

الْحَمْدُ لِلَّهِ الَّذِي أَحْيَانَا بَعْدَ مَا أَمَاتَنَا وَإِلَيْهِ النُّشُورُ

*Al-hamdu lillahil-ladhi ahyana ba'da ma amatana wa ilayhin-nushur.*

**Meaning:** All praise is for Allah, who gave us life after causing us to die, and to Him is the return.

**Source:** [Sahih al-Bukhari 6324](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:6324). Explain to children that every morning is a gift: Allah has given them a brand-new day.

### 2. Getting dressed

الْحَمْدُ لِلَّهِ الَّذِي كَسَانِي هَذَا الثَّوْبَ وَرَزَقَنِيهِ مِنْ غَيْرِ حَوْلٍ مِنِّي وَلاَ قُوَّةٍ

*Al-hamdu lillahil-ladhi kasani hadhath-thawba wa razaqanihi min ghayri hawlin minni wa la quwwah.*

**Meaning:** All praise is for Allah, who clothed me with this garment and provided it for me without any power or strength from me.

**Source:** Sunan Abi Dawud 4023 (graded hasan by al-Albani).

### 3. Entering the bathroom

اللَّهُمَّ إِنِّي أَعُوذُ بِكَ مِنَ الْخُبُثِ وَالْخَبَائِثِ

*Allahumma inni a'udhu bika minal-khubuthi wal-khaba'ith.*

**Meaning:** O Allah, I seek refuge in You from evil and from evil things.

**Source:** Sahih al-Bukhari 142. Teach children to say it *before* going in, not inside.

### 4. Leaving the bathroom

غُفْرَانَكَ

*Ghufranak.*

**Meaning:** I ask for Your forgiveness.

**Source:** Sunan Abi Dawud 30 (graded sahih by al-Albani). At one word, this is one of the easiest duas for toddlers who are toilet training.

### 5. After wudu

أَشْهَدُ أَنْ لاَ إِلَهَ إِلاَّ اللَّهُ وَأَنَّ مُحَمَّدًا عَبْدُ اللَّهِ وَرَسُولُهُ

*Ash-hadu an la ilaha illallah, wa anna Muhammadan abdullahi wa rasuluh.*

**Meaning:** I testify that there is no god but Allah, and that Muhammad is the servant of Allah and His Messenger.

**Source:** Sahih Muslim 234a, which says the eight gates of Paradise are opened for whoever says it after a complete wudu. For the full wudu steps, see our guide on [how to teach kids salah](/blog/teach-kids-salah).

## Duas for leaving home and going out

### 6. Leaving home

بِسْمِ اللَّهِ تَوَكَّلْتُ عَلَى اللَّهِ لاَ حَوْلَ وَلاَ قُوَّةَ إِلاَّ بِاللَّهِ

*Bismillah, tawakkaltu 'alallah, la hawla wa la quwwata illa billah.*

**Meaning:** In the name of Allah, I place my trust in Allah; there is no might and no power except with Allah.

**Source:** [Sunan Abi Dawud 5095](https://sunnah.com/abudawud:5095) (graded sahih by al-Albani). The hadith says the one who says it is told, "You are guided, defended and protected." Children love hearing that.

### 7. Riding in the car

سُبْحَانَ الَّذِي سَخَّرَ لَنَا هَذَا وَمَا كُنَّا لَهُ مُقْرِنِينَ وَإِنَّا إِلَى رَبِّنَا لَمُنْقَلِبُونَ

*Subhanal-ladhi sakhkhara lana hadha wa ma kunna lahu muqrinin, wa inna ila Rabbina lamunqalibun.*

**Meaning:** Glory be to the One who has made this subservient to us, and we could not have done it ourselves; and to our Lord we will surely return.

**Source:** Surah Az-Zukhruf 43:13–14, which tells believers to say it when they mount their ride. The Prophet ﷺ said it, after three takbirs, when setting out on a journey (Sahih Muslim 1342). The school run is a perfect daily practice slot.

### 8. Entering and leaving the masjid

Entering: اللَّهُمَّ افْتَحْ لِي أَبْوَابَ رَحْمَتِكَ

*Allahummaftah li abwaba rahmatik.* **Meaning:** O Allah, open for me the doors of Your mercy.

Leaving: اللَّهُمَّ إِنِّي أَسْأَلُكَ مِنْ فَضْلِكَ

*Allahumma inni as'aluka min fadlik.* **Meaning:** O Allah, I ask You for Your bounty.

**Source:** Sahih Muslim 713a. Teach children to walk in calmly and keep their voices low.

### 9. Coming home

بِسْمِ اللَّهِ ... السَّلَامُ عَلَيْكُمْ

*Bismillah... As-salamu alaykum.*

**Meaning:** In the name of Allah... Peace be upon you.

**Source:** The Prophet ﷺ taught that when a person mentions Allah on entering the home, Satan finds "no place to spend the night" (Sahih Muslim 2018a). The Quran adds: فَإِذَا دَخَلْتُم بُيُوتًا فَسَلِّمُوا۟ عَلَىٰٓ أَنفُسِكُمْ, "when you enter houses, give greetings of peace upon each other" (Surah An-Nur 24:61).

## Mealtime duas

### 10. Before eating (and if you forget)

بِسْمِ اللَّهِ

*Bismillah.* **Meaning:** In the name of Allah.

If you forget at the start: بِسْمِ اللَّهِ أَوَّلَهُ وَآخِرَهُ

*Bismillahi awwalahu wa akhirah.* **Meaning:** In the name of Allah, at its beginning and its end.

**Source:** The Prophet ﷺ told a young boy, Umar ibn Abi Salamah, "O boy! Mention the Name of Allah and eat with your right hand, and eat of the dish what is nearer to you" (Sahih al-Bukhari 5376). The "if you forget" dua is in Sunan Abi Dawud 3767 (graded sahih by al-Albani).

### 11. After eating

الْحَمْدُ لِلَّهِ الَّذِي أَطْعَمَنِي هَذَا وَرَزَقَنِيهِ مِنْ غَيْرِ حَوْلٍ مِنِّي وَلاَ قُوَّةٍ

*Al-hamdu lillahil-ladhi at'amani hadha wa razaqanihi min ghayri hawlin minni wa la quwwah.*

**Meaning:** All praise is for Allah, who fed me this and provided it for me without any power or strength from me.

**Source:** Jami' at-Tirmidhi 3458 (graded hasan). It mirrors the getting-dressed dua, so children who know one learn the other quickly.

## Duas for manners and feelings

### 12. Sneezing

The one who sneezes: الْحَمْدُ لِلَّهِ (*Al-hamdu lillah*, All praise is for Allah)

The one who hears: يَرْحَمُكَ اللَّهُ (*Yarhamukallah*, May Allah have mercy on you)

The sneezer replies: يَهْدِيكُمُ اللَّهُ وَيُصْلِحُ بَالَكُمْ (*Yahdikumullahu wa yuslihu balakum*, May Allah guide you and set your affairs right)

**Source:** Sahih al-Bukhari 6224. Practise it as a three-part game with siblings.

### 13. Thanking someone

جَزَاكَ اللَّهُ خَيْرًا

*Jazakallahu khayran.* **Meaning:** May Allah reward you with good.

**Source:** Jami' at-Tirmidhi 2035 (graded hasan), which says whoever says this "has done the most that he can of praise."

### 14. Feeling angry

أَعُوذُ بِاللَّهِ مِنَ الشَّيْطَانِ الرَّجِيمِ

*A'udhu billahi minash-shaytanir-rajim.* **Meaning:** I seek refuge in Allah from Satan, the outcast.

**Source:** The Prophet ﷺ saw a man red-faced with anger and said that if he said these words, "his anger would go away" (Sahih al-Bukhari 6115). Pair it with a calm-down routine: sit down, breathe, have a sip of water.

### 15. Getting hurt

Put your hand on the place that hurts and say بِسْمِ اللَّهِ three times, then seven times:

أَعُوذُ بِاللَّهِ وَقُدْرَتِهِ مِنْ شَرِّ مَا أَجِدُ وَأُحَاذِرُ

*A'udhu billahi wa qudratihi min sharri ma ajidu wa uhadhir.* **Meaning:** I seek refuge in Allah and His power from the evil of what I feel and what I fear.

**Source:** Sahih Muslim 2202. For little ones, start with the hand on the bump and "Bismillah" three times.

### 16. Visiting someone who is sick

لاَ بَأْسَ طَهُورٌ إِنْ شَاءَ اللَّهُ

*La ba'sa, tahurun in sha Allah.* **Meaning:** No harm; it is a purification, if Allah wills.

**Source:** Sahih al-Bukhari 3616. A gentle phrase children can say to a sick grandparent or friend.

### 17. When it rains

صَيِّبًا نَافِعًا

*Sayyiban nafi'a.* **Meaning:** (O Allah, make it) a beneficial rain.

**Source:** Sahih al-Bukhari 1032. In an Ottawa autumn, your child will get plenty of practice.

### 18. Seeing something you like

مَا شَآءَ ٱللَّهُ لَا قُوَّةَ إِلَّا بِٱللَّهِ

*Ma sha Allah, la quwwata illa billah.* **Meaning:** What Allah has willed; there is no power except with Allah.

**Source:** Surah Al-Kahf 18:39. It teaches children to admire a friend's drawing or new shoes and thank Allah instead of feeling jealous.

### 19. Talking about the future

إِنْ شَاءَ اللَّهُ

*In sha Allah.* **Meaning:** If Allah wills.

**Source:** Allah says not to say "I will do that tomorrow" "except [when adding], 'If Allah wills'" (Surah Al-Kahf 18:23–24). Teach children that "in sha Allah" is a real plan placed in Allah's hands, not a polite "maybe."

### 20. Making a mistake

أَسْتَغْفِرُ اللَّهَ

*Astaghfirullah.* **Meaning:** I seek Allah's forgiveness.

**Source:** The Prophet ﷺ said, "I seek forgiveness from Allah a hundred times a day" (Sahih Muslim 2702a). Children learn that saying sorry to Allah is normal, not shameful.

## Duas from the Quran for kids

### 21. For knowledge

رَّبِّ زِدْنِى عِلْمًا

*Rabbi zidni 'ilma.* **Meaning:** My Lord, increase me in knowledge.

**Source:** Surah Ta-Ha [20:114](https://quran.com/20/114). A natural start to homework, reading time or the school day.

### 22. For parents

رَّبِّ ٱرْحَمْهُمَا كَمَا رَبَّيَانِى صَغِيرًا

*Rabbir-hamhuma kama rabbayani saghira.* **Meaning:** My Lord, have mercy on them as they raised me when I was small.

**Source:** Surah Al-Isra [17:24](https://quran.com/17/24).

### 23. For good in this life and the next

رَبَّنَآ ءَاتِنَا فِى ٱلدُّنْيَا حَسَنَةً وَفِى ٱلْـَٔاخِرَةِ حَسَنَةً وَقِنَا عَذَابَ ٱلنَّارِ

*Rabbana atina fid-dunya hasanatan wa fil-akhirati hasanatan wa qina 'adhaban-nar.*

**Meaning:** Our Lord, give us good in this world and good in the Hereafter, and protect us from the punishment of the Fire.

**Source:** Surah Al-Baqarah 2:201. Anas said this was the Prophet's ﷺ most frequent dua (Sahih al-Bukhari 6389).

## Bedtime duas

### 24. Going to sleep

بِاسْمِكَ اللَّهُمَّ أَمُوتُ وَأَحْيَا

*Bismika Allahumma amutu wa ahya.* **Meaning:** In Your name, O Allah, I die and I live.

**Source:** Sahih al-Bukhari 6324. Add the Prophet's ﷺ nightly habit: reciting Al-Ikhlas, Al-Falaq and An-Nas into cupped hands, blowing lightly and wiping over the body, three times (Sahih al-Bukhari 5017). Our guide to [short surahs for kids](/blog/short-surahs-for-kids) covers all three.

### 25. A parent's protection dua for children

أُعِيذُكَ بِكَلِمَاتِ اللَّهِ التَّامَّةِ مِنْ كُلِّ شَيْطَانٍ وَهَامَّةٍ وَمِنْ كُلِّ عَيْنٍ لاَمَّةٍ

*U'idhuka bi kalimatillahit-tammah, min kulli shaytanin wa hammah, wa min kulli 'aynin lammah.*

**Meaning:** I seek protection for you in the perfect words of Allah from every devil and every poisonous creature, and from every harmful eye.

**Source:** The Prophet ﷺ said this over his grandsons Hasan and Husayn (Sahih al-Bukhari 3371; the wording for two children, أُعِيذُكُمَا, is in [Sunan Abi Dawud 4737](https://sunnah.com/abudawud:4737)). For one child, parents say أُعِيذُكَ (to a boy) or أُعِيذُكِ (to a girl).

## Which duas to teach first, by age

Children can learn far more than we expect, but the order matters. Short and frequent comes first.

| Age | Start with | Why |
|---|---|---|
| 2–3 | Bismillah, Alhamdulillah, Ghufranak, Jazakallahu khayran | One or two words, said many times a day |
| 4–5 | Waking up, sleeping, after eating, leaving home, sneezing | Short sentences tied to daily routines |
| 6–7 | Getting dressed, car, masjid, after wudu, Rabbi zidni 'ilma | Longer phrases; children now enjoy "grown-up" duas |
| 8+ | Getting hurt, Rabbana atina, morning and evening adhkar | Ready to understand meaning and use them independently |

A good next step for older children is the morning and evening dua *Bismillahil-ladhi la yadurru ma'asmihi shay'un fil-ardi wa la fis-sama'i wa huwas-Sami'ul-'Alim*, said three times (Sunan Abi Dawud 5088, graded sahih by al-Albani).

## How to teach daily duas to kids

**Anchor each dua to a moment.** A dua that belongs to the front door gets practised every day without effort. Stick a small card at child height by the door, the bathroom and the dining table.

**One new dua a week.** Say it at its moment every time, then say it slowly together, then let your child lead. By the end of the week, "forget" on purpose and let them remind you.

**Explain the meaning in one sentence.** "We say Alhamdulillah when we wake up because Allah gave us a brand-new day" means more to a five-year-old than the Arabic alone.

**Use rhythm.** Young children remember sounds and patterns. Chant short duas in a steady rhythm, clap the syllables, or use a dua song your child likes.

**Model it out loud.** Children copy what they hear. If they hear you say the travel dua every time you start the car, they'll be saying it with you within weeks.

**Praise the remembering, not the reciting.** "You remembered to say Bismillah all by yourself!" builds the habit. Gently fix pronunciation separately.

**Keep it positive.** Duas are a conversation with Allah, not a test. Never threaten or shame a child for forgetting. For more on mercy-first teaching, see our [prophetic parenting tips](/blog/prophetic-parenting-tips).

## Bringing it all together

You don't need to teach all 25 daily duas for kids at once. Start with two or three that fit your child's day, say them every time, explain what they mean, and add one more each week. Within a year, your child will be remembering Allah from the moment they wake up to the moment they fall asleep, and teaching their younger siblings along the way.
