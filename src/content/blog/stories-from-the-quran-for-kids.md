The best Quran stories for kids are the ones Allah Himself tells: Nuh and the Ark, Ibrahim and the idols, baby Musa in the river, Yunus and the whale, Sulayman and the ant, the People of the Cave and Luqman’s advice to his son. Each one is short enough to tell at bedtime, comes with a clear lesson, and can be read together straight from the Quran.

Below are 15 Quran stories for kids, each with where to find it in the Quran (so you can read the ayat yourself), the story in simple words, its lesson, the best age, and a quick activity.

> **Key takeaways**
> - The Quran tells its stories for a purpose: “There was certainly in their stories a lesson for those of understanding” (12:111).
> - Tell each story from the Quran first, and check the ayat yourself. Many popular retellings add details that are not in the Quran or authentic hadith.
> - Match the story to the age: Sulayman and the ant suits a 4-year-old; Musa and al-Khidr suits an 8- or 9-year-old.
> - End every story with one question: “What did this person do that Allah loved?”
> - Reading the actual ayat together, even a few, connects the story to the Quran your child is learning.

## Why Quran stories matter for children

Allah introduces Surah Yusuf with these words:

نَحْنُ نَقُصُّ عَلَيْكَ أَحْسَنَ ٱلْقَصَصِ بِمَآ أَوْحَيْنَآ إِلَيْكَ هَـٰذَا ٱلْقُرْءَانَ وَإِن كُنتَ مِن قَبْلِهِۦ لَمِنَ ٱلْغَـٰفِلِينَ ﴿٣﴾

“We relate to you the best of stories in what We have revealed to you of this Quran” (12:3, Saheeh International). The stories of the Quran are true, they teach us about Allah, and they show children real people facing real choices: being patient, telling the truth, saying sorry, trusting Allah when things are frightening.

Children also remember stories far better than lists of rules. A child who knows that Yunus called on Allah from inside the whale understands “make dua when you are in trouble” in a way no lecture can teach. This list focuses on the stories as the Quran tells them; for fuller life stories of the prophets, see our companion guide to [prophet stories for kids](/blog/prophet-stories-for-kids).

## 15 Quran stories for kids

### 1. Adam and the tree

**Where:** Al-Baqarah 2:30–38; Al-A‘raf 7:11–25.

**The story:** Allah created Adam and told the angels to bow to him. Iblis refused out of pride, saying, “I am better than him” (7:12). Adam and his wife lived in the Garden and could eat from everything except one tree. Shaytan tricked them, and they ate from it. They felt sorry straight away and prayed:

قَالَا رَبَّنَا ظَلَمْنَآ أَنفُسَنَا وَإِن لَّمْ تَغْفِرْ لَنَا وَتَرْحَمْنَا لَنَكُونَنَّ مِنَ ٱلْخَـٰسِرِينَ ﴿٢٣﴾

“Our Lord, we have wronged ourselves, and if You do not forgive us and have mercy upon us, we will surely be among the losers” (7:23). Allah accepted their repentance (2:37).

**Lesson:** Everyone makes mistakes; the best people say sorry quickly. **Try this:** Learn the dua of Adam together as your family’s “sorry dua.”

### 2. Nuh and the Ark

**Where:** Hud 11:25–48 (and Surah Nuh, 71).

**The story:** Prophet Nuh called his people to worship Allah for a very long time, but most refused and mocked him. Allah told him to build a great ship. When the flood came, Nuh carried believers and pairs of animals aboard (11:40), and the Ark came to rest safely (11:44).

**Lesson:** Keep doing what is right, even when others laugh. **Try this:** Build an ark from a cardboard box and “load” toy animals in pairs.

### 3. Ibrahim and the idols

**Where:** Al-Anbiya’ 21:51–70.

**The story:** Ibrahim, “a young man” (21:60), lived among people who worshipped statues. While they were away, he broke the idols into pieces except the biggest one (21:58). When they asked who did it, he said, “Ask them, if they can speak” (21:63), showing that idols can do nothing. They threw him into a fire, and Allah said, “O fire, be coolness and safety upon Ibrahim” (21:69).

**Lesson:** Allah alone deserves worship, and He protects those who stand for the truth. **Try this:** Ask: “Why couldn’t the idols answer?”

### 4. Ibrahim and Ismail build the Ka‘bah

**Where:** Al-Baqarah 2:127–129.

**The story:** Ibrahim and his son Ismail raised the foundations of the House (the Ka‘bah) together, praying, “Our Lord, accept this from us” (2:127). Millions of Muslims still pray towards it today.

**Lesson:** Do good deeds together, and ask Allah to accept them. **Try this:** Build a Ka‘bah from blocks while reciting their dua.

### 5. Yusuf and his brothers

**Where:** The whole of [Surah Yusuf (12)](https://quran.com/12).

**The story:** Young Yusuf dreamed of eleven stars, the sun and the moon bowing to him (12:4). His jealous brothers threw him into a well (12:15). He was sold, wrongly imprisoned, and later put in charge of the storehouses of Egypt (12:55). Years later, his brothers came asking for food, and Yusuf forgave them: “No blame will there be upon you today” (12:92).

**Lesson:** Patience through hard times, and forgiving those who hurt us. **Try this:** Tell it in four bedtime “episodes”: the dream, the well, the prison, the reunion.

### 6. Baby Musa in the river

**Where:** Al-Qasas 28:7–13; Ta-Ha 20:38–40.

**The story:** Pharaoh was harming the baby boys of Bani Isra’il. Allah inspired Musa’s mother to place him in a chest and set it on the river (20:39), promising to return him (28:7). Pharaoh’s family found him. Baby Musa refused every nurse, his sister suggested a family (28:12), and Allah returned Musa to his own mother (28:13).

**Lesson:** Allah’s promises always come true. **Try this:** Float a small “basket” in the bath or sink while you tell the story.

### 7. Musa and the sea

**Where:** Ash-Shu‘ara’ 26:52–67.

**The story:** Musa led the believers out at night, and Pharaoh’s army chased them to the sea. The people panicked, but Musa said, “Indeed, with me is my Lord; He will guide me” (26:62). Allah told him to strike the sea with his staff, and it parted like great mountains (26:63).

**Lesson:** When we are scared, Allah is with us. **Try this:** Teach the phrase “My Lord is with me” for scary moments.

### 8. Musa and the wise servant

**Where:** [Al-Kahf](https://quran.com/18) 18:60–82.

**The story:** Musa travelled to meet a servant of Allah who had been given special knowledge (18:65), known from hadith as al-Khidr ([Sahih al-Bukhari 3401](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:3401)). He made a hole in a boat, did something that seemed terrible, and repaired a wall for people who refused them food. Musa could not stay patient. At the end, the servant explained the hidden wisdom behind each act (18:79–82).

**Lesson:** Allah knows what we cannot see. **Best for:** ages 8 and up, as one part of the story needs careful explanation.

### 9. The cow of Bani Isra’il

**Where:** Al-Baqarah 2:67–73. This story gives Surah Al-Baqarah (“The Cow”) its name.

**The story:** Allah commanded Bani Isra’il to sacrifice a cow (2:67). Instead of simply obeying, they kept asking more and more questions about its age and colour, making it harder for themselves, until “they could hardly do it” (2:71).

**Lesson:** When Allah asks something, obey simply and sincerely. **Try this:** Ask: “What would have happened if they had obeyed the first time?”

### 10. Sulayman, the ant and the hoopoe

**Where:** [An-Naml](https://quran.com/27) 27:15–44.

**The story:** Sulayman’s army came to the valley of the ants, and one ant warned the others to go inside so they would not be crushed (27:18). Sulayman smiled at her words and thanked Allah (27:19). Later, the hoopoe bird brought news of a queen in Sheba whose people worshipped the sun (27:22–24), and the queen came to believe in Allah (27:44).

**Lesson:** Be grateful, and be gentle with Allah’s creatures. **Try this:** Watch an ant trail and talk about how Sulayman heard the ant.

### 11. Yunus and the whale

**Where:** Al-Anbiya’ 21:87–88; As-Saffat 37:139–148.

**The story:** Prophet Yunus left his people in anger. On a ship at sea, he was thrown overboard and swallowed by a great fish (37:142). In the darkness, he called out:

وَذَا ٱلنُّونِ إِذ ذَّهَبَ مُغَـٰضِبًا فَظَنَّ أَن لَّن نَّقْدِرَ عَلَيْهِ فَنَادَىٰ فِى ٱلظُّلُمَـٰتِ أَن لَّآ إِلَـٰهَ إِلَّآ أَنتَ سُبْحَـٰنَكَ إِنِّى كُنتُ مِنَ ٱلظَّـٰلِمِينَ ﴿٨٧﴾

His dua, “There is no deity except You; exalted are You; indeed, I have been of the wrongdoers” (21:87), was answered, and Allah saved him (21:88). His people later believed (37:148).

**Lesson:** No place is too dark for dua. **Try this:** Learn the dua of Yunus as a family.

### 12. Maryam and baby ‘Isa

**Where:** Maryam 19:16–34; Al ‘Imran 3:42–47.

**The story:** An angel came to Maryam with news that she would have a pure son. Alone and in pain, she was told to shake the palm tree, which dropped fresh dates for her (19:25). When people questioned her, she pointed to the baby, and baby ‘Isa spoke from the cradle: “Indeed, I am the servant of Allah” (19:30).

**Lesson:** Allah takes care of those who trust Him. **Try this:** Share dates at iftar or snack time and remember Maryam.

### 13. The People of the Cave

**Where:** Al-Kahf 18:9–26.

**The story:** Some young believers left their people, who worshipped others besides Allah (18:15). They hid in a cave, prayed for mercy (18:10), and Allah made them sleep with their dog at the entrance (18:18). They slept for 309 years (18:25) and woke up thinking it had been a day (18:19). The Quran tells us not to argue about how many they were: Allah knows best (18:22).

**Lesson:** Hold on to your faith with good friends. **Try this:** Ask: “Who are good friends who help you remember Allah?”

### 14. Luqman’s advice to his son

**Where:** [Surah Luqman](https://quran.com/31) 31:12–19.

**The story:** Luqman, a wise man, gave his son advice the Quran preserves: do not associate anything with Allah (31:13); be good to your parents (31:14); even a deed the size of a mustard seed is known to Allah (31:16); pray, and be patient (31:17); do not be proud (31:18); and lower your voice (31:19).

**Lesson:** A full lesson in manners from the Quran. **Try this:** One piece of advice per day for a week. Our guide to [Islamic manners for kids](/blog/islamic-manners-for-kids) builds on it.

### 15. The owners of the garden

**Where:** Al-Qalam 68:17–33.

**The story:** Some brothers owned a beautiful garden. They planned to harvest it early in the morning so that no poor person could ask for a share (68:24). While they slept, the garden was destroyed (68:19). They realized their mistake and said, “Exalted is our Lord! Indeed, we were wrongdoers” (68:29).

**Lesson:** What we have is from Allah, and sharing brings blessing. **Try this:** Pair it with a family charity project.

## More Quran stories to explore

Once your child knows these, continue with: Prophet Salih and the she-camel (7:73–79; 91:11–15), young Dawud and Jalut (2:246–251), the patience of Ayyub (21:83–84; 38:41–44), Dhul-Qarnayn’s journeys (Al-Kahf 18:83–98), the companions of the elephant (Surah Al-Fil, 105) and the two sons of Adam (5:27–31, best for older children).

## How to tell Quran stories to kids

1. **Read the ayat yourself first.** Use a trusted translation such as those on [Quran.com](https://quran.com). Many books, cartoons and videos add invented details (names, conversations, “facts”) that are not in the Quran or authentic hadith.
2. **Say “Allah tells us in the Quran…”** so your child knows the source.
3. **Read one or two ayat in Arabic**, especially a dua from the story. It links story time to Quran time.
4. **Keep it age-appropriate.** Leave frightening details for older children.
5. **Ask one question at the end.** “What did Yunus do when he was scared?” builds reflection.
6. **Retell, don’t just tell.** Let your child narrate the story back, or act it out with toys.
