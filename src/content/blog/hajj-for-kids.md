Hajj for kids, in one sentence: Hajj is the special journey to Makkah that every Muslim who is able must make once in their life, where pilgrims walk around the Kaaba, stand in prayer at Arafah and remember Prophet Ibrahim and his family. It happens every year in Dhul Hijjah, the last month of the Islamic calendar, and in 2027 it is expected around May 14–19. This guide explains the steps of Hajj simply, with a day-by-day table, the story behind it, key words and activities you can do at home.

> **Key takeaways**
> - Hajj is the fifth pillar of Islam. It is required once in a lifetime for adult Muslims who are physically and financially able (Surah Al-Imran 3:97).
> - The main days are 8 to 13 Dhul Hijjah: Mina, Arafah, Muzdalifah, the Jamarat, the sacrifice and tawaf. Eid al-Adha falls on the 10th.
> - Many rites remember Prophet Ibrahim, Hajar and Ismail, especially the Kaaba, the walk between Safa and Marwa, and Zamzam water.
> - Children can go on Hajj, and the Prophet said a child’s Hajj counts and the parent is rewarded (Sahih Muslim 1336a).
> - Hajj 2027 is expected around May 14–19, depending on moon sighting.

## What is Hajj? A simple explanation for kids

Hajj is a pilgrimage, which means a journey made for Allah to a holy place. Muslims travel from every country to Makkah, in Saudi Arabia, to worship Allah at the Kaaba and nearby places, exactly as the Prophet Muhammad (peace be upon him) showed.

Hajj is one of the five pillars of Islam. The Prophet said Islam is built on five things: the shahadah, prayer, zakat, Hajj and fasting in Ramadan (Sahih al-Bukhari 8). Allah says in the Quran:

فِيهِ ءَايَـٰتٌۢ بَيِّنَـٰتٌ مَّقَامُ إِبْرَٰهِيمَ ۖ وَمَن دَخَلَهُۥ كَانَ ءَامِنًا ۗ وَلِلَّهِ عَلَى ٱلنَّاسِ حِجُّ ٱلْبَيْتِ مَنِ ٱسْتَطَاعَ إِلَيْهِ سَبِيلًا ۚ وَمَن كَفَرَ فَإِنَّ ٱللَّهَ غَنِىٌّ عَنِ ٱلْعَـٰلَمِينَ

“And [due] to Allah from the people is a pilgrimage to the House, for whoever is able to find thereto a way” ([Surah Al-Imran 3:97](https://quran.com/3/97)). That is why a Muslim who is not healthy enough, or cannot afford the journey, does not have to go.

Hajj is huge. Saudi Arabia’s General Authority for Statistics counted [1,707,301 pilgrims at Hajj 2026](https://stats.gov.sa/en/w/news/194). That is about 1.7 million people worshipping in the same few places at the same time.

## The steps of Hajj for kids

Use these steps as a bedtime story over a week, one or two steps a night.

1. **Step 1: Ihram.** Before reaching Makkah, pilgrims get ready with a bath and make the intention for Hajj. Men wear two plain white cloths; women wear simple, modest clothes. Everyone looks alike, rich or poor. Then they begin saying the talbiyah (below).
2. **Step 2: Tawaf.** Pilgrims walk around the Kaaba seven times, with the Kaaba on their left. The Kaaba is the House of Allah that Muslims face in every prayer.
3. **Step 3: Sa’i.** Pilgrims walk seven times between two small hills, Safa and Marwa, remembering Hajar’s search for water for baby Ismail (Surah Al-Baqarah 2:158).
4. **Step 4: Mina (8th).** Pilgrims go to Mina, a valley filled with tents, to pray and prepare.
5. **Step 5: Arafah (9th).** After sunrise they travel to the plain of Arafah and spend the afternoon standing, praying and making dua until sunset. This is the heart of Hajj. Muslims who are not on Hajj often fast this day.
6. **Step 6: Muzdalifah (night of the 10th).** After sunset, pilgrims go to Muzdalifah, pray Maghrib and Isha, rest under the open sky and collect small pebbles.
7. **Step 7: The Jamarat (10th).** Back in Mina, pilgrims throw seven pebbles at the largest pillar, saying “Allahu akbar” with each one, as the Prophet did (Sahih al-Bukhari 1751). Many teachers explain it to children as rejecting Shaytan and bad deeds.
8. **Step 8: The sacrifice.** An animal is sacrificed and the meat is shared. Muslims all over the world celebrate Eid al-Adha on this day; see our guide to [Eid al-Adha activities for kids](/blog/eid-al-adha-for-kids).
9. **Step 9: Cutting the hair.** Men shave or shorten their hair and women trim a small amount (Sahih al-Bukhari 1729 describes the Prophet and companions doing this).
10. **Step 10: Tawaf al-Ifadah.** Pilgrims return to the Kaaba for the main tawaf of Hajj, and do sa’i if they have not done it already.
11. **Step 11: Days in Mina (11th–13th).** Pilgrims throw pebbles at all three pillars each day, then make a farewell tawaf before going home.

Different types of Hajj order some of these steps differently, and adults follow detailed rules. For children, these eleven steps are plenty.

### The talbiyah

The Prophet’s talbiyah, which pilgrims repeat from the start of ihram ([Sahih al-Bukhari 1549](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:1549)):

لَبَّيْكَ اللَّهُمَّ لَبَّيْكَ، لَبَّيْكَ لاَ شَرِيكَ لَكَ لَبَّيْكَ، إِنَّ الْحَمْدَ وَالنِّعْمَةَ لَكَ وَالْمُلْكَ، لاَ شَرِيكَ لَكَ

*Labbayk Allāhumma labbayk, labbayka lā sharīka laka labbayk, innal-ḥamda wan-ni‘mata laka wal-mulk, lā sharīka lak*: “Here I am, O Allah, here I am. Here I am, You have no partner, here I am. All praise and blessings are Yours, and all sovereignty. You have no partner.”

Children pick it up quickly if you chant it together in the car in the days before Eid.

## The story behind Hajj: Ibrahim, Hajar and Ismail

Many parts of Hajj go back thousands of years to Prophet Ibrahim (peace be upon him) and his family. Ibn Abbas narrated the story in detail ([Sahih al-Bukhari 3364](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:3364)):

- **An empty valley.** By Allah’s command, Ibrahim left his wife Hajar and baby Ismail in the empty valley of Makkah with some dates and water. Hajar asked, “Has Allah ordered you to do so?” He said yes. She replied that then Allah would not neglect them.
- **The search for water.** When the water ran out, Hajar ran between the hills of Safa and Marwa seven times looking for help. The Prophet said this is the origin of the sa’i.
- **Zamzam.** An angel appeared and dug the ground with his heel (or wing), and water flowed out. That spring is Zamzam, which pilgrims still drink today.
- **Building the Kaaba.** Years later, Ibrahim and Ismail built the Kaaba together, praying:

وَإِذْ يَرْفَعُ إِبْرَٰهِـۧمُ ٱلْقَوَاعِدَ مِنَ ٱلْبَيْتِ وَإِسْمَـٰعِيلُ رَبَّنَا تَقَبَّلْ مِنَّآ ۖ إِنَّكَ أَنتَ ٱلسَّمِيعُ ٱلْعَلِيمُ

“Our Lord, accept [this] from us. Indeed, You are the Hearing, the Knowing” (Surah Al-Baqarah 2:127).

Allah then commanded Ibrahim to call people to Hajj, promising they would come “on foot and on every lean camel… from every distant pass” (Surah Al-Hajj 22:27). Children are often amazed that the call is still being answered by people flying in from Canada.

## Hajj words every child should know

| Word | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Hajj | The pilgrimage to Makkah in Dhul Hijjah |
| Hajji / Hajjah | A man / woman who has made Hajj |
| Umrah | A shorter pilgrimage that can be done any time of year |
| Kaaba | The cube-shaped House of Allah in Makkah |
| Ihram | The state of worship pilgrims enter, and the simple clothes they wear |
| Talbiyah | The chant “Labbayk Allahumma labbayk” |
| Tawaf | Walking seven times around the Kaaba |
| Sa’i | Walking seven times between Safa and Marwa |
| Zamzam | The blessed water spring in Makkah |
| Arafah | The plain where pilgrims stand in dua on the 9th |
| Muzdalifah | Where pilgrims rest and collect pebbles |
| Jamarat | The three stone pillars in Mina |

## Hajj vs Umrah

| | Hajj | Umrah |
|---|---|---|
| When | Only 8–13 Dhul Hijjah | Any time of year |
| Required? | Once in a lifetime, if able | Highly recommended (scholars differ on whether required) |
| Main rites | Ihram, tawaf, sa’i, Mina, Arafah, Muzdalifah, Jamarat, sacrifice, hair | Ihram, tawaf, sa’i, hair |
| Length | Several days | A few hours |

## Can children go on Hajj?

Yes. Ibn Abbas reported that a woman lifted up a young boy to the Prophet and asked whether he had Hajj. The Prophet replied, “Yes, and you will have a reward” ([Sahih Muslim 1336a](https://sunnah.com/muslim:1336a)). Mainstream scholars explain that a child’s Hajj is valid and rewarded, but the obligatory Hajj is still due after puberty for those who are able.

Hajj is physically demanding, with heat, crowds and long walks. Saudi authorities set the rules for pilgrims each year, so families planning to take a child should check current requirements before booking. Some families choose Umrah with children first, and save Hajj for when they are older.

## Hajj activities for kids at home

1. **Mini-Hajj role play:** a box Kaaba in the living room, two cushions for Safa and Marwa, and a “tent” for Mina. Walk through the steps in order. Ages 4–10.
2. **Talbiyah practice:** chant it together while getting ready in the morning during the first ten days of Dhul Hijjah. Ages 3+.
3. **Map the journey:** draw Makkah, Mina, Muzdalifah and Arafah on a large sheet and move a paper pilgrim each day of Hajj. Ages 6–12.
4. **Pebble maths:** 7 pebbles on the 10th, then 21 a day (7 at each of three pillars) for two or three days. How many in total? (49 or 70.) Ages 7–12.
5. **Arafah dua list:** on the 9th, children write duas for family members, for people on Hajj and for the whole ummah. Ages 5+.
6. **Write to a pilgrim:** if someone you know is going, children write a card asking them to make dua for the family. Ages 5+.
