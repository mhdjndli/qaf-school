Kids soccer in Ottawa is easy to find: dozens of community clubs run recreational leagues from about age 3, with outdoor games from late spring to early fall and indoor futsal or dome programs through the winter. This guide covers the main clubs by area, how age groups and seasons work, what it costs, a Sunday league run by Ottawa's Muslim community, and practical tips on modest sportswear and prayer times.

> **Key takeaways**
> - Most Ottawa clubs belong to Eastern Ontario Soccer, which publishes a list of every member club so you can find one near you.
> - Age groups use "U" (under) categories based on birth year, from toddler programs around U3 up to U18.
> - Recreational soccer is the best starting point for most kids; competitive teams usually start around U8 to U12 with evaluations.
> - The Ottawa Islamic Soccer League runs Sunday games from May to September for ages 4 and up.
> - Under the Laws of the Game, head covers, long-sleeve undershirts and tights are permitted when they match team colours.

## How youth soccer works in Ottawa

Youth soccer in Ottawa is organized by community clubs that belong to Eastern Ontario Soccer (EOS), the district association, which sits under Ontario Soccer and Canada Soccer. Each club runs its own programs, sets its own fees and books its own fields.

### Age groups

Players are grouped by **birth year** into "U" categories, where U9 means "under 9" for that season. Because clubs group by calendar year, a child born in January and one born in December of the same year play together.

### Seasons

- **Outdoor season:** roughly late spring to early fall, depending on weather and field openings.
- **Indoor season:** fall and winter programs in domes, gyms or futsal courts. Fall and winter registration often opens in late summer or early fall.
- **Camps:** March break and summer camps are common at larger clubs.

### Recreational vs competitive

| | Recreational | Competitive |
|---|---|---|
| Who it suits | Beginners and kids playing for fun and fitness | Committed players who want to train more |
| Selection | Open registration | Evaluations or tryouts |
| Time commitment | Usually one game or session a week | Two to four sessions a week plus games and travel |
| Typical start age | From toddler programs | Often U8 to U12 depending on club |
| Cost | Lower | Higher (training, uniforms, tournaments) |

For most children under 10, recreational soccer gives the best balance of fun, skill and family time.

**What it is:** A Nepean club offering youth recreational and competitive programs, specialty programs and camps, with its office at 2-210 Colonnade Rd.
**Best for:** Families in central Nepean, Carlington, Merivale and the Prince of Wales corridor.
**Our take:** Its recreational program uses an age-appropriate weekly curriculum for coaches, which is what you want for beginners. It also lists financial assistance on its site. The office is close to QAF's Dynes Rd location, though fields vary, so ask where your child's age group plays.

### 2. Ottawa South United (Manotick and south Ottawa)

**What it is:** A large club based at 5650 Mitch Owens Rd in Manotick, with a community pathway (First Kicks for U3–U5, Community Soccer for U6–U8, and a Recreational League for U9–U18) plus a full competitive academy.
**Best for:** Families in Riverside South, Findlay Creek, Manotick, Barrhaven and the south end.
**Our take:** Good for families who want a clear route from first kicks to competitive soccer within one club ([OSU](https://www.osu.ca/)).

### 3. West Ottawa Soccer Club (Kanata, Stittsville, west end)

**What it is:** A west-end club at 1927 Richardson Side Rd in Kanata, with Lil Legends (U2–U4), WeeWarriors (U4–U7), Recreation (U8–U18), a Development Centre (U8–U11), competitive and academy programs, and an inclusive Warriors AllPlay program.
**Best for:** Kanata, Stittsville and western suburbs.
**Our take:** The AllPlay program is worth asking about if your child has a disability or needs extra support.

### 4. Atlético Ottawa Juniors (Orléans and east end)

**What it is:** The youth club formerly known as Ottawa TFC, now aligned with the city's professional club, based at 2075 Trim Rd with facilities at Millennium Park and the Orléans Dome.
**Programs:** Grassroots (U2–U9), drop-in sessions for young children, winter skills centre and winter 4v4, open programs for U10–U18, plus academy and competitive teams ([Atlético Ottawa Juniors](https://atleticoottawajuniors.com/)).

### 5. Other community clubs

Ottawa City Soccer Club (Greenbank Rd), Gloucester Dragons and Ottawa Gloucester Hornets (Gloucester and the east end), Ottawa Internationals (east end) and Ottawa St. Anthony Soccer Club (St. Anthony St) are among the other EOS member clubs in the city. ## A Sunday league from Ottawa's Muslim community

### 6. Ottawa Islamic Soccer League (OISL)

**What it is:** A community league founded in 2024 for boys and girls of all backgrounds.
**Regular season:** Sundays from May to September, ages U4 to U18, with each team playing a one-hour game between 11:30 AM and 3:30 PM at Lytle Park. No games on holiday weekends.
**Extras:** A five-session pre-season camp (U7–U18) in April and May, weekday evening practices (U7–U18) in summer, and a 2026–27 indoor futsal recreational program for fall and winter.
**Our take:** A welcoming option for families who want their children playing alongside Muslim peers. The league also posts a financial assistance application. Details are on the [OISL website](https://ottawaisl.ca/).

## Modest sportswear tips for Muslim kids

Modest dress and soccer fit together well, and the rules support it. The [Laws of the Game (Law 4, Players' Equipment)](https://www.theifab.com/laws/latest/the-players-equipment/) published by IFAB, the body that sets soccer's rules worldwide, allow:

- **Head covers**, as long as they are black or the main colour of the shirt, match teammates who wear one, are not attached to the shirt, have no protruding parts and are not dangerous (for example, no fastening around the neck).
- **Long-sleeve undershirts** that match the main colour of the shirt sleeve.
- **Tights or leggings** under shorts in the main colour of the shorts.

Recreational leagues are often flexible, but ask your club before the season so there are no surprises on game day.

**Practical tips:**

1. **Choose a pull-on sports hijab** made of breathable, stretchy fabric with no pins. Pins are a safety risk in contact sports.
2. **Match colours early.** Once you know your team's jersey colour, buy undershirts, leggings or a head cover to match.
3. **Layer for Ottawa weather.** Spring and fall games can be cold; a thin thermal undershirt adds warmth and coverage.
4. **Shin guards are mandatory**, and long socks cover them; this also helps with coverage.
5. **Pack a water bottle.** If your child is fasting in Ramadan, talk to the coach about lighter participation on fasting days.

## Fitting soccer around salah and Sundays

**Check game times against prayer times.** Summer games often run through Dhuhr and Asr. Bring a travel prayer mat and find a quiet spot near the field; many families pray together after the game.

**Watch Sunday conflicts.** Weekend Islamic school, Quran classes and soccer often compete for the same Sunday hours. If your child attends a Sunday program, a weekday-evening or Saturday league may fit better, or choose a program that already includes sport.

**Keep it fun.** Canada's [24-Hour Movement Guidelines](https://csepguidelines.ca/guidelines/children-youth/) recommend that children and youth aged 5 to 17 get at least 60 minutes of moderate to vigorous physical activity a day. Soccer is a great way to get there, but it should add to family life, not crowd out rest.

## Costs and financial help

Recreational soccer is usually the most affordable entry point, while competitive programs cost more because of extra training, uniforms and tournaments. Each club sets its own fees, so compare what is included: jersey, number of weeks, indoor vs outdoor, and whether a parent volunteer commitment is expected.

Several clubs, including Nepean Hotspurs, Atlético Ottawa Juniors and OISL, list financial assistance options on their websites. Ask about sibling discounts too.
