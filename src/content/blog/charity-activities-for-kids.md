The best sadaqah ideas for kids are small, regular and hands-on: a coin jar for the masjid, a smile, packing a bag for a food drive, donating an outgrown snowsuit or watering a neighbour's plants. In Islam, sadaqah (voluntary charity) isn't only money. The Prophet ﷺ said that every good deed is charity. Below are 30 sadaqah ideas sorted by type and age, plus Ottawa charities that welcome children's help.

> **Key takeaways**
> - Sadaqah is any voluntary good deed or gift done for Allah's sake. Zakat is the obligatory annual charity for those who meet its conditions.
> - Small and consistent beats big and rare: the deed most beloved to Allah is the most regular one, even if it is little (Sahih al-Bukhari 6464).
> - Children learn giving best when they choose the cause, handle the item or coin themselves, and see the result.
> - Ottawa has kid-friendly ways to give, from donating used snowsuits to the Snowsuit Fund to running a lemonade stand for CHEO's Kids Helping Kids.

## What is sadaqah? Explaining it to kids

**Sadaqah** is a voluntary act of giving or kindness done to please Allah. It can be money, food, time, effort or even a kind word. For a child, try: *"Sadaqah is when you share something good with someone else, just because Allah loves it."*

The Quran describes how Allah multiplies charity, like a single seed that grows seven ears of grain, each with a hundred seeds:

مَّثَلُ ٱلَّذِينَ يُنفِقُونَ أَمْوَٰلَهُمْ فِى سَبِيلِ ٱللَّهِ كَمَثَلِ حَبَّةٍ أَنۢبَتَتْ سَبْعَ سَنَابِلَ فِى كُلِّ سُنۢبُلَةٍ مِّا۟ئَةُ حَبَّةٍ ۗ وَٱللَّهُ يُضَـٰعِفُ لِمَن يَشَآءُ ۗ وَٱللَّهُ وَٰسِعٌ عَلِيمٌ

([Surah Al-Baqarah 2:261](https://quran.com/2/261)). Planting a seed and counting the grains is a perfect way to explain this verse to little ones.

The Sunnah makes it clear that children of any age can give:

- "Doing all that is good is charity" (Sahih al-Bukhari 6021).
- "Your smiling in the face of your brother is charity" (Jami' at-Tirmidhi 1956, graded sahih by al-Albani).
- "Protect yourself from the Fire, even with half a date" (Sahih al-Bukhari 1417). No gift is too small.
- "Charity does not decrease wealth" (Sahih Muslim 2588).

### Sadaqah, zakat and sadaqah jariyah

| Term | What it means | Kid-friendly example |
|---|---|---|
| Sadaqah | Voluntary charity of any size, any time | Sharing snacks, a coin in the masjid box |
| Zakat | Obligatory annual charity on wealth above a set threshold, for those who meet the conditions | Ask your local imam about your family's situation |
| Sadaqah jariyah | Ongoing charity whose benefit continues, such as a well, a mushaf or useful knowledge | Donating a Quran to the masjid, planting a tree |

Sadaqah jariyah is explained in the hadith: when a person dies, their deeds end except three, namely ongoing charity, beneficial knowledge, and a righteous child who prays for them (Sahih Muslim 1631).

## Sadaqah with money

### 1. The sadaqah jar

**What it is:** A clear jar on the kitchen counter where children drop coins from their allowance or Eid money.
**Ages:** 3+.
**How:** Let your child choose where the full jar goes, such as the masjid, a children's hospital or an animal shelter. Seeing coins pile up teaches that small amounts add up.

### 2. The masjid donation box

Give your child a coin to put in the box every time you go to Jumu'ah or the masjid. It's a tiny ritual that builds a lifelong habit.

### 3. Three jars: spend, save, share

Split allowance into three jars. Even 10% in the "share" jar teaches a child that giving is part of every budget.

### 4. Birthday or Eid money donation

Invite your child to give a portion of birthday or Eid money to a cause. CHEO's Kids Helping Kids campaign lists donating birthday money as one way children can support the hospital.

### 5. Lemonade stand or bake sale

Through the [CHEO Foundation's Kids Helping Kids](https://www.cheofoundation.com/kids-helping-kids/) campaign, children in the Ottawa-Gatineau region run their own fundraisers, like lemonade stands, bake sales, chore-a-thons and read-a-thons, for patient care and research at Ottawa's children's hospital. Make sure baked goods are halal and clearly labelled for allergens.

### 6. A read-a-thon or Quran-a-thon

Family members pledge a small amount for every page read or every surah revised, and the total goes to charity. It's double reward: learning and giving.

### 7. "Feed a fasting person" in Ramadan

Let older children contribute to an iftar at the masjid or a charity's Ramadan food program. Our [Ramadan activities for kids](/blog/ramadan-activities-for-kids) has more ideas for the month.

### 8. Choose a Muslim charity together

Look at the work of a registered Canadian Muslim charity together. Islamic Relief Canada is a registered charity (No. 821896875RR0001) that works internationally, while the National Zakat Foundation Canada focuses on supporting Muslims in hardship within Canada. Older children can compare where the money goes.

### 9. A sadaqah jariyah project

For tweens and teens: raise money to donate a mushaf, a book shelf or a water fountain to your masjid or school. Explain why this kind of charity keeps earning reward.

## Sadaqah with things

### 10. Outgrown winter clothes

The [Snowsuit Fund](https://snowsuitfund.com/) is an Ottawa charity that provides winter clothing to children 15 and under. It accepts donations of used snowsuits as well as money. Let your child carry their outgrown snowsuit in and hand it over themselves. It's a very Ottawa form of sadaqah.

### 11. The "give one" toy rule

Before a birthday or Eid, choose one good-condition toy to give away for every new one coming in.

### 12. Food drive bag

Children pick non-perishable items at the grocery store for a food drive at school or the masjid. Schools and masjids often run drives in Ramadan and the winter months. The [Ottawa Food Bank](https://www.ottawafoodbank.ca/) supports a network of local programs, so check its site for current needs.

### 13. Care kits for people experiencing homelessness

Pack socks, gloves, hygiene items and a granola bar into zip bags. [The Ottawa Mission](https://ottawamission.com/) and [Shepherds of Good Hope](https://www.sghottawa.com/) both accept donated items. Check each organisation's current wish list first, as needs change by season.

### 14. Books for other children

Donate gently used children's books to a classroom, a masjid library or a little free library in your neighbourhood.

### 15. Supplies for animals

Islam teaches mercy to animals. The [Ottawa Humane Society](https://www.ottawahumane.ca/) accepts donated supplies and runs child and youth programs. Children can collect items from its wish list.

## Sadaqah with time and effort

### 16. Smile and greet

Teach your child that a smile is sadaqah (Tirmidhi 1956). Make a game of greeting family with "As-salamu alaykum" and a smile every morning.

### 17. Help at home without being asked

Setting the table, folding laundry for a parent or helping a younger sibling with shoes all count. The "every joint" hadith lists helping someone load their belongings as charity (Sahih al-Bukhari 2989).

### 18. Clearing the path

Removing something harmful from the road is charity, according to the same hadith. Pick up litter on a walk, or shovel the front path for an elderly neighbour after a snowfall.

### 19. Water a neighbour's plants or feed the birds

Small acts of care for Allah's creation count. In winter, fill a bird feeder together and talk about how Allah provides.

### 20. Kind words

A kind word is charity. Have children write encouraging notes and hide them in a sibling's lunch bag or a parent's coat pocket.

### 21. Cards for patients or seniors

Make cheerful cards for a children's hospital or a seniors' residence. Contact the facility first to ask what they accept.

### 22. Volunteering as a family

Many charities have minimum ages for volunteers, so families with younger children often help by collecting items or fundraising instead. Teens can look for youth volunteer roles at the masjid, the library or local charities. Ontario high school students also need at least 40 hours of [community involvement](https://www.ontario.ca/page/high-school-graduation-requirements) to graduate, and they can start collecting hours the summer before Grade 9.

### 23. Help at the masjid

Children can stack chairs after an event, hand out dates at iftar or help tidy the shoe area. The [Ottawa Mosque](https://www.ottawamosque.ca/), run by the Ottawa Muslim Association at 251 Northwestern Avenue, is one of many local masjids that hold community events through the year.

## Sadaqah with knowledge and worship

### 24. Teach someone a surah

Teaching is beneficial knowledge, which is one of the three deeds that continue after death. An older sibling can teach a younger one a short surah. Our guide to [short surahs for kids](/blog/short-surahs-for-kids) is a good starting point.

### 25. Share what you learned

After a Quran or Islamic Studies class, children can teach their family one new thing at dinner.

### 26. Make dua for others

Teach children to make dua for sick relatives, friends and people around the world. It's a gift anyone can give.

### 27. Dhikr as a gift

The "every joint" hadith also counts walking to prayer as charity. Make dhikr part of your daily walk or drive.

## Sadaqah as a habit

### 28. A family sadaqah calendar

Choose one sadaqah each day for a month, like a Ramadan calendar or a "30 days of giving" chart. Use the ideas above.

### 29. Give from what you love

Allah says, "You will never attain righteousness until you spend from what you love" ([Surah Al-Imran 3:92](https://quran.com/3/92)). For older kids, try giving away something they actually like, not just what they've outgrown, and talk about how it felt.

### 30. Give secretly

Allah says that giving openly is good, but giving secretly to those in need is better ([Surah Al-Baqarah 2:271](https://quran.com/2/271)). Teach children to give quietly, without showing off. Leave an anonymous treat for a neighbour, or drop coins in the donation box when no one is looking.

## Tips for raising generous kids

- **Let them choose.** A child who picks the cause cares more about it.
- **Make it physical.** Young children understand handing over a snowsuit better than an online transfer.
- **Talk about intention.** Before giving, say together: "We're doing this for Allah."
- **Celebrate without bragging.** Praise effort privately: "I saw how you shared your treat. Allah saw it too."
- **Connect to gratitude.** Giving flows from noticing what we have. Pair these ideas with our [Islamic gratitude activities for kids](/blog/gratitude-activities-islamic).
- **Model it.** Children copy what parents do far more than what they say.
