Prophetic parenting means raising children the way the Prophet Muhammad ﷺ treated the children around him: with open affection, play, patience, fairness and attention. The hadith collections are full of small, vivid moments, from a grandson riding on his back in sujud to a question about a little boy's pet bird. Below are 15 prophetic parenting tips, each grounded in a verified hadith with its reference, and each translated into what it looks like with a toddler or a kindergartener today.

> **Key takeaways**
> - The Prophet ﷺ kissed, hugged, carried and played with children openly, and said, "Whoever is not merciful to others will not be treated mercifully" (Sahih al-Bukhari 5997).
> - Gentleness was his default: "Kindness is not to be found in anything but that it adds to its beauty" (Sahih Muslim 2594a).
> - He corrected children briefly and kindly, explained the reason, and avoided nagging.
> - He was fair between children, kept his word, and paused even important tasks to give them attention.
> - None of this requires perfection. Choose one tip a week and practise it.

## Prophetic parenting tips for love and affection

### 1. Show affection openly

**The hadith:** The Prophet ﷺ kissed his grandson al-Hasan while al-Aqra' ibn Habis sat beside him. Al-Aqra' said he had ten children and had never kissed any of them. The Prophet ﷺ looked at him and said:

مَنْ لاَ يَرْحَمُ لاَ يُرْحَمُ

"Whoever is not merciful to others will not be treated mercifully" ([Sahih al-Bukhari 5997](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:5997)). To a bedouin who said "We don't kiss them," he replied, "I cannot put mercy in your heart after Allah has taken it away from it" (Sahih al-Bukhari 5998).

**With a toddler:** Kisses at drop-off and pick-up, cuddles during stories, and lots of "I love you." Physical affection is how toddlers know they are safe.

**With a kindergartener:** Keep the hugs coming even when they start acting "big." Ask before kissing in front of friends; respecting their comfort is part of mercy.

### 2. Hug your children and make dua for them

**The hadith:** Usama ibn Zayd said the Prophet ﷺ would sit him on one thigh and al-Hasan on the other, embrace them both and say, "O Allah! Please be merciful to them, as I am merciful to them" (Sahih al-Bukhari 6003). When young Ibn Abbas set out water for his wudu, he prayed, "O Allah! Make him a learned scholar in religion" (Sahih al-Bukhari 143).

**With a toddler:** Make a short dua out loud while you hold them: "Ya Allah, bless my little one."

**With a kindergartener:** When they do something helpful, thank them with a dua, as the Prophet ﷺ did for Ibn Abbas: "May Allah make you wise." Children remember being prayed for.

### 3. Play with them on their level

**The hadith:** During a congregational prayer, the Prophet ﷺ made one sujud so long that the Companions thought something had happened. Afterwards he explained, "my son was riding on my back and I did not like to disturb him until he had enough" ([Sunan an-Nasa'i 1141](https://sunnah.com/nasai:1141), graded sahih).

**With a toddler:** Get on the floor. Let them climb on you, play peekaboo, roll a ball back and forth. Ten minutes of undivided floor time counts for a lot.

**With a kindergartener:** Let them lead the game and follow their rules. Board games, building blocks or a race to the park gate all build connection.

### 4. Take an interest in their world

**The hadith:** Anas ibn Malik said the Prophet ﷺ used to mix with them so closely that he would ask Anas's little brother, "O Aba Umair! What did the Nughair do?", asking after the boy's pet bird by its name (Sahih al-Bukhari 6129).

**With a toddler:** Name and notice what they love: "You found the red truck again!"

**With a kindergartener:** Remember the names of their friends, stuffed animals and favourite characters, and ask about them. "How is Teddy today?" says "your world matters to me."

## Prophetic parenting tips for gentleness and discipline

### 5. Make gentleness your default

**The hadith:**

إِنَّ الرِّفْقَ لاَ يَكُونُ فِي شَىْءٍ إِلاَّ زَانَهُ وَلاَ يُنْزَعُ مِنْ شَىْءٍ إِلاَّ شَانَهُ

"Kindness is not to be found in anything but that it adds to its beauty and it is not withdrawn from anything but it makes it defective" (Sahih Muslim 2594a). Aisha also reported that the Prophet ﷺ never struck anyone with his hand, "neither a woman nor a servant" (Sahih Muslim 2328a).

**With a toddler:** Lower your voice and your body. Kneel to eye level before giving an instruction.

**With a kindergartener:** Use a calm, firm tone for limits: "I won't let you hit. Hands down." The Canadian Paediatric Society's guide to [positive discipline for young children](https://caringforkids.cps.ca/handouts/behavior-and-development/positive-discipline-for-young-children) describes the same idea: discipline is about teaching, built on a warm relationship.

### 6. Don't nag over every mistake

**The hadith:** Anas said, "I served the Prophet ﷺ for ten years, and he never said to me, 'Uf' and never blamed me by saying, 'Why did you do so or why didn't you do so?'" (Sahih al-Bukhari 6038). Anas was about ten years old when he began (Sahih al-Bukhari 5166).

**With a toddler:** Let small things go. Spilled water, mismatched socks and a slow walk to the car are not battles.

**With a kindergartener:** Pick one or two behaviours to work on at a time. Replace "Why did you do that?" with "What can we do now?"

### 7. Stay calm about messes

**The hadith:** A baby was brought to the Prophet ﷺ and urinated on his clothes. He simply asked for water and poured it over the spot (Sahih al-Bukhari 222). No scolding, no fuss.

**With a toddler:** Accidents, spills and smeared food are part of learning. Clean up together with a calm "Oops, let's fix it."

**With a kindergartener:** Give them a role in the clean-up. Responsibility without shame teaches more than a lecture.

### 8. Teach manners with short, kind instructions

**The hadith:** Umar ibn Abi Salamah, who grew up in the Prophet's ﷺ household, said his hand used to wander around the dish while eating. The Prophet ﷺ told him:

يَا غُلاَمُ سَمِّ اللَّهَ، وَكُلْ بِيَمِينِكَ وَكُلْ مِمَّا يَلِيكَ

"O boy! Mention the Name of Allah and eat with your right hand, and eat of the dish what is nearer to you." Umar said he followed those instructions ever after ([Sahih al-Bukhari 5376](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:5376)).

**With a toddler:** One short instruction at a time: "Bismillah first."

**With a kindergartener:** Three clear steps, said warmly, at the moment it matters. Notice the Prophet ﷺ told the boy what *to* do rather than only what not to do. Our list of [daily duas for kids](/blog/daily-duas-for-kids) includes the eating duas.

### 9. Explain the reason behind a rule

**The hadith:** When young al-Hasan put a date from the charity collection in his mouth, the Prophet ﷺ told him to take it out and explained, "Don't you know that we do not eat a thing which is given in charity?" (Sahih al-Bukhari 1491).

**With a toddler:** Keep reasons very short: "Hot. Ouch." "Gentle, the cat is little."

**With a kindergartener:** Give a one-sentence reason: "We don't take toys from the classroom because they belong to everyone." Children follow rules they understand.

## Prophetic parenting tips for fairness, respect and trust

### 10. Be fair between siblings

**The hadith:** An-Nu'man ibn Bashir's father gave him a gift and asked the Prophet ﷺ to witness it. The Prophet ﷺ asked whether he had given the same to all his children. When he said no, the Prophet ﷺ said:

فَاتَّقُوا اللَّهَ، وَاعْدِلُوا بَيْنَ أَوْلاَدِكُمْ

"Be afraid of Allah, and be just to your children." The father took the gift back ([Sahih al-Bukhari 2587](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:2587)).

**With a toddler:** Fair doesn't always mean identical. A baby needs more holding; a toddler needs more patience. Make sure each child gets their own warm attention.

**With a kindergartener:** Be careful with gifts, treats and praise. Avoid "Why can't you be like your sister?" Comparisons quietly break sibling bonds.

### 11. Greet children with salam

**The hadith:** Anas ibn Malik passed by some boys and greeted them with salam, then said, "The Prophet ﷺ used to do so" (Sahih al-Bukhari 6247).

**With a toddler:** Say "As-salamu alaykum" when you walk into their room in the morning. They'll start answering long before they can say it clearly.

**With a kindergartener:** Greet them first at pick-up, before asking about their day. Being greeted like a person tells a child they matter.

### 12. Keep your word, even in small things

**The hadith:** Abdullah ibn Amir's mother called him over, saying, "Come here and I shall give you something." The Prophet ﷺ asked what she intended to give. She said dates. He said, "If you were not to give him anything, a lie would be recorded against you" (Sunan Abi Dawud 4991, graded hasan by al-Albani).

**With a toddler:** Don't use fake promises to get compliance ("Come here, I have candy"). Toddlers learn quickly whether your words are true.

**With a kindergartener:** If you say "five more minutes," mean five. If you promise the park on Saturday, go, or explain honestly why plans changed.

## Prophetic parenting tips for faith and attention

### 13. Make room for children in worship

**The hadith:** The Prophet ﷺ prayed while carrying his granddaughter Umamah, putting her down when he prostrated and picking her up when he stood (Sahih al-Bukhari 516). He also said, "When I stand for prayer, I intend to prolong it but on hearing the cries of a child, I cut it short, as I dislike to trouble the child's mother" (Sahih al-Bukhari 707).

**With a toddler:** Let them stand beside you on the mat, wander, and come back. Their presence is the lesson.

**With a kindergartener:** Give them their own prayer mat and invite them to pray one salah with you daily. Our guide on [how to teach kids salah](/blog/teach-kids-salah) walks through it by age.

### 14. Pause what you're doing for them

**The hadith:** While the Prophet ﷺ was giving a sermon, his grandsons al-Hasan and al-Husayn came stumbling in, wearing red shirts. He came down from the pulpit, picked them up and took them back up with him, then continued (Sunan Abi Dawud 1109, graded sahih by al-Albani). When his daughter Fatimah visited, he would stand to welcome her, take her by the hand, kiss her and seat her in his own place (Sunan Abi Dawud 5217, graded sahih).

**With a toddler:** When they run to show you something, put the phone down and look. A few seconds of real attention prevents many tantrums.

**With a kindergartener:** Make greetings an event. Stand up, smile, hug, and ask one specific question about their day.

### 15. Teach big ideas in a few words

**The hadith:** Ibn Abbas, a young boy at the time, was behind the Prophet ﷺ one day when he said:

يَا غُلاَمُ إِنِّي أُعَلِّمُكَ كَلِمَاتٍ احْفَظِ اللَّهَ يَحْفَظْكَ

"O boy! I will teach you a statement: Be mindful of Allah and He will protect you..." ([Jami' at-Tirmidhi 2516](https://sunnah.com/tirmidhi:2516), graded hasan sahih by at-Tirmidhi).

**With a toddler:** One idea, often repeated: "Allah loves you." "Allah made the moon."

**With a kindergartener:** Car rides and walks side by side are perfect for these short, memorable lessons. For more on this, see our guide to [teaching kids about Allah](/blog/teaching-kids-about-allah).

## Why the Prophet's ﷺ example works with young children

What stands out across these narrations is how ordinary the moments are: mealtimes, a pet bird, a wet garment, a child running into the masjid. The Prophet ﷺ didn't save his tenderness for special occasions. He made it the texture of everyday life, and that is exactly how young children learn.

He also showed that tenderness and strength go together. When his infant son Ibrahim was dying, the Prophet ﷺ wept and said, "The eyes are shedding tears and the heart is grieved, and we will not say except what pleases our Lord" (Sahih al-Bukhari 1303). Children who see their parents name their feelings and still turn to Allah learn to do the same.

The Prophet ﷺ summed up the principle: "Those who do not show mercy to our young ones and do not realise the right of our elders are not from us" (Sunan Abi Dawud 4943, graded sahih by al-Albani).

## How to start: one tip a week

Fifteen tips can feel like a lot. Try this instead:

1. **Step 1: Pick one tip** that would make the biggest difference in your home this week.
2. **Step 2: Write it on a sticky note** where you'll see it at your hardest moment of the day (often the morning rush or bedtime).
3. **Step 3: Practise it daily** and notice what changes in your child's response.
4. **Step 4: Talk about it with your spouse or co-parent** so children get the same message from both of you.
5. **Step 5: Add the next tip** once the first feels natural.

## Bringing it all together

Prophetic parenting isn't a technique. It's a way of seeing children: as people worthy of mercy, attention, respect and honesty. The Prophet ﷺ kissed them, played with them, paused his sermon for them, prayed with them on his shoulders and taught them in a few words they remembered for life. Pick one of these 15 prophetic parenting tips today, and let your children feel the difference.
