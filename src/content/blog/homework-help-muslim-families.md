The homework routine tips that work best are simple: the same start time every day, a short break first, a set place to work, and a clear finish line. For Muslim families there is one more anchor that makes the routine easier, not harder: the daily prayers. This guide shows how to build a calm after-school routine that fits homework, salah and Quran together, with timings by age and a seasonal plan for Canadian winters and summers.

> **Key takeaways**
> - A good homework routine is predictable: same time, same place, same order, with a break before starting.
> - A common rule of thumb is about 10 minutes of homework per grade per night, so roughly 30 minutes in Grade 3 and an hour in Grade 6.
> - Use salah as natural break points. In a Canadian winter, Asr and Maghrib often fall right after school; in summer they come much later.
> - Keep Quran review short and daily rather than long and occasional, and protect sleep: 9 to 11 hours for ages 5 to 13.

## What makes a good homework routine?

A homework routine is a consistent daily sequence for when, where and how your child does schoolwork. The goal is to make starting automatic, so you are not negotiating every evening.

The best routines share four features:

1. **A fixed start time** linked to something that always happens, like a snack or a prayer.
2. **A set workspace** with supplies ready, away from the TV.
3. **A predictable order**: what gets done first, when breaks happen, and what "finished" means.
4. **A parent nearby but not hovering**, available for questions and a final check.

Children thrive on predictability. When the routine is the boss, you do not have to be.

## How much homework is normal?

Researcher Harris Cooper, whose work is often cited on this question, describes the "10-minute rule": about 10 minutes of homework per grade, per night. By that measure a Grade 4 student would do around 40 minutes and a Grade 12 student around two hours. His team also found homework is more strongly linked to achievement in middle and high school than in elementary, where its main value is building study habits ([Duke Today summary](https://today.duke.edu/2006/03/homework.html)).

If your child regularly spends far longer than this, or melts down every night, talk to the teacher. The work may be too hard, too unclear, or simply too much.

## Homework routine at a glance by age

| Age | Typical homework | Quran review | Sleep target |
|---|---|---|---|
| 5–7 (K–Grade 2) | 10–20 minutes of reading or practice | 5–10 minutes | 9–11 hours |
| 8–10 (Grades 3–5) | 30–50 minutes | 10–15 minutes | 9–11 hours |
| 11–13 (Grades 6–8) | 60–80 minutes | 15–20 minutes | 9–11 hours |
| 14–15 (Grades 9–10) | 90–100 minutes, varies by course | 15–20 minutes | 8–10 hours |

Homework times follow the 10-minute rule; your child's school may assign more or less. Sleep targets come from the [Canadian 24-Hour Movement Guidelines for Children and Youth](https://csepguidelines.ca/guidelines/children-youth/), which also recommend at least 60 minutes of moderate to vigorous activity a day and no more than two hours of recreational screen time.

## Build the routine around salah

Prayer times give a Muslim household five built-in anchors every day. Instead of squeezing salah in around homework, let the prayers frame the work.

The challenge in Canada is that prayer times move a lot through the year. In Ottawa, Maghrib falls around 4:20 PM in December but closer to 8:50 PM in late June. The same routine cannot work in both seasons, so plan two versions.

| Season | When kids get home | How prayers fall | Suggested order |
|---|---|---|---|
| Winter (Nov–Feb) | About 3:30 PM | Asr is already in; Maghrib comes soon after, and Isha early in the evening | Snack, Asr, homework, Maghrib, dinner, Quran, Isha, bed |
| Spring and fall | About 3:30 PM | Asr in the mid to late afternoon; Maghrib around dinner or later | Snack and play, homework, Asr, dinner, Quran, Maghrib |
| Late spring to summer | About 3:30 PM | Asr in the late afternoon (around 5:30 PM or later); Maghrib and Isha very late | Snack, homework, Asr, play outside, dinner, Quran, Maghrib; younger children may sleep before Isha |

Check your local masjid's timetable or a trusted prayer-time app for exact times.

**A practical note for winter:** with Asr and Maghrib so close together, pray Asr as soon as your child is home and has had a snack. If you wait until homework is done, the window can close before you notice.

## Homework routine tips, step by step

### Step 1: Arrive, unpack and refuel

Bags open, lunch containers out, agenda or school app checked. Then a real snack: protein and fruit, not just crackers. A hungry child cannot focus.

### Step 2: Move first

Give 15 to 30 minutes of active play before sitting down: outside if possible, a dance or a trampoline inside if not. Children who have sat still all day need to move before they can sit still again.

### Step 3: Pray, then start

Use the next prayer as the starting gun. "We pray Asr, then homework" is easier to follow than "start at 4:15." Wudu itself is a reset: cool water and a few quiet minutes help a scattered child calm down.

### Step 4: Set up the workspace

A kitchen table works well for younger children, because a parent is nearby. Older children can use a desk, but keep phones and tablets in another room unless they are needed for the task.

### Step 5: Do the hardest task first

Attention is highest at the start. Begin with the subject your child finds hardest, then move to easier work. For long assignments, break them into chunks of 15 to 25 minutes with a short stretch between.

### Step 6: Check, sign and pack

When work is done, glance over it together (do not fix it for them), sign anything that needs a signature, and pack the bag by the door. A packed bag tonight means a calm morning tomorrow.

### Step 7: Wind down

Quran review, a story, Isha if it falls before bed, and lights out. Switching screens off about an hour before bed is a widely recommended sleep habit.

## Where Quran fits

Quran time works best as a short, fixed slot rather than a long session competing with homework. Ten to fifteen minutes after dinner, or between Maghrib and bed, is enough for most children who attend public school. The key is daily review: a few lines revised every day protect what your child already knows.

## Make study an act of worship

Islam treats seeking knowledge as worthy and rewarded, and that includes schoolwork done with good intentions. A few small habits make that visible to children.

**Start with a dua.** Many families teach the dua of Prophet Musa (Moses), peace be upon him, before studying ([Surah Ta-Ha 20:25–28](https://quran.com/20/25-28)):

رَبِّ ٱشْرَحْ لِى صَدْرِى وَيَسِّرْ لِىٓ أَمْرِى وَٱحْلُلْ عُقْدَةً مِّن لِّسَانِى يَفْقَهُوا۟ قَوْلِى

*Rabbi-shrah lī ṣadrī, wa yassir lī amrī, waḥlul ʿuqdatan min lisānī, yafqahū qawlī*: "My Lord, expand my chest for me, make my task easy for me, and untie the knot from my tongue so they may understand my speech."

A shorter option for young children is from [Surah Ta-Ha 20:114](https://quran.com/20/114):

رَّبِّ زِدْنِى عِلْمًا

*Rabbi zidnī ʿilmā*: "My Lord, increase me in knowledge."

**Talk about intention.** Remind children that learning to read, count and understand the world can be done for Allah: to be useful to their family and community.

**Value consistency over intensity.** The Prophet ﷺ said that "the most beloved deed to Allah is the most regular and constant even if it were little" ([Sahih al-Bukhari 6464](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:6464)). That applies beautifully to homework: 30 calm minutes every day beats a three-hour panic on Sunday night.

## Common homework problems and fixes

### "I'll do it later"

Procrastination usually means the child does not know how to start. Ask, "What is the first small thing you need to do?" and stay nearby until they have done it. A visual timer helps many children.

### Tears and meltdowns

If homework ends in tears most nights, the problem is usually difficulty, fatigue or hunger. Check the basics (snack, movement, sleep), then talk to the teacher about the workload.

### Too many activities

Between sports, Quran class and homework, some weeknights simply do not work. Keep at least two weekday evenings light, and move learning that does not have a due date, such as extra Arabic or Quran practice, to the weekend.

### Homework during Ramadan

Ramadan 2027 is expected to begin around February 8, depending on moon sighting, when Ottawa days are still short. For children who fast, do homework soon after school while they still have energy, and keep the evening for iftar, family and taraweeh. Tell the teacher if your child is fasting so they understand slower afternoons.
