Setting up parental controls for kids takes about an hour and protects them for years. The most effective setup for Muslim families combines four layers: YouTube Kids in "Approve content yourself" mode, Google Family Link (Android) or Apple Screen Time (iPhone and iPad), a family-safe DNS filter on your home router, and a hand-picked playlist of halal content. This guide gives you numbered, step-by-step instructions for each, checked against the current help pages for October 2026.

> **Key takeaways**
> - No single tool is enough. Layer device controls, app controls and router filtering.
> - For young children, YouTube Kids' **"Approve content yourself"** setting is the safest option: your child sees only the videos and channels you choose, and search is off.
> - Apple's Screen Time has been redesigned for iOS 27, with new **Time Allowances** and **Screen Time Schedules**.
> - The Canadian Paediatric Society advises no screen use under 2 (except video chats) and about an hour or less of routine screen time a day for ages 2 to 5.
> - Controls work best alongside clear family rules, shared viewing and good examples from parents.

## Why parental controls matter for Muslim families

Every family wants its children to be safe online. Muslim parents often add a second goal: protecting the values, modesty and adab they work hard to nurture at home. Autoplay, algorithm-driven recommendations and in-app ads can undo that quickly, especially for children who can't yet read.

Parental controls are not about mistrust. They are a way of building a safe "garden" where children can explore, while you choose what grows in it. As children mature, you gradually open the gate. ## How much screen time is right for kids?

Before setting limits, it helps to know what paediatricians recommend. The Canadian Paediatric Society's [guidance for young children](https://caringforkids.cps.ca/handouts/behavior-and-development/screen-time-and-young-children) says:

| Age | Canadian Paediatric Society guidance |
|---|---|
| Under 2 | Screen use is not recommended, except video chatting with caring adults such as grandparents |
| 2 to 5 | About an hour or less per day of routine or sedentary screen time |
| All ages | No screens for at least 1 hour before bed; keep screens out of bedrooms; turn devices off during meals |

For school-aged children, the CPS [advice for parents of school-aged children and teens](https://caringforkids.cps.ca/handouts/behavior-and-development/screen-time-and-digital-media) does not set a fixed daily limit. Instead, it recommends that you **manage** (a family media plan with rules and limits), **encourage meaningful use** (educational, age-appropriate content, with sleep, school, meals and play coming first) and **model** healthy habits yourself.

## Layer 1: Set up YouTube Kids safely

YouTube Kids is a separate app from YouTube, designed for children 12 and under. Each child gets their own profile (up to 8 per parent account), and you pick a content setting for each.

| Content setting | Designed for | Search |
|---|---|---|
| Preschool | Ages 4 and under | Limited to content for 4 and under |
| Younger | Ages 5 to 8 | Limited to content for 8 and under |
| Older | Ages 9 to 12 | Limited to content for 12 and under |
| Approve content yourself | Any age | Off: your child sees only what you approve |

YouTube says its systems try to filter unsuitable content in the age-based settings, but not all videos are manually reviewed. That's why we recommend **Approve content yourself** for young children.

### How to turn on "Approve content yourself" in YouTube Kids

Step 1: Open the YouTube Kids app on your child's device.

Step 2: Tap the **Settings** icon in the corner of the screen.

Step 3: Solve the multiplication problem or enter your custom passcode.

Step 4: Select your child's profile and enter your parent account password.

Step 5: Tap **EDIT SETTINGS**.

Step 6: Select **Approve content yourself**, then review the "Getting Started" pop-up.

Step 7: Tap **Select**, then tap the add icon on any collection, channel or video you want to approve.

Step 8: Tap **DONE** at the bottom of the screen.

To add or remove items later, go back to the profile's settings and tap **Manage** under "Approved content only." Note that this setting can be turned on only in the YouTube Kids app, not from the main YouTube app.

### Two more YouTube Kids settings worth using

- **Set a timer:** Tap **Settings**, complete the passcode, tap **Set Timer**, choose a time, and tap **START TIMER**. When time is up, your child sees a "Time's up!" message and the app locks. See YouTube's guide to [limiting screen time on YouTube Kids](https://support.google.com/youtubekids/answer/6130558?hl=en).
- **Set a custom passcode:** Older children quickly learn multiplication. A custom passcode keeps settings in your hands.

## Layer 2: Google Family Link for Android devices

Google Family Link is a free app that lets parents supervise a child's Google Account and Android device: approving apps, setting time limits and filtering content. Install **Family Link** on your own phone (Android or iPhone). Your child and their device need to be nearby while you set it up, and Google estimates about 15 minutes to create a child's account.

### How to set up Family Link

Step 1: On your phone, install the Family Link app and sign in with your own Google Account.

Step 2: Create a Google Account for your child (for children under 13, or the applicable age in your country) and follow the prompts to give parental consent. If your child already has an account, you can add supervision instead: on the child's Android device, go to **Settings**, then **Google**, then **All services**, then **Parental controls** under "Kids & family."

Step 3: Sign your child in on their device and link it to Family Link.

### Set daily limits and schedules

Step 4: In Family Link, select your child at the top left, then tap **Screen time**, then **Time limits**. Turn on **Daily limit** and tap **Weekly schedule** to set each day's limit.

Step 5: For bedtime and school hours, tap **Screen time**, then **Schedules**, and turn on **Downtime** or **School time**. Use **Weekly schedule** to set start and end times. Google's [schedule guide](https://support.google.com/families/answer/15938652?hl=en) explains how to allow certain "Unlimited time" apps during these periods.

Step 6: For individual apps, tap **Screen time**, then **Time limits**, then **App limits**, choose an app and tap **Set limit**. You can also mark apps as **Unlimited time** (for example, a Quran app) or block them.

### Filter Google Play, Chrome and YouTube

Step 7: Tap **Controls**, then **Google Play**, choose a content type (apps and games, movies, books) and set the highest rating you allow. You can also require your approval for downloads and purchases.

Step 8: Still under **Controls**, review the filters for Google Chrome and Search, then tap **YouTube** and change the content level under **YouTube Kids Settings**.

## Layer 3: Apple Screen Time for iPhone and iPad

Apple redesigned Screen Time for iOS 27 and iPadOS 27. According to Apple's [Screen Time guide for parents](https://support.apple.com/en-ca/108806), all devices in your Family Sharing group should be updated to version 27 to use the new parental controls. Devices that can't be updated can keep using the previous version of Screen Time.

### Before you start

Step 1: Set up **Family Sharing** on your own iPhone or iPad.

Step 2: Create a **Child Account** for your child if they don't have one. Each child should have their own Apple Account, not share yours.

Step 3: Update all family devices to the latest software, then follow the prompts to upgrade your parental controls.

### Turn on Screen Time for your child

Step 4: On your own device, open **Settings**, tap **Family**, then tap your child's name.

Step 5: Tap **Screen Time** and follow the onscreen instructions. Apple recommends managing your child's controls from the parent's device, where you also set the Screen Time passcode.

### Choose apps and websites

Step 6: In your child's settings, tap **Apps & Websites**. Here you can allow or block specific apps and websites, and manage **Ask to Buy** (your child requests approval before downloading or buying) and **Ask to Browse** (your child requests approval before visiting a new website).

Step 7: Tap **Restrictions**, then **Filtering** under Websites, and choose **Approved Websites Only**, **Limit Adult Websites** or **Unrestricted**. Apple says Approved Websites Only is on by default for Child Accounts under 13, and Limit Adult Websites is on by default for under 18 (ages vary by region).

### Set time allowances and schedules

Step 8: Tap **Time Allowances**, then the Add button, to set how long your child can use categories such as Entertainment, Games and Social Media each day. Apple provides age-based starting points that you can adjust.

Step 9: Tap **Screen Time Schedule**, turn on **Schedule**, then tap **Add Schedule**. Use time windows like Before School, School and After School, with separate routines for weekdays and weekends.

Step 10: Under **Always Allowed**, choose the contacts and apps your child can always reach, such as Phone, Messages with parents, or a Quran app.

### Check content ratings and communication safety

Step 11: Use **Content & Privacy Restrictions** to set age ratings for music, films, TV and books, and to lock settings like Share My Location.

Step 12: Make sure **Communication Safety** is on. It warns children about photos and videos detected to contain nudity, gore or violence, and Apple doesn't get access to the images.

**Tip for shared family iPads:** If your young child uses your iPad, consider **Guided Access** (in Settings, then Accessibility), which locks the device into a single app until you enter a passcode.

## Layer 4: Router-level filtering for the whole home

Device controls protect one device. Router-level filtering protects every device on your Wi-Fi, including smart TVs, game consoles and visitors' tablets. The simplest method is a **family-safe DNS service**, which blocks known adult and malicious websites before they load.

Two free, widely used options:

| Service | DNS addresses (IPv4) | What it blocks |
|---|---|---|
| Cloudflare 1.1.1.1 for Families | 1.1.1.3 and 1.0.0.3 | Malware and adult content |
| OpenDNS FamilyShield | 208.67.222.123 and 208.67.220.123 | Adult content (preset, no account needed) |

Cloudflare's [setup documentation](https://developers.cloudflare.com/1.1.1.1/setup/) lists these addresses and includes instructions for routers and devices.

### How to set a family DNS on your router

Step 1: Find your router's admin address (often printed on a sticker on the router) and log in.

Step 2: Look for **DNS settings**. They may be labelled DNS, WAN, Internet or Advanced settings.

Step 3: Switch from automatic DNS to manual, and enter the two addresses from your chosen service.

Step 4: Save, then restart the router.

Step 5: Test by visiting a site you expect to be blocked from a device on your Wi-Fi.

**Important limitations:** Some internet-provider routers don't allow DNS changes; in that case, check your provider's own parental-control features or set the DNS on each device. DNS filtering doesn't work on mobile data, and tech-savvy children can sometimes bypass it with a VPN or private DNS. Treat it as one layer, not the whole solution.

## Build a halal content playlist

Blocking harmful content is only half the job. The other half is filling the space with content that nourishes your child. Using **Approve content yourself** in YouTube Kids, you can build a playlist that reflects your family's values.

Step 1: **Choose categories.** For example: Quran recitation for children, duas and Islamic manners, Arabic letters and words, prophet stories told for children, nature and science, and gentle crafts.

Step 2: **Preview before approving.** Watch at least one or two full videos from each channel, including the ending, to check for unsuitable ads, music, language or messages your family avoids.

Step 3: **Prefer channels over single videos** once you trust them, so new uploads appear without extra work, but re-check them every few months.

Step 4: **Respect your family's approach to music.** Families and scholars differ on this. If your family avoids instruments, look for vocals-only nasheeds and recitation.

Step 5: **Rotate.** Swap in new content monthly so your child doesn't feel restricted, and remove anything that no longer fits.

## Family rules that make controls work

Technology can't replace parenting. These habits turn settings into a healthy family culture:

- **Make a family media plan together,** as the CPS recommends: where devices live, when they're used and what's allowed.
- **Keep devices in shared spaces,** never in bedrooms overnight.
- **Watch together** when you can, and talk about what you see.
- **Protect prayer, meal and family times** as screen-free.
- **Model what you teach.** Children notice when parents scroll during dinner.
- **Explain the "why."** Connect rules to values: guarding our eyes, using time well, and being kind online.

The gentle, consistent guidance of the Prophet Muhammad (peace be upon him) is a good model here; our article on [prophetic parenting tips](/blog/prophetic-parenting-tips) explores this further.

## An Ontario note for school-aged children

If your child attends an Ontario public school, the province introduced rules in 2024 requiring students in kindergarten to Grade 6 to keep phones silent and out of sight during the school day unless an educator gives permission. Check your child's school for its current policy. For younger children, the bigger question is usually tablets and TV at home, which is where the settings above make the most difference.
