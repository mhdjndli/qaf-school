The best questions to ask on a school tour are the ones that reveal how the school works on an ordinary Tuesday: who teaches, what children actually do all day, how safety is handled, and what you will pay in total. A tour is short, and it is easy to leave with a good feeling but few facts. Below are 40 questions to ask on a school tour of an Islamic or private school, grouped by topic, plus what to watch for and the red flags that should make you pause.

> **Key takeaways**
> - Go in with a list. Pick the 10 to 15 questions that matter most to your family and ask every school the same ones.
> - In Ontario, private elementary schools are not inspected by the ministry, so questions about teachers, curriculum, safety checks and contracts matter more.
> - For an Islamic school, ask how much time goes to Quran, Arabic and Islamic Studies, and how progress is measured.
> - Watch the classroom as much as you listen to the tour guide. Children's behaviour and teachers' tone tell you a lot.
> - Ask for fees, refund rules and policies in writing before you sign anything.

## Why your questions matter more at a private school

Public school boards set curriculum, hire certified teachers and follow provincial rules. Private schools are different. Ontario's [private schools page](https://www.ontario.ca/page/private-schools) says the ministry does not inspect private elementary schools, does not require them to follow the Ontario curriculum, and does not require teachers to be members of the Ontario College of Teachers. It also tells parents they "must do your own research."

That does not mean private schools are weaker. Many are excellent. It means **you** are the quality check. The ministry itself suggests questions about criminal reference checks, codes of conduct, complaints procedures, contracts and liability insurance, and we have built those into the list below.

## Before the tour: quick prep

- **Check the school is listed.** Ontario publishes a [list of private schools operating in the province](https://data.ontario.ca/dataset/private-school-contact-information). Being listed is not an endorsement, but every private school must submit an annual Notice of Intention to Operate.
- **Read the website and fee page.** Note anything unclear to ask about.
- **Ask to visit during class time.** Seeing a real morning is worth more than an empty classroom after hours.
- **Bring your child if the school allows it.** Watch how staff greet them.

If you want to know what a typical visit involves, see [what to expect when you visit an Islamic school](/blog/visit-islamic-school-what-to-expect).

## 40 questions to ask on a school tour

### Teaching and curriculum

1. **Do you follow the Ontario curriculum, and how?** For kindergarten, ask how the program aligns with *The Ontario Curriculum, Kindergarten, 2026*.
2. **What does a typical day look like, hour by hour?** Ask to see a written schedule.
3. **How much of the day is play, hands-on work, whole-group teaching and outdoor time?**
4. **How do you teach early reading and math?** Ask for an example of a lesson.
5. **How do you support a child who is ahead, or one who is struggling?**
6. **How do children here do when they move to Grade 1 or a new school?**

### Teachers and staff

7. **Are your teachers members of the Ontario College of Teachers?** You can search the public register at [oct.ca](https://www.oct.ca/en-ca).
8. **Are any staff registered Early Childhood Educators?** The College of ECE has its own [public register](https://college-ece.ca/members/public-register/).
9. **What training do your Quran and Arabic teachers have?** Ask about tajweed qualifications or ijazah for Quran teachers.
10. **How long have the current teachers been at the school?** High turnover is worth asking about.
11. **What is the adult-to-child ratio in my child's class, and who covers when a teacher is absent?**

### Quran, Arabic and Islamic Studies

12. **How many minutes a day go to Quran, Arabic and Islamic Studies?**
13. **Which surahs or Quran milestones do children usually reach by the end of the year?**
14. **How do you teach Quran reading: which qaida or method, and from what age?**
15. **Is Arabic taught as a subject, or used as a language of instruction during the day?** Our guide to [Arabic immersion in kindergarten](/blog/arabic-immersion-kindergarten) explains the difference.
16. **How do you include children who do not speak Arabic at home?**
17. **How is salah taught, and do children pray at school?**
18. **What is your approach to Islamic teaching?** Ask whether it is mainstream and non-sectarian, and how teachers handle questions where families differ.

### Daily routine and environment

19. **What are drop-off and pick-up times, and what happens if I am late?**
20. **How often do children go outside, including in winter?**
21. **How do you handle discipline and big emotions?** Ask for a real example.
22. **Do you have a code of conduct or behaviour policy I can read?**
23. **How do you help a new child settle in the first weeks?**

### Safety, health and food

24. **Do all staff and volunteers have criminal reference checks?**
25. **Are you nut-free, and how do you handle allergies and medication?**
26. **Is all food required to be halal, and who provides lunch and snacks?**
27. **How do you handle illness, injuries and emergencies? Who is first-aid trained?**
28. **Who can pick up my child, and how do you check?**
29. **Do you carry liability insurance for accidents involving students?**

### Communication with parents

30. **How will I know how my child's day went?** Daily photos, an app, a weekly note?
31. **How do I contact the teacher, and how quickly do you reply?**
32. **How often do you report on progress, and in what form?**
33. **Do you have a written procedure for parent complaints?**

### Fees, contracts and policies

34. **What is the total yearly cost, including every mandatory fee?**
35. **Is there a sibling discount or payment plan?**
36. **Do I sign a contract, and what is the refund or withdrawal policy?**
37. **Do you publish your admissions policy?**
38. **How long has the school been operating?**
39. **What receipts do you issue, and when?** This helps if you plan to claim any child care portion on your taxes.
40. **Is there a waitlist, and when do I need to confirm a place for September?**

For more on reading fee pages, see our guide to [Ottawa Islamic school fees](/blog/ottawa-islamic-school-fees-explained).

## What to watch for while you are there

Some of the best information is not in the answers.

- **The children.** Are they engaged and calm, or drifting? Do they seem comfortable approaching adults?
- **The teachers' tone.** Warm and firm is what you want. Listen for how adults speak to a child who is upset.
- **The room.** Is it clean, organized and set up for children to work independently? Are materials within reach?
- **The walls.** Children's own work, Arabic letters, duas and real writing tell you what is valued.
- **Transitions.** Watch how a class moves from one activity to the next. Smooth transitions usually mean strong routines.
- **How your questions are received.** A good school is glad you asked.

## Red flags on a school tour

Pause before you commit if you notice:

- Vague answers about teacher qualifications or reference checks.
- No written fee schedule, contract or refund policy.
- Pressure to pay a deposit before you have had time to think.
- Children spending long periods sitting and copying, with little talk or play, in kindergarten.
- A Quran program that is all memorization with no attention to reading, meaning or manners, or no clear way of tracking progress.
- Reluctance to let you see a real class in session.

One red flag is not necessarily a deal-breaker. Ask a follow-up question and see how the school responds.

## After the tour: compare schools fairly

Write down your impressions the same day, while they are fresh. Then:

**Step 1:** Score each school on the same criteria. Our [Islamic kindergarten checklist](/blog/choosing-islamic-kindergarten-checklist) gives you a ready-made scorecard.

**Step 2:** Put the total yearly cost and the daily commute side by side.

**Step 3:** Ask other parents. Current families will tell you what the tour cannot.

**Step 4:** Revisit your goals. If you are unsure whether the extra cost makes sense, read [is Islamic school worth it?](/blog/is-islamic-school-worth-it).

Our guide to [Islamic schools in Ottawa](/blog/islamic-schools-in-ottawa-guide) lists the local options so you can plan your visits.

## Conclusion

A school tour is your best chance to see past the brochure.
