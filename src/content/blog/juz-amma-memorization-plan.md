A good Juz Amma memorization plan gives your child a small, fixed portion each week, builds in review weeks, and works through the 37 surahs from An-Nas back to An-Naba. Spread over 52 weeks, that is roughly one or two short lines a day, which most children aged 7 and up can manage in 15–20 minutes. Below is a complete week-by-week table you can print, plus how to adjust it for younger or faster children.

Juz Amma (the 30th juz) runs from Surah An-Naba (78) to Surah An-Nas (114): 37 surahs and 564 ayat in total, about 23 pages in the common 604-page Madani mushaf. The plan below is the one we would hand a family who asks, “Where do we even start?”

> **Key takeaways**
> - Juz Amma has 37 surahs and 564 ayat; a one-year plan means about 11 ayat a week on average.
> - Start at An-Nas and work backwards: the surahs get longer as your child’s stamina grows.
> - Every sixth or seventh week is a review week with no new memorization. Do not skip these.
> - Daily sessions of 15–20 minutes beat one long weekend session.
> - If a week is too heavy, split it in two. Taking 15–18 months is a success, not a failure.

## How the Juz Amma memorization plan works

The plan uses three simple rules.

**1. Backwards order.** Children usually learn the shortest surahs first (An-Nas, Al-Falaq, Al-Ikhlas), so the plan moves from surah 114 down to 78. Early weeks are light, which builds confidence. By the time your child reaches An-Naba and An-Nazi‘at, they have months of practice behind them.

**2. Weeks are balanced by length, not by surah.** A week might be two tiny surahs (Al-Kawthar and Al-Ma‘un) or half of one long surah (Al-Fajr 15–30). Where we split a surah, we split at a natural break in meaning, so your child learns complete ideas.

**3. Review weeks are built in.** Weeks 6, 13, 19, 26, 31, 39, 44 and 49–52 have no new lessons. They are for making old surahs solid. The Prophet ﷺ warned that the Quran slips away quickly if it is not looked after: “Commit yourself to the Qur’an … it is surely more prone to break away than a camel in its bind” ([Sahih al-Bukhari 5033](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:5033)).

Before Week 1, make sure your child knows **Surah Al-Fatiha** (1:1–7). It is not part of Juz Amma, but it is recited in every rak‘ah of salah and should come first. Our guide to [short surahs for kids](/blog/short-surahs-for-kids) covers Al-Fatiha with transliteration.

The load rises gently through the year. In the first term your child learns fewer than 10 ayat in most weeks. By the final term, the ayat are short (An-Naba’s ayat are often three or four words) but there are more of them, about 20 a week, or 3–4 a day.

## A daily routine for each week

The table tells you *what* to learn. This routine tells you *how* to spend each day of the week. Keep it to 15–20 minutes for children aged 7–10, and 10 minutes for younger children on the gentle track below.

**Step 1: Listen first (3–5 minutes).** Play the new portion from a clear, slow reciter on [Quran.com](https://quran.com/juz/30) or a trusted app. Your child follows with a finger in the mushaf if they can read, or just listens if they cannot.

**Step 2: Repeat in small pieces (5 minutes).** Break the portion into phrases. Your child repeats each phrase three to five times, then links two phrases together, then the whole ayah.

**Step 3: Recite the new portion without help (2 minutes).** Correct gently. If a word is shaky, go back to Step 2 for that word only.

**Step 4: Revise yesterday and last week (5 minutes).** Recite the previous day’s portion, then one older surah from your rotation.

**Step 5: Close with a dua.** Many families end with the Quranic dua:

وَقُل رَّبِّ زِدْنِى عِلْمًا

“And say, ‘My Lord, increase me in knowledge’” ([Surah Ta-Ha 20:114](https://quran.com/20/114)).

### What each day of the week looks like

| Day | Focus |
|---|---|
| Monday–Thursday | Steps 1–5 with that week’s new portion, about a quarter each day |
| Friday | Recite the whole week’s portion from memory |
| Saturday | Review only: last two weeks plus one older surah |
| Sunday | Rest, or recite to a teacher in a class or halaqa |

If your family prefers a different rest day, move it. The point is five short days of new work and at least one day of pure review.

## Adjusting the plan for your child’s age

Children differ a lot in memory, attention and reading ability, so treat this as a template rather than a race. Our article on the [best age to start hifz](/blog/best-age-to-start-hifz) goes deeper into readiness.

### Gentle track: ages 4–6

Use only **Weeks 1–13** as your first year. That is Juz Amma’s last 16 surahs (An-Nas to Az-Zalzalah), with plenty of time to repeat. Young children memorize by ear, so the listening step does most of the work. Ten minutes a day is enough, and surahs learned at this age tend to stay for life if they are recited in salah and before bed.

### Standard track: ages 7–10

Follow the 52-week table as written. Expect some weeks to need splitting, especially Weeks 25, 37, 42 and 45. When you split a week, push everything after it back by a week. A child who finishes in 60 weeks has still memorized a full juz.

### Faster track: ages 11 and up, or a child already in hifz

Combine two weeks into one for the lighter first half (Weeks 1–26), and keep the review weeks. Some older children finish Juz Amma in about six months this way. The temptation is to drop review to go faster; resist it.

## Making new memorization stick

Fast memorization is easy. Keeping it is the hard part. A few habits make the difference.

- **Recite new surahs in salah.** Once a surah is solid, your child should use it in their prayers that week. Salah turns a memorized surah into a used surah.
- **Use a revision rotation.** After Week 13, divide everything already memorized into five or six groups and revise one group a day. Our [hifz revision schedule](/blog/hifz-revision-schedule) explains the sabaq, sabqi and manzil system in detail.
- **Learn the meaning in one sentence.** Before starting a surah, tell your child what it is about in a single sentence (“Al-Fil is about the army with elephants that tried to destroy the Ka‘bah”). Meaning helps memory and builds love.
- **Watch similar verses.** Several surahs in Juz Amma share phrases that children mix up, for example the openings of At-Takwir, Al-Infitar and Al-Inshiqaq, which all begin with “when.” Point these out early.
- **Track it visibly.** A printed copy of the table on the fridge with a sticker for each completed week works well. For more ideas, see our guide to building a [hifz tracker](/blog/hifz-progress-tracker).

The Prophet ﷺ said that “the most beloved deed to Allah is the most regular and constant even if it were little” ([Sahih al-Bukhari 6464](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:6464)). Fifteen minutes every day will carry your child further than an hour once a week.

## Common reasons a plan stalls, and what to do

**Your child keeps forgetting older surahs.** Stop new lessons for a week and run a review week early. Then reduce the size of new portions by a third.

**Recitation is fast but full of mistakes.** Slow down the audio, and have a teacher listen at least weekly. Mistakes memorized early are hard to remove later. A short course in [tajweed rules for kids](/blog/tajweed-rules-for-kids) helps once your child reads.

**Your child dreads the session.** Shorten it, move it to a better time (after a snack, not before bed when tired), and add praise. The Prophet ﷺ said the one who recites the Quran with difficulty, stumbling over it, has two rewards ([Sahih Muslim 798a](https://sunnah.com/muslim:798a)). Share that with a child who feels slow.

**The family travels or Ramadan changes the routine.** Pause new lessons and keep a minimum of daily review. Pick up the table where you left off.

## Final thoughts

Juz Amma is where most Muslims begin their relationship with memorizing the Quran, and a clear plan turns “we should really do this” into steady progress. Print the table, start at An-Nas this week, protect the review weeks, and adjust freely. The goal is not to finish fast; it is for your child to know these surahs well enough to recite them in salah for the rest of their life.
