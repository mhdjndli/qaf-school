Islamic gratitude activities for kids turn *shukr* (gratitude to Allah) from a word into a daily habit. Saying "Alhamdulillah" at set moments, keeping a blessings jar, making a "99 blessings" chain and writing thank-you notes all help children notice Allah's gifts and thank the people around them. Below are 20 simple routines, crafts and games for ages 3 to 12, each rooted in the Quran and Sunnah.

> **Key takeaways**
> - Shukr has three parts: recognising a blessing in the heart, praising Allah with the tongue, and using the blessing well with our actions.
> - Allah promises, "If you are grateful, I will surely increase you" (Surah Ibrahim 14:7).
> - Thanking people is part of thanking Allah. The Prophet ﷺ said that whoever does not thank people has not thanked Allah (Sunan Abi Dawud 4811, graded sahih).
> - Short, daily habits work better than one big lesson. Attach "Alhamdulillah" to moments that already happen, like waking up, meals and bedtime.

## What is shukr? Teaching gratitude in Islam

**Shukr** is gratitude to Allah for His blessings, shown in the heart, on the tongue and through our actions. For a child, you can put it simply: *"Shukr means noticing Allah's gifts, saying thank you, and using them in good ways."*

The Quran returns to gratitude again and again. Allah says:

وَإِذْ تَأَذَّنَ رَبُّكُمْ لَئِن شَكَرْتُمْ لَأَزِيدَنَّكُمْ ۖ وَلَئِن كَفَرْتُمْ إِنَّ عَذَابِى لَشَدِيدٌ

"And remember when your Lord proclaimed: 'If you are grateful, I will surely increase you; but if you are ungrateful, My punishment is severe'" ([Surah Ibrahim 14:7](https://quran.com/14/7)).

Luqman, the wise father whose advice to his son fills a whole surah, was told first of all to be grateful: "Be grateful to Allah. Whoever is grateful benefits only himself" ([Surah Luqman 31:12](https://quran.com/31/12)). Allah also reminds us that His blessings are too many to count ([Surah An-Nahl 16:18](https://quran.com/16/18)). That makes a lovely challenge for kids: *let's try to count them anyway.*

The Prophet ﷺ modelled gratitude through worship. He prayed at night until his feet swelled, and when asked why, he replied, "Should I not be a grateful servant?" (Sahih al-Bukhari 4836).

Gratitude also helps children's well-being. Research summarised by UC Berkeley's [Greater Good Science Center](https://greatergood.berkeley.edu/topic/gratitude/definition) links gratitude with greater life satisfaction, more positive emotion and more generosity, including among children and teens.

## Daily shukr routines

### 1. "Alhamdulillah" anchor moments

**What it is:** Say "Alhamdulillah" together at the same moments every day: waking up, after meals, after sneezing and when something good happens.
**Ages:** 2+.
**How:** Start with one anchor, such as meals, and add another each week. Young children copy what they hear, so say it out loud yourself.

### 2. The waking-up dua

**What it is:** The Prophet's ﷺ morning words of gratitude for a new day:

الْحَمْدُ لِلَّهِ الَّذِي أَحْيَانَا بَعْدَ مَا أَمَاتَنَا وَإِلَيْهِ النُّشُورُ

*Al-hamdu lillahil-ladhi ahyana ba'da ma amatana wa ilayhin-nushur.* "All praise is for Allah, who gave us life after causing us to die, and to Him is the return" (Sahih al-Bukhari 6324).
**Ages:** 3+.
**How:** Put a picture card by the bed. Our [daily duas for kids](/blog/daily-duas-for-kids) guide has the full set of everyday duas.

### 3. The after-eating dua

**What it is:**

الْحَمْدُ لِلَّهِ الَّذِي أَطْعَمَنِي هَذَا وَرَزَقَنِيهِ مِنْ غَيْرِ حَوْلٍ مِنِّي وَلاَ قُوَّةٍ

*Al-hamdu lillahil-ladhi at'amani hadha wa razaqanihi min ghayri hawlin minni wa la quwwah.* "All praise is for Allah, who fed me this and provided it for me without any power or strength from me" (Jami' at-Tirmidhi 3458, graded hasan).
**Ages:** 4+.
**How:** Ask, "Who made this possible?" Allah, the farmer, the truck driver, the grocer, the cook. One meal becomes a lesson in how many people Allah uses to provide for us.

### 4. Three blessings at bedtime

**What it is:** Before sleep, each person shares three things they're grateful for today, one of which must be "small," like warm socks or a funny moment.
**Ages:** 4+.
**How:** Fold it into your [Islamic bedtime routine](/blog/islamic-bedtime-routine) after the short surahs and before the story.

### 5. Gratitude in salah

**What it is:** Help children notice that every prayer begins with gratitude: *Al-hamdu lillahi Rabbil-'alamin*, "All praise is for Allah, Lord of all the worlds" (Al-Fatiha 1:2).
**Ages:** 6+.
**How:** After prayer, ask one question: "What's one thing you thanked Allah for in your heart today?"

### 6. The dua of Prophet Sulayman

**What it is:** When Prophet Sulayman (peace be upon him) heard an ant warning its colony, he smiled and asked Allah to help him be grateful:

رَبِّ أَوْزِعْنِىٓ أَنْ أَشْكُرَ نِعْمَتَكَ ٱلَّتِىٓ أَنْعَمْتَ عَلَىَّ وَعَلَىٰ وَٰلِدَىَّ

"My Lord, inspire me to be grateful for Your favour which You have given me and my parents" (part of [Surah An-Naml 27:19](https://quran.com/27/19)).
**Ages:** 7+.
**How:** Tell the story of the ant, then learn this short part of the dua together. Children love that a prophet was grateful because of a tiny ant.

## Shukr crafts for kids

### 7. The blessings jar

**What it is:** A jar where family members drop a slip of paper whenever they notice a blessing.
**Ages:** 3+ (with help writing).
**How:** Read the slips together on Friday after Maghrib, or save them for the last night of the year. Younger children can draw instead of writing.

### 8. "Too many to count" paper chain

**What it is:** Based on Surah An-Nahl 16:18, each paper link names one blessing. Watch the chain grow across the room.
**Ages:** 4–9.
**How:** Aim for 99 links in a month, and talk about how even 99 is nowhere near all of Allah's gifts.

### 9. Gratitude tree

**What it is:** A paper or real branch with leaf-shaped cards naming blessings.
**Ages:** 3–8.
**How:** Add leaves in different colours for different types of blessings: family, body, food, nature and faith.

### 10. My body says Alhamdulillah

**What it is:** Trace your child's outline on large paper, then label each part with what it lets them do: "Eyes, to read Quran," "Feet, to walk to the masjid."
**Ages:** 4–8.
**How:** This links gratitude to the third part of shukr, using blessings well.

### 11. Nature gratitude collage

**What it is:** Collect leaves, pinecones or snow photos on a walk, then make a collage titled "Fabi ayyi ala'i Rabbikuma tukadhdhiban" ("Which of your Lord's favours will you deny?", [Surah Ar-Rahman 55:13](https://quran.com/55/13)).
**Ages:** 4–10.
**How:** Read a few verses of Surah Ar-Rahman first. It's full of blessings children can see and touch.

### 12. Shukr journal

**What it is:** A small notebook for writing or drawing one blessing a day, plus one way they used it well.
**Ages:** 6–12.
**How:** Keep it short (two lines). The habit matters more than the length.

### 13. Thank-you cards

**What it is:** Handmade cards for people who help: teachers, grandparents, the crossing guard or the masjid volunteer.
**Ages:** 3+.
**How:** Older children can add "Jazakallahu khayran" ("May Allah reward you with good") inside.

## Gratitude games and conversations

### 14. The alphabet of blessings

**What it is:** Take turns naming a blessing for each letter of the English or Arabic alphabet.
**Ages:** 5+.
**How:** Perfect for car rides. For Arabic practice, use the letters from our [Arabic alphabet guide](/blog/teach-arabic-alphabet-to-kids).

### 15. The hidden-blessings challenge

**What it is:** Find blessings people usually forget: clean water, electricity, being able to breathe, a working fridge.
**Ages:** 8–12.
**How:** Have a "no complaints" hour and see who can spot the most hidden blessings.

### 16. Gratitude walk

**What it is:** A slow walk where everyone points out three blessings they can see, hear and feel.
**Ages:** 3+.
**How:** Say "SubhanAllah" for beautiful things and "Alhamdulillah" for things we benefit from. It's a lovely way to teach both words.

### 17. Learn names of Allah linked to gratitude

**What it is:** Learn names such as Ar-Razzaq (the Provider), Al-Karim (the Most Generous) and Ash-Shakur (the Most Appreciative).
**Ages:** 5+.
**How:** Our [names of Allah for kids](/blog/names-of-allah-for-kids) guide suggests 20 names and activities. Explain that Allah is Ash-Shakur. He appreciates even the smallest good deed.

### 18. "Look below, not above"

**What it is:** A conversation based on the Prophet's ﷺ advice to look at those who have less than us rather than those who have more, so that we don't belittle Allah's blessings (Sahih Muslim 2963).
**Ages:** 8–12.
**How:** Use it gently when a child compares themselves to a friend with a newer phone or bigger house.

## Gratitude in action

### 19. Thanking people

The Prophet ﷺ said, "He who does not thank the people is not thankful to Allah" (Sunan Abi Dawud 4811, graded sahih). Teach children to thank people by name and with eye contact: "Thank you, Ms. Amina, for helping me." Saying "Jazakallahu khayran" is a beautiful habit to build early. Our guide to [Islamic manners for kids](/blog/islamic-manners-for-kids) covers this and 19 other adab.

### 20. Sharing blessings with others

Sharing is gratitude in action. Allah says, "And proclaim the blessings of your Lord" ([Surah Ad-Duha 93:11](https://quran.com/93/11)). Let children choose a toy to donate, help pack a food bank bag or share their treats. Our list of [sadaqah ideas for kids](/blog/charity-activities-for-kids) has 30 more ways to give, including Ottawa charities.

## Gratitude when things are hard

Gratitude isn't only for good days. The Prophet ﷺ marvelled at the believer's affair: when something good happens, they are grateful, and that is good for them; when hardship comes, they are patient, and that is also good for them (Sahih Muslim 2999).

When a child is sick, disappointed or grieving:

- **Don't rush to "Alhamdulillah."** Name their feeling first: "You're really sad your trip was cancelled."
- **Then gently widen the view.** "What's one good thing we still have today?"
- **Model it yourself.** Children learn the most from how parents speak about their own hard days.

## Common mistakes to avoid

- **Forcing gratitude.** "Say thank you!" in a sharp voice teaches compliance, not shukr. Model it warmly instead.
- **Using guilt.** "Children elsewhere are starving, so finish your food" can make kids anxious. Talk about sharing and helping instead.
- **Making it a one-time lesson.** Gratitude grows from tiny daily repetitions.
- **Forgetting the third part of shukr.** Thanking Allah includes using blessings well, such as caring for toys, not wasting food and helping others.
