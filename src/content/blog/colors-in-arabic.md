Learning colors in Arabic for kids starts with six core words, red, blue, yellow, green, white and black, which follow one neat pattern, then adds colours named after things in nature, like orange (the fruit) and pink (the rose). This guide gives you every common colour with harakat, pronunciation and the boy/girl forms, plus useful phrases, a Quran connection and 12 games that make colours stick for preschoolers and school-age children alike.

> **Key takeaways**
> - Start with the six "core" colours: أَحْمَر (red), أَزْرَق (blue), أَصْفَر (yellow), أَخْضَر (green), أَبْيَض (white), أَسْوَد (black).
> - Core colours change form for feminine words: أَحْمَر becomes حَمْرَاء. Young children can learn the basic form first.
> - Many other colours come from objects: بُرْتُقَالِيّ (orange) from بُرْتُقَال (the fruit), وَرْدِيّ (pink) from وَرْدَة (rose).
> - Colours are perfect for immersion: name them all day, in snacks, clothes, toys and nature walks.
> - Learn the word for "colour," لَوْن (lawn), and the question مَا لَوْنُهُ؟ (What colour is it?) early.

## Colors in Arabic for kids: the core six

Arabic has a small group of basic colour words that share the same shape, a pattern grammarians call *afʿal*. They all start with أَ and sound alike, which makes them easy to learn as a set.

| Colour | Arabic (masculine) | Say it | Arabic (feminine) | Say it |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Red | أَحْمَر | aḥmar | حَمْرَاء | ḥamrāʾ |
| Blue | أَزْرَق | azraq | زَرْقَاء | zarqāʾ |
| Yellow | أَصْفَر | aṣfar | صَفْرَاء | ṣafrāʾ |
| Green | أَخْضَر | akhḍar | خَضْرَاء | khaḍrāʾ |
| White | أَبْيَض | abyaḍ | بَيْضَاء | bayḍāʾ |
| Black | أَسْوَد | aswad | سَوْدَاء | sawdāʾ |

**Pronunciation tips.** The ح in أَحْمَر is a breathy "h" from the throat, stronger than the English "h." The ص in أَصْفَر and the ض in أَخْضَر and أَبْيَض are "heavy" letters, said with the tongue low and the mouth slightly rounded. The خ in أَخْضَر is like the "ch" in the Scottish "loch." Let your child hear these words from a native speaker or a good app before drilling them.

## More colours in Arabic

Most other colours are made by adding ـِيّ (-ī) to the name of something that has that colour. Children love learning where the word comes from.

| Colour | Arabic (masculine) | Say it | Feminine | Comes from |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Orange | بُرْتُقَالِيّ | burtuqālī | بُرْتُقَالِيَّة | بُرْتُقَال, orange (fruit) |
| Pink | وَرْدِيّ | wardī | وَرْدِيَّة | وَرْدَة, rose |
| Purple | بَنَفْسَجِيّ | banafsajī | بَنَفْسَجِيَّة | بَنَفْسَج, violet (flower) |
| Brown | بُنِّيّ | bunnī | بُنِّيَّة | بُنّ, coffee beans |
| Grey | رَمَادِيّ | ramādī | رَمَادِيَّة | رَمَاد, ash |
| Gold | ذَهَبِيّ | dhahabī | ذَهَبِيَّة | ذَهَب, gold |
| Silver | فِضِّيّ | fiḍḍī | فِضِّيَّة | فِضَّة, silver |
| Sky blue | سَمَاوِيّ | samāwī | سَمَاوِيَّة | سَمَاء, sky |

To make the feminine form, add ة: بُرْتُقَالِيّ becomes بُرْتُقَالِيَّة (burtuqāliyya).

**Light and dark.** Add فَاتِح (fātiḥ, light) or غَامِق (ghāmiq, dark) after a colour: أَزْرَق فَاتِح (light blue), أَخْضَر غَامِق (dark green).

**A note on dialects.** Spoken Arabic varies by country, and you may hear different everyday words for some colours, especially pink, purple and orange. For example, many speakers in the Levant say زَهْرِيّ (zahrī) for pink. The words in these tables are standard Arabic (fusha), the form used in children's books and classrooms. Our guide to [fusha vs dialect](/blog/fusha-vs-dialect-for-kids) explains how to handle both at home.

## Useful colour phrases for kids

| English | Arabic | Say it |
|---|---|---|
| Colour | لَوْن | lawn |
| Colours | أَلْوَان | alwān |
| What colour is it? | مَا لَوْنُهُ؟ | mā lawnuhu? |
| It's red. | أَحْمَرُ. | aḥmar. |
| My favourite colour is blue. | لَوْنِي الْمُفَضَّلُ أَزْرَقُ. | lawnī al-mufaḍḍal azraq. |
| A red pen | قَلَمٌ أَحْمَرُ | qalamun aḥmar |
| A blue car | سَيَّارَةٌ زَرْقَاءُ | sayyāratun zarqāʾ |
| The sky is blue. | السَّمَاءُ زَرْقَاءُ. | as-samāʾu zarqāʾ. |
| The grass is green. | الْعُشْبُ أَخْضَرُ. | al-ʿushbu akhḍar. |
| The banana is yellow. | الْمَوْزُ أَصْفَرُ. | al-mawzu aṣfar. |
| The apple is red. | التُّفَّاحَةُ حَمْرَاءُ. | at-tuffāḥatu ḥamrāʾ. |
| Rainbow | قَوْسُ قُزَح | qawsu quzaḥ |

Notice two patterns your child will absorb naturally over time. First, in Arabic the colour comes **after** the thing it describes: قَلَمٌ أَحْمَرُ is literally "pen red." Second, feminine words such as سَيَّارَة (car), سَمَاء (sky) and تُفَّاحَة (apple) take the feminine colour: زَرْقَاء, not أَزْرَق.

## Should young children learn both forms?

Not at first. For ages 3 to 5, teach one form of each colour, usually the masculine form, and use it in short phrases. Children in Arabic-rich environments start hearing حَمْرَاء and زَرْقَاء naturally, and they pick up the pattern by ear long before anyone explains it.

From about age 7 or 8, once children read and write Arabic, you can show the masculine and feminine columns side by side and turn agreement into a game: hold up a noun card, and your child chooses the right colour card.

| Age | Realistic goal | Best activities |
|---|---|---|
| 2–3 | Recognize and point to 3–4 colours when named | Colour sorting, songs, naming toys |
| 4–5 | Name the six core colours plus orange, pink, brown | I spy, colour of the day, painting |
| 6–7 | Use colours in phrases: قَلَمٌ أَحْمَرُ | Describing pictures, simple bingo |
| 8+ | Use feminine forms; read and write colour words | Matching games, writing short descriptions |

## Colours and the Quran

Colours are a lovely way to connect Arabic to the Quran. Allah describes the variety of human languages and colours as one of His signs:

وَمِنْ ءَايَـٰتِهِۦ خَلْقُ ٱلسَّمَـٰوَٰتِ وَٱلْأَرْضِ وَٱخْتِلَـٰفُ أَلْسِنَتِكُمْ وَأَلْوَٰنِكُمْ ۚ إِنَّ فِى ذَٰلِكَ لَـَٔايَـٰتٍ لِّلْعَـٰلِمِينَ

In simple words for children: the heavens and the earth, and all the different languages and skin colours of people, are signs of Allah, for those who think and learn ([Surah Ar-Rum 30:22](https://quran.com/30/22)). Point out the word أَلْوَٰنِكُمْ (your colours), which comes from the same root as لَوْن.

Children who know the story of the cow in Surah Al-Baqarah will enjoy spotting a colour word they know: the cow is described as بَقَرَةٌ صَفْرَآءُ, "a yellow cow" ([Surah Al-Baqarah 2:69](https://quran.com/2/69)). Notice that بَقَرَة is feminine, so the colour is صَفْرَاء, not أَصْفَر.

## 12 games for learning colours in Arabic

1. **Colour of the day.** Pick one colour each morning. Everyone hunts for it all day and names it in Arabic.
2. **I spy in Arabic.** Say أَرَى شَيْئًا لَوْنُهُ أَحْمَرُ (arā shayʾan lawnuhu aḥmar, "I see something whose colour is red"). Your child guesses.
3. **Touch something blue.** A Simon Says variation: اِلْمِسْ شَيْئًا أَزْرَقَ (ilmis shayʾan azraq, "touch something blue"). Switch colours fast.
4. **Pom-pom sort.** Sort pom-poms, blocks or buttons into cups labelled with Arabic colour words. Great for fine motor skills too.
5. **Paint mixing.** Mix أَحْمَر and أَصْفَر to make بُرْتُقَالِيّ, أَزْرَق and أَصْفَر to make أَخْضَر, أَحْمَر and أَزْرَق to make بَنَفْسَجِيّ. Children say each colour as it appears.
6. **Rainbow snack.** Arrange fruit and vegetables in rainbow order and name each colour before eating. Strawberries, oranges, bananas, cucumbers, blueberries and grapes work well (and pair nicely with our [halal lunch ideas for kids](/blog/halal-lunch-ideas-for-kids)).
7. **Traffic light.** Red means stop, green means go: call out أَحْمَر or أَخْضَر while children walk or ride around the room or yard.
8. **Colour bingo.** Make cards with coloured squares; call the colours in Arabic.
9. **Dress-up describe.** At getting-dressed time, name each item's colour: قَمِيصٌ أَزْرَقُ (a blue shirt).
10. **Nature walk.** Ottawa's autumn trees are perfect for this: collect أَحْمَر, بُرْتُقَالِيّ, أَصْفَر and بُنِّيّ leaves, then make a leaf collage. In winter, find everything أَبْيَض.
11. **Colour by number in Arabic.** Combine colours with numbers: a picture where ١ = أَحْمَر, ٢ = أَزْرَق, and so on. Our [Arabic numbers for kids](/blog/arabic-numbers-for-kids) chart has all the digits.
12. **Mystery bag.** Put coloured objects in a bag. Your child pulls one out and names its colour before you count to five.

For more play-based ideas, see our list of [Arabic learning games](/blog/arabic-games-for-kids), and to grow beyond colours into animals, food and family words, our list of [Arabic words for kids](/blog/arabic-vocabulary-for-kids).

## How colours are learned in an immersion classroom

Colours are one of the first topics young children master in a second language, because they are visual, concrete and everywhere. In an immersion classroom, children don't learn colours from a worksheet; they hear them dozens of times a day. "Pass me the أَحْمَر crayon." "Who is wearing أَخْضَر today?" "Let's put the أَزْرَق blocks away."

That repeated, back-and-forth talk is exactly what child development researchers call ["serve and return"](https://developingchild.harvard.edu/science/key-concepts/serve-and-return/): an adult responds to a child's interest with words, the child responds back, and language grows. You can do the same at home in a few minutes a day, even if your own Arabic is limited. Our guide to [teaching Arabic at home](/blog/teach-arabic-at-home) shows how to build a simple daily routine.

## The bottom line

Colors in Arabic for kids are one of the easiest and most rewarding places to start: six core words with one pattern, a handful of colours named after fruit and flowers, and endless chances to practise in everyday life.
