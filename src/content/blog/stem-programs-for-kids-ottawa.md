## What counts as a good STEM program for kids?

A good STEM program for kids is one where children *build, test and explain* rather than watch. Science, technology, engineering and math come alive when a child designs a bridge, codes a robot to turn, or tests why one paper plane flies further than another.

Ontario's elementary [Science and Technology curriculum](https://www.dcp.edu.gov.on.ca/en/curriculum/science-technology) already includes coding and hands-on design, so a strong weekend program reinforces what children meet at school instead of repeating worksheets.

Look for three things:

- **Hands-on time.** Most of the session spent making, not listening.
- **Small groups.** Children get help when their build fails, which is where the real learning happens.
- **A clear progression.** Beginner to intermediate levels, or a new theme each term.

## University and museum STEM programs

### 1. uOttawa Engineering Outreach Saturday clubs

**What it is:** Hands-on science and engineering clubs run by the University of Ottawa's Faculty of Engineering, in a bilingual English and French environment.
**Ages:** Grades 2 to 6.
**When and where:** Saturdays from 9:30 AM to noon or 1:00 to 3:30 PM, in person at the SITE building, 800 King Edward Avenue.
**Cost:** $145 for five in-person sessions.
**2026/27 themes:** Cosmic Explorers (space, September 19 to October 17, 2026), Mini Makers (engineering, October 31 to November 28, 2026), Earth Explorers (environmental science, January 16 to February 13, 2027) and Super Scientists (chemistry, February 27 to March 27, 2027).
**Our take:** One of the best-value structured STEM options in the city, with real university labs and a fresh theme each block. Spots can fill quickly, so register early on the [uOttawa clubs for kids page](https://www.uottawa.ca/faculty-engineering/outreach/clubs-kids).

### 2. uOttawa Engineering Outreach summer camps

**What it is:** Week-long STEM day camps grouped by grade, including EngSci camps and STEMspired Girls, a girls-only camp for grades 2 to 7 with access to university biology and chemistry labs.
**Best for:** Families planning summer 2027 who want a full STEM week.
**Our take:** The girls-only option is worth knowing about for families who prefer it. Dates and fees are posted on the uOttawa Engineering Outreach website. For more seasonal options, see our [March break camps in Ottawa](/blog/march-break-camps-ottawa) roundup.

### 3. Canada Science and Technology Museum

**What it is:** Ottawa's national hands-on science museum at 1867 St. Laurent Blvd, part of Ingenium.
**Ages:** About 3 to 14, with free admission for children 2 and under.
**When:** Tuesday to Sunday, 9 AM to 5 PM; closed Mondays.
**Cost:** Youth (3 to 17) $12.50, adults $19.50, family (up to 6 people, max 2 adults) $51, plus tax. Admission is free daily from 4 to 5 PM ([Ingenium visit info](https://ingenium.ca/scitech/en/visit)).
**Our take:** Perfect for an unstructured STEM afternoon. Weekend mornings are busiest; a Sunday visit after a morning program, timed around Asr, works well for many families.

## Library makerspaces and free STEM options

### 4. Ottawa Public Library Imagine Space (Nepean Centrepointe)

**What it is:** Ottawa's first public makerspace, opened in 2014 by the Ottawa Public Library and the U.S. Embassy, at the Nepean Centrepointe branch, 101 Centrepointe Dr.
**Equipment:** 3D printing and modelling, a 3D scanner, a Cricut cutting machine, sewing and embroidery machines, and a button maker. Using a 3D printer requires signing a user agreement, and staff can give an introduction during opening hours. The laser cutters were listed as temporarily unavailable when we checked.
**Cost:** Free to access; check whether materials or prints have a fee.
**Our take:** A great way for older kids to see a design become a real object. Check the [Imagine Space page](https://biblioottawalibrary.ca/en/imagine-space) for any age requirements and booking rules before you go, and plan to stay with younger children.

### 5. Ottawa Public Library branch STEM programs

**What it is:** Free STEM programs, coding clubs, LEGO and maker sessions that branches run through the year.
**Best for:** Trying STEM without a commitment.
**Our take:** Programs vary by branch and season and some need registration, so check the Ottawa Public Library events calendar for your nearest branch.

### 6. Free coding at home: Scratch and ScratchJr

**What it is:** Free block-based coding tools from MIT and partners. ScratchJr is designed for ages 5 to 7, and Scratch suits roughly 8 and up.
**Our take:** A good test of interest before you pay for a coding class. Keep sessions short and co-play with younger children; our [parental controls guide for Muslim families](/blog/parental-controls-muslim-families) covers screen settings.

## Robotics, coding and competition programs

### 7. Private robotics and coding centres

Ottawa has several private STEM centres offering weekly robotics and coding classes, camps and birthday parties. One example is **STEMquest** in Orléans, which lists robotics programs for ages 5 to 14 and coding for ages 8 to 14 in small groups, plus winter, March break and summer camps ([STEMquest](https://www.stemquest.ca/)).

**Our take:** These centres suit children who want weekly building and programming with a clear progression. Before you register anywhere, ask about group size, instructor background, the robotics kits used, and whether a trial class is available.

### 8. FIRST LEGO League teams

**What it is:** A worldwide robotics program delivered in Canada through [FIRST Robotics Canada](https://www.firstroboticscanada.org/), with FLL Explore for younger children and FLL Challenge for older elementary and middle-school students. Teams build and program LEGO robots and research a real-world problem each season.
**Best for:** Kids who love teamwork and a goal to work towards.
**Our take:** Teams are often formed through schools, community groups and STEM centres. Ask your child's school whether it has a team, or contact FIRST Robotics Canada about teams near you.

## How to choose the right STEM program

| If your child… | Look for… |
|---|---|
| Is 5 to 7 and curious | Museum visits, library programs, ScratchJr, short LEGO sessions |
| Is 8 to 12 and loves building | Saturday clubs, robotics classes, a FIRST LEGO League team |
| Loves coding | A class with a clear progression from block coding to text coding |
| Gets bored easily | Themed blocks that change every five weeks |
| Has a busy weekend already | A combined program, or a monthly museum or library visit |

Questions worth asking any provider:

1. How many children per instructor?
2. How much of each session is hands-on?
3. Is there a trial class or a refund policy?
4. Are staff screened, and is there a sign-in and pick-up procedure?
5. What will my child be able to do by the end of the term?

## Tips for Muslim families

**Plan around salah.** In late fall and winter, Dhuhr and Asr can both fall during a Saturday or Sunday program. Ask whether there is a quiet space to pray, or choose a session that ends before Asr.

**Pack halal snacks.** Many programs include a snack break; packing your own avoids any guesswork.

**Girls-only options exist.** uOttawa's STEMspired Girls summer camp is one example for families who prefer it.

**Connect STEM to Islamic history.** Children love hearing that the word *algorithm* comes from the name of the Muslim mathematician al-Khwarizmi, and that Ibn al-Haytham pioneered the experimental study of light and vision. It helps them see science and faith as partners, not rivals.

**Protect rest.** Two structured programs plus a weekend Islamic school can be too much. If your child is tired, alternate STEM terms with [screen-free weekend activities](/blog/screen-free-weekend-ideas) and family outings from our [things to do with kids in Ottawa](/blog/things-to-do-with-kids-ottawa) guide.
