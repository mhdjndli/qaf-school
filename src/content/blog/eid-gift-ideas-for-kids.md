The best Eid gift ideas for kids combine something fun, something meaningful and a little Eid joy: a toy they will actually play with, an Islamic book or prayer item sized for them, and an envelope of Eidi. This guide sorts Eid gifts for kids by age (toddlers, 4–6, 7–10 and 11–15) and by budget, with experience gifts, Eidi tips and an easy DIY gift bag you can put together the night before.

> **Key takeaways**
> - A good Eid gift is permissible, affordable and something the child genuinely likes. Price is the least important of the three.
> - Mix one Islamic gift (prayer mat, book, Quran pen) with one everyday gift (building toy, art kit, sports gear) so Eid feels joyful, not like homework.
> - For toddlers, follow Health Canada's small-parts guidance and skip coins and small loose pieces.
> - Experiences and Eidi can mean more to older kids than another object. Pair Eidi with a simple save-spend-give lesson.
> - Eid al-Fitr 2027 is expected around March 9–10 and Eid al-Adha around May 16, 2027 (depending on moon sighting), so shop early.

## How to choose a good Eid gift

The Prophet (peace be upon him) said, "Give gifts to one another and you will love one another" ([Al-Adab Al-Mufrad 594](https://sunnah.com/adab:594)). Gifts on Eid are a sunnah-rooted way to build love, not a competition.

Three simple tests help when you are choosing:

1. **Step 1: Is it permissible?** Skip items with inappropriate images, music-heavy content you would not allow at home, or games with themes that clash with your family's values.
2. **Step 2: Is it affordable?** A thoughtful $10 gift beats a stressful $100 one. Children remember the feeling more than the price.
3. **Step 3: Will the child actually like it?** Ask parents, or watch what the child reaches for. An "educational" toy that stays in the box teaches nothing.

Add a fourth check for little ones: **is it safe for their age?** Health Canada's [toy safety guidance](https://www.canada.ca/en/health-canada/services/toy-safety) warns that small objects are choking, ingestion and inhalation hazards for young children. Always read the age label.

## Eid gifts for toddlers (ages 1–3)

Toddlers enjoy wrapping paper as much as what is inside. Focus on sturdy, sensory toys and simple Islamic first books.

**Islamic gifts for toddlers**

- **Board books** with simple duas, colours or the five pillars. *Golden Domes and Silver Lanterns* by Hena Khan (Chronicle Books) is a colour-concept book many families love. See more in our list of the [best Islamic books for kids](/blog/best-islamic-books-for-kids).
- **Soft plush toys** with Islamic themes, such as a plush crescent moon, lantern or mosque.
- **A small prayer mat** so they can "pray" next to you. At this age, copying is the lesson.
- **Recorded nasheeds or surah audio** for calm time, with the device kept by parents.

**Everyday gifts for toddlers**

- Chunky blocks, shape sorters, stacking cups and large-piece puzzles.
- Push-and-pull toys, ride-ons and balls for active play.
- Washable crayons and large paper for first scribbles.

> **Safety tip:** Give toddlers' Eidi to their parents in an envelope, or as a deposit into a savings account. Coins are a common choking hazard, and Health Canada's [Play Time safety page](https://www.canada.ca/en/health-canada/services/consumer-product-safety/reports-publications/consumer-education/your-child-safe/play-time.html) lists them among household items to keep away from children under three.

## Eid gifts for kids ages 4–6

Four- to six-year-olds are building early literacy, fine motor skills and a strong sense of "I can do it myself." They love gifts that let them make, build and pretend.

**Islamic gifts for ages 4–6**

- **A child-size prayer mat** with their name on it, or a matching prayer outfit, kufi or prayer dress for Eid salah.
- **Arabic alphabet puzzles, magnets or flashcards.** Kids who are learning their letters, as children do in daily Arabic at QAF, love seeing them at home. Our guide on how to [teach the Arabic alphabet to kids](/blog/teach-arabic-alphabet-to-kids) has ideas for using them.
- **Picture books about Ramadan, Eid and the prophets.** *Night of the Moon* by Hena Khan (Chronicle Books) is a classic story about a young Muslim girl's Ramadan and Eid.
- **A "my first" Islamic set:** small tasbih, a dua card for bedtime, and a little bag to keep them in.

**Everyday gifts for ages 4–6**

- Building toys such as LEGO Duplo or magnetic tiles.
- Play dough, kinetic sand, and craft kits with large pieces.
- Dress-up clothes, play kitchens and doctor kits for pretend play.
- A balance bike, scooter or skipping rope for spring.

## Eid gifts for kids ages 7–10

School-age children want gifts that feel a little more grown up. This is a great age for skill-building gifts and first "real" Islamic items they can own and look after.

**Islamic gifts for ages 7–10**

- **A Quran reading pen** (a talking pen that recites when it touches the page). It helps with pronunciation and independent review.
- **A kids' dua book or Islamic journal** where they can write goals, duas and favourite verses. Pair it with our [daily duas for kids](/blog/daily-duas-for-kids) list.
- **A digital tasbih counter** for dhikr after salah. Children who are learning salah love having their own.
- **Chapter books with Muslim characters** that reflect their own lives.
- **Prophet stories or Islamic history** in illustrated form.

**Everyday gifts for ages 7–10**

- Science and engineering kits, such as circuits, crystals or simple robotics.
- Family board games and card games. Check the age on the box.
- Sports gear: a soccer ball, skates, a helmet or a basketball.
- Art supplies: sketchbooks, markers, watercolours or a beginner's calligraphy set.

## Eid gifts for tweens and teens (ages 11–15)

Older kids often care less about toys and more about independence, hobbies and time with friends. Ask what they want, or give them a choice of two.

**Islamic gifts for ages 11–15**

- **A personal mushaf** with a protective cover, or a colour-coded tajweed Quran.
- **A good Islamic history or seerah book** written for young readers.
- **A quality prayer mat and travel prayer kit** for school, sports trips and outings.
- **Enrolment in a Quran or Arabic program.** For ages 8–15 in Ottawa, a term at [QAF Sunday Camp](/sunday-camp) (Quran, Arabic, STEM and soccer on Sundays) is a gift that lasts all year.

**Everyday gifts for ages 11–15**

- Hobby gear: art supplies, a sewing kit, a musical instrument they already play, or a camera.
- Sports equipment or a season of a sport they love.
- Books from their favourite series, or a bookstore gift card.
- Practical tech accessories, chosen with your family's screen-time rules in mind. Our guide to [parental controls for Muslim families](/blog/parental-controls-muslim-families) can help.

## Experience gifts kids remember

An experience can be the most memorable Eid gift for kids of any age. It also cuts down on clutter.

- **A family day out:** a museum, a science centre, a farm, a trampoline park or an indoor play centre.
- **A class or program:** swimming, martial arts, art, coding or a Quran class.
- **A special Eid meal** at a halal restaurant the child chooses.
- **A "yes day" coupon book:** "breakfast for dinner," "stay up 30 minutes late," "pick the family movie."
- **One-on-one time** with a parent, grandparent or uncle, doing something just for them.

In Ottawa, Eid al-Fitr 2027 falls in early March, when winter activities are winding down, while Eid al-Adha in mid-May is perfect for outdoor trips. Our guide to [things to do with kids in Ottawa](/blog/things-to-do-with-kids-ottawa) lists museums, parks and indoor play options by season.

## Eidi: the tradition of Eid money

Eidi (also called eidiya) is the custom of older relatives giving money to children on Eid, often in decorated envelopes after Eid prayer. It is a beloved cultural tradition rather than a religious obligation, and it fits the Islamic value of generosity and joy on Eid. The Prophet (peace be upon him) told Abu Bakr on an Eid day, "Every nation has its Eid, and this is our Eid" ([Sahih al-Bukhari 952](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:952)).

Families handle Eidi in different ways. Some give equal amounts to every child, some scale by age, and some give a small amount plus a toy.

**Turn Eidi into a money lesson**

1. **Step 1: Prepare three jars or envelopes** labelled Save, Spend and Give.
2. **Step 2: Let your child divide their Eidi** with your guidance. A simple split such as half save, some spend and some give works well.
3. **Step 3: Follow through on "give"** by choosing a charity together, or adding it to the family sadaqah box.
4. **Step 4: Let them spend "spend" freely** on something they choose. Making small choices is how kids learn.

If relatives live far away, many families now send Eidi by e-transfer to the parents. A small printed or handwritten card for the child keeps the moment special.

## DIY Eid gift bags for kids

Eid gift bags are perfect for cousins, classmates and kids at the mosque. You can make a dozen in an evening.

1. **Step 1: Choose your bags.** Plain paper bags, small boxes or fabric pouches all work. Let your kids decorate them with stars, lanterns and "Eid Mubarak."
2. **Step 2: Add one small treat.** Choose halal candy (check for gelatin) or dates. If bags are going to a school or mosque group, choose nut-free treats.
3. **Step 3: Add one small activity.** Stickers, a mini colouring book, crayons, bubbles or a small puzzle.
4. **Step 4: Add one meaningful item.** A dua bookmark, a mini tasbih for older kids, or a printed card with a short surah.
5. **Step 5: Add a personal note.** A short "Eid Mubarak, we love you" in your child's handwriting makes it special.

> **Tip:** Make two versions: one for under-threes (no small parts, no hard candy) and one for older kids. Label them so they do not get mixed up on Eid morning.

## Gifts that give back

Eid is a time to remember children who do not receive gifts. Some families give each child a "gift that gives," such as:

- A donation in the child's name to an orphan sponsorship or children's charity.
- A shared shopping trip to buy a gift for a child in need through a mosque or charity Eid drive.
- A family visit to bring treats to elderly neighbours.

These ideas fit naturally after the charity habits children build during the month. Our [Ramadan activities for kids](/blog/ramadan-activities-for-kids) guide has a sadaqah box and family charity vote that lead straight into Eid giving.
