The best Laylatul Qadr activities for kids are short, special and repeated across the last ten nights: learning Surah Al-Qadr, saying one beautiful dua, praying two rak’ahs together, and giving a little charity every night. Children do not need to stay up until dawn to share in the Night of Decree. Below is a simple explanation for kids, the expected 2027 dates, 15 Laylatul Qadr activities for kids sorted by age, and a ten-night family plan.

> **Key takeaways**
> - Laylatul Qadr (the Night of Decree, or Night of Power) is the night the Quran was sent down, and Allah calls it “better than a thousand months” (Surah Al-Qadr 97:3).
> - The Prophet (peace be upon him) told us to seek it in the odd nights of the last ten nights of Ramadan. In 2027, the first of these is expected on the evening of Saturday, February 27 (depending on moon sighting).
> - Spread small activities across all ten nights rather than one exhausting late night.
> - The key dua for this night is the one the Prophet taught Aisha: “O Allah, You are Pardoning and You love to pardon, so pardon me.”

## What is Laylatul Qadr? A simple explanation for kids

Laylatul Qadr is the most blessed night of the year. It is the night the Quran was sent down, and Allah tells us about it in a whole surah of just five verses, Surah Al-Qadr. That makes it one of the easiest surahs for children to learn and understand.

Here is how you can explain it to a child in one breath: *“There is one night in Ramadan that is better than a thousand months. The angels come down, and the whole night is full of peace until Fajr. We don’t know exactly which night it is, so we try to do something good on all of the last ten nights.”*

### Surah Al-Qadr (97:1–5)

إِنَّآ أَنزَلْنَـٰهُ فِى لَيْلَةِ ٱلْقَدْرِ ۝ وَمَآ أَدْرَىٰكَ مَا لَيْلَةُ ٱلْقَدْرِ ۝ لَيْلَةُ ٱلْقَدْرِ خَيْرٌ مِّنْ أَلْفِ شَهْرٍ ۝ تَنَزَّلُ ٱلْمَلَـٰٓئِكَةُ وَٱلرُّوحُ فِيهَا بِإِذْنِ رَبِّهِم مِّن كُلِّ أَمْرٍ ۝ سَلَـٰمٌ هِىَ حَتَّىٰ مَطْلَعِ ٱلْفَجْرِ

| Verse | Transliteration | Meaning (adapted from Saheeh International) | Kid-friendly idea |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Innā anzalnāhu fī laylatil-qadr | Indeed, We sent it [the Quran] down during the Night of Decree. | The Quran came on this night. |
| 2 | Wa mā adrāka mā laylatul-qadr | And what can make you know what is the Night of Decree? | It is so special, Allah asks us to think about it. |
| 3 | Laylatul-qadri khayrum-min alfi shahr | The Night of Decree is better than a thousand months. | One night is better than 83 years! |
| 4 | Tanazzalul-malā’ikatu war-rūḥu fīhā bi-idhni rabbihim min kulli amr | The angels and the Spirit [Jibreel] descend therein by permission of their Lord for every matter. | Angels fill the earth. |
| 5 | Salāmun hiya ḥattā maṭla‘il-fajr | Peace it is until the emergence of dawn. | Peace all night, until Fajr. |

You can listen to the surah verse by verse on [Quran.com](https://quran.com/97), which is a good way for children to hear correct pronunciation before they repeat.

## When is Laylatul Qadr 2027?

No one knows the exact night. The Prophet (peace be upon him) said, “Search for the Night of Qadr in the odd nights of the last ten days of Ramadan” ([Sahih al-Bukhari 2017](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:2017)). Islamic nights begin at sunset, so “the 21st night” is the evening *before* the 21st day of fasting.

Ramadan 2027 is expected to begin on Monday, February 8, based on the North American projection at [moonsighting.com](https://moonsighting.com/ramadan-eid.html). If that holds, the odd nights are:

| Night | Expected evening (2027) | School night? |
|---|---|---|
| 21st | Saturday, February 27 | No |
| 23rd | Monday, March 1 | Yes |
| 25th | Wednesday, March 3 | Yes |
| 27th | Friday, March 5 | No |
| 29th | Sunday, March 7 | Yes |

*All dates are expected, depending on moon sighting. If Ramadan starts a day later in your community, shift every night by one day. Confirm with your mosque in early February.*

Many families pay special attention to the 27th night, and in 2027 it is expected to fall on a Friday, which makes a slightly later family evening easier. Still, try not to put all your effort into one night. The odd nights are all candidates.

## How to explain the Night of Decree by age

| Age | What they can understand | Best activity length |
|---|---|---|
| 3–5 | “A very special night when angels come down and Allah loves to forgive.” | 5–10 minutes before bed |
| 6–8 | The surah’s meaning, why we don’t know the exact night, the dua for forgiveness | 15–30 minutes |
| 9–12 | Why “better than a thousand months” matters, intention, a personal dua list | 30–60 minutes, some later nights |
| 13+ | Qiyam at the mosque, reading more Quran, planning their own worship | Their own plan, with your support |

## 15 Laylatul Qadr activities for kids

Each activity lists the ages it suits best. Pick four or five and repeat them every night of the last ten.

### 1. Learn Surah Al-Qadr

Teach one verse a night for the first five of the last ten nights, then recite the whole surah together for the remaining five. Our guide to [short surahs for kids](/blog/short-surahs-for-kids) explains a simple repeat-and-echo method. **Best for:** ages 4–10.

### 2. The “1,000 months” maths challenge

Ask your child: how many years are 1,000 months? (1,000 ÷ 12 = about 83 years and 4 months.) Then compare it with the ages of people they know. It makes verse 3 unforgettable. **Best for:** ages 7–12.

### 3. Learn the Laylatul Qadr dua

Aisha (may Allah be pleased with her) asked the Prophet what to say if she knew which night it was. He taught her ([Jami’ at-Tirmidhi 3513](https://sunnah.com/tirmidhi:3513), graded sahih):

اللَّهُمَّ إِنَّكَ عَفُوٌّ كَرِيمٌ تُحِبُّ الْعَفْوَ فَاعْفُ عَنِّي

*Allāhumma innaka ‘afuwwun karīmun tuḥibbul-‘afwa fa‘fu ‘annī*: “O Allah, You are Pardoning and Generous, You love to pardon, so pardon me.” Many people say it without the word *karīm*; both are fine to teach. **Best for:** ages 4+.

### 4. Ten-night lantern countdown

Hang ten paper lanterns or stars. Light (or switch on) one each night. Mark the odd nights with a gold star so children see which nights are “search nights.” **Best for:** ages 3–8.

### 5. Personal dua list

Older children write five things they want to ask Allah for: something for themselves, their family, a friend, the ummah and the akhirah. They keep the list in their prayer mat pocket. **Best for:** ages 7–15.

### 6. Two rak’ahs together

Pray two extra rak’ahs as a family after Isha, with your child beside you. Short and sincere is better than long and tired. **Best for:** ages 6+.

### 7. A coin every night

Set up a jar and add sadaqah each of the ten nights, so that if one of them is Laylatul Qadr, your child has given charity on it. At the end, let your child choose where it goes. **Best for:** ages 3–12.

### 8. “Forgive someone” night

The dua is about Allah’s pardon, so link it to pardoning others. Each child chooses one person to forgive or one apology to make. Siblings usually provide plenty of material. **Best for:** ages 5–12.

### 9. Angel-and-peace quiet hour

Turn off screens, dim the lights and sit together for 20 minutes with soft recitation. Explain verse 5: this night is peace until dawn. **Best for:** ages 4–12.

### 10. Home prayer corner

Set up a small corner with a prayer mat, a mushaf or Juz Amma, a dua card and a lamp. Children can retreat there for a few minutes of their own worship. **Best for:** ages 5–12.

### 11. Quran story night

Tell the story of how the Quran came to the Prophet through the angel Jibreel, and connect it to verse 1. Ramadan is the month the Quran was revealed (Surah Al-Baqarah 2:185). **Best for:** ages 5–10.

### 12. Dhikr bracelet

Thread 33 beads on elastic. Children say *SubhanAllah*, *Alhamdulillah* or *Allahu akbar* on each bead. **Best for:** ages 4–9.

### 13. Last-ten journal

Each night, older kids write one thing they did for Allah, one dua and one thing they are grateful for. **Best for:** ages 8–15.

### 14. Visit the mosque for part of qiyam

Many Ottawa mosques hold extra night prayers during the last ten nights. Take older children for the first part, then come home. Check your mosque’s schedule and whether children are welcome late at night. **Best for:** ages 8+.

### 15. Suhoor together on a weekend night

On the Saturday or Friday nights, wake children for suhoor and a few minutes of dua before Fajr. Combine it with a [first fast for kids](/blog/first-ramadan-fast-for-kids) if your child is ready. **Best for:** ages 7+.

## Balancing late nights, sleep and school

The Prophet (peace be upon him) made the last ten nights different for his whole household. Aisha reported that he “used to pray all the night, and used to keep his family awake for the prayers” ([Sahih al-Bukhari 2024](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:2024)). Waking the family is Sunnah, but young children still need sleep to learn and stay well.

In 2027, three of the five odd nights are expected to fall on school nights. A workable approach:

- **School nights:** 15–20 minutes of activities after Isha, then normal bedtime. Our [Islamic bedtime routine](/blog/islamic-bedtime-routine) adds Surah Al-Qadr and the dua easily.
- **Friday and Saturday nights:** a later bedtime for older children, or waking them for suhoor instead of keeping them up.
- **Teens:** let them plan their own night, including mosque qiyam, with a sensible agreement about getting home and sleeping.

The Prophet also said that whoever stands in prayer on Laylatul Qadr out of faith and hoping for reward will have their past sins forgiven ([Sahih al-Bukhari 1901](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:1901)). For a child, “standing” can be two rak’ahs and a heartfelt dua. Tell them that, so they feel their small effort counts.
