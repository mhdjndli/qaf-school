The Ottawa child care subsidy, officially the City of Ottawa child care fee subsidy, helps families with low and moderate incomes pay for licensed child care for children aged 12 and under. The process has two parts that many parents mix up. First, you register on the **Ottawa Child Care Registry and Waitlist (OCCRW)** and secure a space in a licensed program. Then you apply for subsidy through the City's **Children's Services Online (CSO) portal** and upload your documents. This guide walks through both, step by step, with the documents you need and the mistakes that cause delays.

> **Key takeaways**
> - You must secure a space in a licensed program before the City can assess your subsidy eligibility. For children 0 to 5, the program must be enrolled in the Canada-wide (CWELCC) system.
> - Register for child care on the Ottawa Child Care Registry and Waitlist at onehsn.com/ottawa, then apply for subsidy in the Children's Services Online portal.
> - You must live in Ottawa, have a reason for care (such as work or school), meet the financial test, and be a citizen, permanent resident or hold eligible status.
> - Log into your Registry account every 60 days, or your applications can become inactive.
> - CWELCC already caps fees at $22 a day at enrolled programs. Subsidy can reduce your share further, based on your family's adjusted net income.

## Child care subsidy vs CWELCC: what's the difference?

There are two separate programs that lower child care costs in Ottawa, and you may benefit from both.

| | CWELCC fee reduction | City of Ottawa fee subsidy |
|---|---|---|
| **What it is** | Federal-provincial fee reduction for licensed child care | Income-tested financial help from the City |
| **Who gets it** | All families with children under 6 at enrolled programs | Families who meet the financial need and other criteria |
| **Ages** | Under 6 (with some eligibility to June 30 of the year they turn 6) | 12 and under |
| **How much** | Fees capped at $22/day; about $19/day on average | Depends on adjusted family net income; can lower your cost substantially |
| **Do you apply?** | No, it is applied automatically by the provider | Yes, through the City |
| **Where** | Any CWELCC-enrolled licensed program | Licensed programs; for ages 0 to 5, must be CWELCC-enrolled |

In short, CWELCC lowers the price for everyone at enrolled programs, and subsidy lowers it further for families who qualify. We explain CWELCC and the $10-a-day timeline in [daycare vs preschool vs kindergarten in Ontario](/blog/daycare-vs-preschool-vs-kindergarten-ontario).

## Who is eligible for the Ottawa child care subsidy?

According to the City's subsidy information on the [Child Care Registry and Waitlist](https://onehsn.com/ottawa), you can be eligible if you:

- **Live in the City of Ottawa.**
- **Have a child 12 years old or younger.** Ontario's [child care subsidy page](https://www.ontario.ca/page/child-care-subsidies) notes that children with special needs may be eligible up to age 18 if they meet other criteria.
- **Have a reason for care.** For example, you are employed, self-employed, returning from parental leave, in school or training, receiving Ontario Works or ODSP, or you or your child has a special or social need.
- **Meet the financial need for care**, based on your family's income.
- **Are a Canadian citizen, a permanent resident, or hold eligible immigration, refugee or Indian status.**

If you have a spouse or common-law partner, both parents generally need a reason for care. If one parent is at home without a qualifying reason, ask the subsidy office how that affects your application.

### How much will I pay?

Ontario's subsidy is income-tested. The province says the amount you pay depends on your family's **adjusted net income**, taken from your Canada Revenue Agency tax return (the net income line on your Notice of Assessment). The lower your income, the smaller your share. Some families with low incomes pay nothing.

The City offers an online **fee subsidy eligibility calculator** on ottawa.ca. It gives an estimate only. The calculator itself warns that it does not guarantee eligibility or approval, and the subsidy office makes the final decision on a complete application.

## Step-by-step: how to apply for child care subsidy in Ottawa

### Step 1: Create your Child Care Registry and Waitlist account

Go to the [Ottawa Child Care Registry and Waitlist](https://onehsn.com/ottawa) and register with your email address. Account emails come from no-reply@onehsn.com, so add it to your safe senders and check your junk folder.

Add your child's name, date of birth, and your **preferred start date**. Register as early as you can. In Ottawa, many infant and toddler programs have long waitlists, and families often register during pregnancy.

### Step 2: Choose programs

Select the licensed programs you want. Through the Registry, you can apply to licensed centres, licensed home child care, nursery schools, and before- and after-school programs. The City's registry guide says you can choose up to 9 centre-based programs and 5 home child care options per child; check the current limit when you sign up.

Tips for choosing:

- **If you need subsidy for a child under 6, choose CWELCC-enrolled programs.** Subsidy for ages 0 to 5 requires a space at a CWELCC-enrolled program. Check each provider's Registry profile or ask the provider directly.
- **Mix locations.** Choose programs near home and near work to improve your chances.
- **Consider licensed home child care.** Adding home child care options alongside centres widens your choices.
- **Unlicensed care and recreation programs are not on the Registry**, and they are not eligible for subsidy.

If you need help paying, select the option on the Registry to indicate you need help with fees (subsidy). The City describes this as one application covering both the full-fee registry and the subsidy waitlist.

### Step 3: Log in every 60 days

This is the most common reason families lose their place. The City requires everyone on the Registry and Waitlist to **log in every 60 days** to update their information. If you do not, you will receive a final notice, and your applications can become inactive.

Set a recurring phone reminder for every 50 days. Logging in and confirming your details takes a few minutes.

### Step 4: Accept a space

When a space opens and you are next on a provider's list, the provider contacts you directly. If you are approaching your preferred start date and have not heard anything, it is fine to call or email the programs you selected.

Before accepting, confirm:

- The start date and any gradual entry plan.
- Whether the program is CWELCC-enrolled (for children under 6).
- The daily fee and what it includes.
- That the provider knows you are applying for subsidy.

If you are offered a space but are not ready, ask the provider about postponing. Changing your preferred start date in your account can change your priority on that provider's list.

### Step 5: Apply for subsidy in the Children's Services Online portal

Once you have a confirmed space, apply for subsidy through the City's **Children's Services Online (CSO) portal**. The City's [CSO user guide](https://documents.ottawa.ca/sites/default/files/UG-CSO-Apply_EN.pdf) lays out the steps:

1. Sign in or create a CSO account.
2. Select **"Child Care Fee Subsidy Application"**, read the instructions, then **"Apply for Child Care Fee Subsidy"**.
3. Complete your personal and contact information.
4. Complete **"Reason for Child Care"** and select every reason that applies.
5. If you are married or common-law, complete the same sections for your spouse.
6. List **all children under 18 living in the home**. For the child with a secured space, enter the preferred start date and choose the provider.
7. Review the **Documentation** section, which lists what you will need to upload.
8. Certify your information and select **Submit**.

### Step 6: Upload your documents

After you submit, the portal takes you to a confirmation page. Select **"Upload Files"** to add your documents. The City's checklist states that your application cannot be assessed until all required documents are received, so upload everything as soon as you can.

### Step 7: Wait for assessment and confirmation

The subsidy office reviews your application and may ask for more information. Wait times vary with funding and space availability, and the City prioritizes families most in need. If you are approved, the City will tell you your parent contribution, the amount you pay toward care.

## Documents you need

The City's [fee subsidy document checklist](https://app06.ottawa.ca/online_services/forms/childcare/document_checklist_en.pdf) (June 2025 version) lists the following. Gather them before you apply.

**1. Signed consent form.** The Child Care Subsidy Program Rights and Responsibilities Agreement and Consent to Disclose and Verify Information form.

**2. Proof of identity and status for every family member.** Copies must be current and include both sides. Driver's licences, health cards and SIN cards are not accepted.

- Born in Canada: birth certificate, statement of live birth, Canadian passport, or status card.
- Born outside Canada: Canadian passport, citizenship certificate, PR card, Confirmation of Permanent Residence (IMM5292), Record of Landing (IMM1000), or Protected Person status.

**3. Proof of Ottawa address**, if your Notice of Assessment does not show your current address. Examples are a lease, a recent rent receipt or landlord letter with their name and phone number, a utility bill, or property tax documents. They must show your name and be from the last 90 days.

**4. Custody documents**, if your child's other parent does not live with you, such as a separation agreement or court order.

**5. Income verification for the most recent tax year**, such as each parent's Notice of Assessment, a Canada Child Benefit notice, an Ontario Child Benefit notice, or proof of Ontario Works or ODSP. From July each year, only the previous year's Notice of Assessment is accepted. Tax returns and T4s are not. Newcomers who have not filed taxes yet can provide their Record of Landing or PR card.

**6. Proof of your reason for care:**

- **Employed:** two recent consecutive pay stubs, or a signed employer letter on letterhead with your start date and weekly schedule.
- **Returning from leave:** an employer letter confirming your return date, or your EI claim summary showing the end date.
- **Self-employed:** a declaration of self-employment, your latest T1 General if applicable, and business registration, licence, contract or business plan.
- **In school or training:** registration with your class schedule, or an OSAP assessment showing your student status.
- **Ontario Works or ODSP:** proof that you receive benefits.
- **Special or social need:** the City provides a medical form for your doctor.

The checklist also lists mail and in-person drop-off options if you cannot upload online.

> **Tip for newcomer families:** If you have recently arrived and do not have a Notice of Assessment yet, your Record of Landing or PR card is accepted for income purposes. File your first Canadian tax return as soon as you can. You will need a Notice of Assessment going forward, and filing every year is also required to keep receiving the Canada Child Benefit.

## Before- and after-school care for kindergarten children

If your child is starting JK, you may still need care before 9:00 a.m. and after 3:00 p.m. In Ottawa, many school-based Extended Day Programs are also applied for through the City's Registry. The Ottawa Catholic School Board, for example, says registration for its extended day programs begins with an online application through the City of Ottawa Child Care Registry and Waitlist.

Kindergarten-aged children in licensed before- and after-school programs can benefit from CWELCC, and fee subsidy can apply too. ## Common mistakes that delay subsidy

- **Applying for subsidy before you have a space.** The City cannot assess eligibility until a space is secured.
- **Choosing non-enrolled programs for a child under 6.** Subsidy for ages 0 to 5 requires a CWELCC-enrolled program.
- **Missing the 60-day login.** Set reminders.
- **Uploading the wrong income document.** From July, only last year's Notice of Assessment works. T4s and tax returns are not accepted.
- **Incomplete reason for care for a spouse.** Both parents' situations are assessed.
- **Expired ID or a missing back side.** Copy both sides of current documents.

## What about private and Islamic kindergartens?

Fee subsidy and CWELCC apply only to licensed child care programs. A private school kindergarten, including an Islamic kindergarten, is generally a school rather than a licensed child care program, so tuition is not covered by City subsidy or CWELCC. Some families combine a private kindergarten with subsidized licensed care for a younger sibling.

## Conclusion

Getting an Ottawa child care subsidy is a sequence, not a single form. Register on the Child Care Registry and Waitlist early, choose CWELCC-enrolled programs for children under six, log in every 60 days, accept a space, then apply in the Children's Services Online portal and upload every document on the checklist. With CWELCC capping fees at $22 a day, subsidy can bring your cost down further if you qualify.

Rules and portals change, so check ottawa.ca/childcare before you apply.
