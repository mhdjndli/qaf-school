March Break camps in Ottawa for 2027 will run the week of **March 15 to 19, 2027**, the dates in the Ottawa-Carleton District School Board's approved calendar. The best spots, from City of Ottawa recreation camps to university sports camps, usually open for registration between November and January and fill fast. This guide lists the main camp types, when to register, typical costs, and the questions Muslim families should ask before booking.

> - March Break 2027 is March 15–19 for OCDSB elementary schools, and most Ontario boards use the same week. Check your own board's calendar to confirm.

## March Break 2027 dates in Ottawa

The OCDSB's [2026–2027 elementary calendar](https://www.ocdsb.ca/download/556470) lists March 15–19, 2027 as March Break, followed by Good Friday on March 26 and Easter Monday on March 29. Several other Ontario boards, including Toronto and Halton, have published the same March Break week.

If your child attends the Ottawa Catholic School Board or a French-language board (CEPEO or CECCE), confirm the dates on your board's calendar before you book. Private and Islamic schools set their own calendars, so check with your school too.

For Muslim families, the timing is good news. Ramadan 1448 is expected to begin around February 8, 2027, and Eid al-Fitr is expected around March 9 or 10, 2027, depending on moon sighting. March Break should arrive just after Eid, so children will not be fasting during camp.

## The main March Break camp options

### 1. City of Ottawa recreation camps

**What it is:** Day camps run at community centres across the city, in English and French, from sports and swimming to arts and general recreation.
**Best for:** Families who want a camp close to home at a community-centre price.
**Ages:** In recent years, children and youth aged 4 to 14.
**How to register:** Browse and register online through [register.ottawa.ca](https://register.ottawa.ca). In past years registration opened on a January evening, with in-person registration the next morning. Non-residents pay an extra fee.
**Our take:** These are the camps most parents compete for. Make a short list and set an alarm for registration night.

### 2. OCSB Continuing Education camps

**What it is:** Specialty camps held at Ottawa Catholic School Board high schools, run with outside partners. In 2026 the line-up included multi-sport, fitness, art, volleyball, basketball and a voice-acting camp.
**Best for:** Kids with a clear interest, like basketball or art.
**Ages:** About 4 to 17, depending on the camp.
**Who can attend:** The [OCSB March Break camps page](https://conted.ocsb.ca/elementary/march-break-camps/) says they are open to students from all school boards.
**Our take:** A good choice if you want a specific skill and a full day (most run 9 AM to 4 PM, with extended care at many camps).

### 3. Carleton Ravens March Break camps

**What it is:** Sport and aquatic camps at Carleton University, including multisport, tennis, high-performance conditioning and lifesaving certification courses.
**Best for:** Active kids and teens, and older children working toward lifeguard certifications.
**Ages:** Multisport is for ages 5 to 14, tennis for 7 to 12, and conditioning for 13 to 16.
**How to register:** See [Carleton's March Break camps page](https://athletics.carleton.ca/community/camps-and-childrens-programs/march-break-camps/). In 2026, holiday and March Break registration opened in early November.
**Our take:** Excellent for sporty kids. Because registration opens so early, put it in your calendar now.

### 4. University of Ottawa sports camps

**What it is:** uOttawa runs March Break sports camps through its recreation department, and its camps page says they return in 2027.
**Best for:** Families in central Ottawa, Sandy Hill and the east end.
**Our take:** Watch the uOttawa camps page through the winter for 2027 details and prices.

### 5. YMCA school break camps

**What it is:** The YMCA of the National Capital Region runs [school break camps](https://www.ymcaottawa.ca/programs-services/camps/school-break-camps) for March Break and PA days, with games, friendship and outdoor exploration.
**Ages:** 4 to 12.
**Our take:** A dependable, general day camp. Ask which location runs the camp and whether it includes a swim.

### 6. BGC Ottawa March Break day camps

**What it is:** Multi-activity day camps at BGC Ottawa clubhouses. In 2026, the listed sites were the Tomlinson Family Foundation Clubhouse at 1463 Prince of Wales Drive and the Don McGahan Clubhouse at 430 McArthur Avenue.
**Ages:** 6 to 12.
**Cost:** The 2026 session was listed from about $170 for the week.
**Our take:** One of the more affordable full-week options, with special needs support available.

### 7. Museums, libraries and day trips

Not every family needs a camp. Ottawa's national museums, library branches and parks make an easy week of outings, especially if one parent is home. The [Ottawa Public Library](https://biblioottawalibrary.ca/en) posts March Break programs in its events calendar. Our guide to [things to do with kids in Ottawa](/blog/things-to-do-with-kids-ottawa) covers free museum hours and winter outings, and our list of [screen-free weekend ideas](/blog/screen-free-weekend-ideas) works just as well for a week at home.

### 8. Masjid and Muslim-run programs

We could not confirm any Muslim-run March Break camps for 2027 at the time of writing. Ottawa masjids are more likely to run summer camps, youth nights and one-off events during school breaks. Some, like the Kanata Muslim Association, run youth programs year-round, and Masjid Bilal in Orléans announced a winter-break Fajr challenge for December 2026.

Call your local mosque in January and ask. Our guide to [Muslim youth programs in Ottawa](/blog/muslim-youth-programs-ottawa) lists who to contact by area, and [Muslim Link](https://muslimlink.ca) posts community events.

## How much do March Break camps cost in Ottawa?

March Break camp prices in Ottawa vary widely by provider and activity. As a reference point, BGC Ottawa's 2026 March Break day camp was listed from about $170 for the week. Community and nonprofit camps tend to be the most affordable, while specialty sports, tech and certification camps usually cost more.

Before you compare prices, check what is included:

- **Extended care:** Many camps charge extra for before-care and after-care. If you work 8 to 5, that can add a lot to the week.
- **Swimming and field trips:** Some camps include them; others add a fee.
- **Sibling discounts:** Common at community and masjid programs.
- **Financial help:** Ask the City and nonprofit providers whether fee assistance is available for your family before you rule a camp out.

## Muslim-friendly camp checklist

A camp can be excellent and still need a few adjustments for a Muslim child. These are the questions we suggest asking before you register.

### Prayer time

Clocks spring forward on March 14, 2027, so during March Break Dhuhr falls around 1:15 PM in Ottawa. A full-day camp will include Dhuhr, and a late pick-up may include Asr. Ask: "Is there a quiet spot where my child can pray for five minutes after lunch?" Most camps say yes when asked politely and in advance. Pack a small travel mat.

### Food and snacks

Most day camps ask families to pack lunch, which makes halal easy. Ask about **pizza days, treat days and field-trip food**, which are where gelatin candies and non-halal meat tend to appear. Send a halal alternative so your child does not feel left out. Our [halal lunch ideas for kids](/blog/halal-lunch-ideas-for-kids) can help.

### Swimming and clothing

If the camp includes a daily swim, ask whether your child can wear a modest swimsuit or rash guard and leggings, and how changing rooms are supervised. For older girls, ask whether swims are mixed. If your child will skip the swim, ask what they will do instead.

### Field trips and activities

Ask for the week's schedule. Most activities are fine, but it is easier to plan ahead for anything you want your child to sit out, such as a movie screening you have not checked.

### Supervision and staff

Ask about the counsellor-to-camper ratio, staff training (first aid, for example), and how the camp handles pick-up authorization. These questions matter more than the theme of the week.

## Building an Islamic touch into March Break

March Break is also a chance to keep Quran and Arabic moving after Ramadan. A few simple ideas:

1. **Pick one surah for the week.** Review a short surah each morning before camp, and recite it together at Maghrib.
2. **Visit the masjid for Fajr or a youth night.** Teens love having a "grown-up" outing with a parent.
3. **Book a family outing on the free days.** If camp is only three or four days, keep one day for a museum and a halal lunch.
4. **Keep the weekly class going.** If your child attends one of the [weekend Islamic schools in Ottawa](/blog/weekend-islamic-schools-ottawa), ask whether it runs on the March Break weekends so you can plan around it.
