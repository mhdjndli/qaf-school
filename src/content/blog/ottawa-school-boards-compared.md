There are four publicly funded Ottawa school boards: the Ottawa-Carleton District School Board (OCDSB), the Ottawa Catholic School Board (OCSB), the Conseil des écoles publiques de l'Est de l'Ontario (CEPEO) and the Conseil des écoles catholiques du Centre-Est (CECCE). Two are English-language and two are French-language; two are secular and two are Catholic. All four offer free full-day kindergarten, and private schools are a fifth path. Here is how Ottawa school boards compare on eligibility, kindergarten, French, busing and size, so you can work out which ones your child can attend and which fits your family.

> **Key takeaways**
> - Any Ottawa resident can register at the OCDSB. The OCSB says families of all faiths are welcome.
> - The French-language boards (CEPEO and CECCE) automatically admit children of French-language rights holders. Other families can apply through an admission committee.
> - Both English boards run a 50/50 English-French kindergarten. The French boards teach kindergarten in French.
> - OCDSB and OCSB buses are run by the Ottawa Student Transportation Authority (OSTA). JK/SK children generally qualify at 0.8 km or more from their designated school.
> - Your address decides your designated school in each board. Use each board's school locator before you register.

All four boards follow the Ontario curriculum, including [The Kindergarten Program](/blog/ontario-kindergarten-program-explained), and hire teachers certified by the Ontario College of Teachers. The real differences are language, faith, and who is eligible.

## OCDSB: Ottawa's English public board

The Ottawa-Carleton District School Board is the largest board in Ottawa. It describes itself as serving about 77,000 students in close to 150 schools across the city.

**Who can attend:** Any child who lives in Ottawa can attend the OCDSB school designated for their address. There is no faith or language requirement.

**Kindergarten:** The OCDSB runs a two-year, full-day kindergarten in which all classes receive 50% of instruction in English and 50% in French. Children must turn four by December 31 of the school year to start JK.

**Grade 1 and up:** From Grade 1, families choose between two pathways:

- **English with Core French:** All subjects are in English, with four one-hour Core French blocks a week.
- **Early French Immersion (EFI):** 60% of instruction in French in Grades 1 to 6 and 50% in Grades 7 and 8. From September 2026, children can enter EFI at the start of Grade 1, 2 or 3 at their designated school.

The OCDSB is phasing out its Middle French Immersion program, which starts in Grade 4; the final intake is September 2028. Details are in the board's [January 2026 parent update](https://www.ocdsb.ca/about-us/connect-with-ocdsb/parent-caregiver-updates/post/january-19-2026-middle-french-immersion-mfi-phase-out-early-french-immersion-efi-entry-points). We compare the two pathways in depth in [French Immersion vs English in Ottawa](/blog/french-immersion-vs-english-ottawa).

**Faith:** The OCDSB is secular. It publishes religious accommodation guidelines, and families can request Eid absences, prayer space for older students, and similar accommodation.

## OCSB: Ottawa's English Catholic board

The Ottawa Catholic School Board is publicly funded, free, and follows the Ontario curriculum with Catholic religious education included.

**Who can attend:** In its [kindergarten registration article](https://www.ocsb.ca/?p=58182), the OCSB answers "Do we have to be Catholic?" with a clear "No," and says its schools welcome children and families of all faiths and backgrounds. Expect prayer, religion classes and Catholic celebrations to be part of school life, and ask your local school how it supports Muslim students.

**Kindergarten:** OCSB kindergarten is full-day and bilingual, about 50% French and 50% English. Children do not need French at home. The age rule is the same: four by December 31.

**Grade 1 and up:** According to the [OCSB French programs page](https://www.ocsb.ca/french/), all students take Extended French (25% French) in Grades 1 to 3. In Grade 4, families choose Core French (about 15% French) or French Immersion (50% French). The OCSB offers these pathways in all its schools.

**Busing:** When you register, the OCSB sends your details to OSTA automatically, so you do not sign up for the yellow bus separately.

## CEPEO: Ottawa's French public board

The Conseil des écoles publiques de l'Est de l'Ontario runs secular, French-language public schools in Ottawa and eastern Ontario. Everything, including kindergarten, is taught in French. This is not immersion: it is first-language French education for francophone and eligible families.

**Kindergarten:** In French-language boards, JK is called *maternelle* and SK is *jardin*. CEPEO's admission policy requires a child to be four by December 31 for *maternelle*.

**Who can attend:** This is where French boards differ most from English ones. See the eligibility section below.

## CECCE: Ottawa's French Catholic board

The Conseil des écoles catholiques du Centre-Est is a large French-language Catholic board with more than 32,000 students and over 60 schools and centres across eastern Ontario, including Ottawa.

**Who can attend:** CECCE's [registration page](https://www.ecolecatholique.ca/inscription) says its schools welcome children of all faiths while respecting their Catholic and francophone character. It asks families registering for *maternelle* to Grade 6 for a baptismal certificate for the child or a parent, and says to tell the school at registration if you do not have one. Language eligibility follows the same rights-holder and committee rules as CEPEO.

**Kindergarten:** Full-day *maternelle* (age 4) and *jardin* (age 5), taught in French. CECCE accepts registrations year-round, but some schools have waitlists, so register early.

## Who is eligible for French-language schools?

French-language school boards exist to serve the francophone minority protected by section 23 of the Canadian Charter of Rights and Freedoms. According to [Ontario's French-language education page](https://www.ontario.ca/page/french-language-education), a parent or guardian is a **French-language rights holder** if they live in Ontario, are a Canadian citizen, and meet at least one of these:

- French is the first language they learned and still understand.
- They received their elementary education in a French-language school in Canada.
- They have a child who attends or attended a French-language elementary or secondary school in Canada.

Children of rights holders are admitted automatically.

### The admission committee route

If you are not a rights holder, your child can still be admitted. Each French board has an **admission committee** that reviews applications under section 293 of Ontario's Education Act. Committees typically look at the child's French, the family's commitment to French-language schooling, and the support available at home. CEPEO's policy, for example, has both a standard and an accelerated committee process, and a committee decision for one CEPEO school applies to all CEPEO schools.

### Why this matters for many Muslim families

Ottawa has many Muslim families from French-speaking countries, such as Morocco, Algeria, Tunisia, Lebanon, Senegal and others. Some parents grew up speaking French, but are not yet Canadian citizens, or learned French as a second language. In those cases, the committee route is usually the path. Ontario's policy treats French-speaking newcomers as a priority group for access to French-language schools, and the province notes that French-language schools also welcome newcomers who speak neither English nor French.

If French is a real part of your family life, contact the school directly. Admission is not guaranteed, but it is often possible.

## Kindergarten across the four boards

Whichever board you choose, Ontario kindergarten is free, full-day, and two years long, for children who turn four (JK) or five (SK) by December 31. Classes of more than 16 children are taught by a teacher and an early childhood educator.

The key difference is language:

- **OCDSB and OCSB:** Half English, half French. A child with no French at home will be fine; the program is designed for that.
- **CEPEO and CECCE:** Fully French. A child with little French can still thrive if admitted, but the adjustment is bigger.

## Busing and transportation

The OCDSB and OCSB share the **Ottawa Student Transportation Authority (OSTA)**. According to the [OCDSB transportation page](https://www.ocdsb.ca/our-schools/transportation), OSTA's distance thresholds from your home to your designated school are:

- **JK and SK:** 0.8 km or more
- **Grades 1 to 8:** 1.6 km or more
- **Grades 9 to 12:** 3.2 km or more

Students who attend a school outside their attendance boundary usually have to arrange their own transportation. The OCDSB notes that OSTA's empty seat program is suspended for 2026–27. Check your address on OSTA's website (ottawaschoolbus.ca) and use its Bus Planner once you are registered.

CEPEO and CECCE run transportation through French-language consortia rather than OSTA. Check each board's transportation page for eligibility at your address.

## How to find your designated school

Each board draws its own boundaries, so the same address has a different designated school in each board. Before you register:

**Step 1:** Look up your address in each board's tool: the [OCDSB school locator](https://www.ocdsb.ca/our-schools/school-locator), the OCSB school locator on ocsb.ca/schools, and the CECCE and CEPEO school finders.

**Step 2:** Note which schools offer the program you want. For the OCDSB, check whether your designated school offers Early French Immersion or whether a different school does.

**Step 3:** Check bus eligibility for each option with OSTA or the French-language consortium.

**Step 4:** Visit, or attend an open house. Boards and schools often hold kindergarten information nights in the winter.

**Step 5:** Register. English boards typically open kindergarten registration in the winter for the following September. French boards accept registrations year-round, but earlier is better.

If you need before- or after-school care, apply separately through the City of Ottawa's child care registry. Our [Ottawa child care subsidy guide](/blog/ottawa-child-care-subsidy-guide) explains how that works.

## The fifth path: private and Islamic schools

Ottawa also has private schools, including Islamic schools, Montessori schools and independent schools. They charge tuition, receive no government funding, and set their own admission rules, calendars and class sizes. Ontario does not inspect private elementary schools or require them to follow the Ontario curriculum, so ask each school how it aligns with the provincial program and who teaches.

Families choose an Islamic school mainly for daily Quran, Arabic and Islamic manners, and an environment where Muslim practice is the norm. Many families combine paths. A common pattern is an Islamic kindergarten such as QAF for JK and SK, then a board school from Grade 1, with weekend Quran and Arabic to keep building.

## Which Ottawa school board is right for you?

There is no single best board. A few honest rules of thumb:

- **You want a secular English school close to home:** the OCDSB.
- **You value a faith-based atmosphere and are comfortable with Catholic religious education:** the OCSB.
- **French is a home or heritage language, or you are a rights holder:** CEPEO or CECCE.
- **You want strong French but your home languages are English and Arabic:** an English board's immersion pathway is usually the more realistic choice.
- **You want Quran, Arabic and Islamic Studies every day:** a private Islamic school, at least for the early years.

## Conclusion

Ottawa's four school boards all offer free, full-day kindergarten and certified teachers, but they differ in language, faith, eligibility and how French is taught. Start with your address, check which boards your child is eligible for, compare the French pathways, and visit before you register.
