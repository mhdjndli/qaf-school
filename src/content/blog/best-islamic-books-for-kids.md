The best Islamic books for kids teach faith through a story your child wants to hear again: a board book about Allah for a toddler, a picture book about Ramadan or hijab for a preschooler, a funny chapter book with a Muslim hero for an eight-year-old. This list covers 23 of the best Islamic books for kids, sorted by age from babies to age 12, with publisher details we checked and tips for reading them aloud.

> **Key takeaways**
> - For ages 0–3, choose short, sturdy board books about Allah, the Quran and everyday duas. Learning Roots and The Islamic Foundation (Kube Publishing) are strong starting points.
> - For ages 3–7, picture books by authors such as Hena Khan build both Islamic knowledge and a proud Muslim identity.
> - For ages 7–12, chapter books with Muslim main characters help children see themselves in stories.
> - Read the same favourites again and again, pause to ask questions, and connect the story to real life at home or at the mosque.

## How we chose these books

An Islamic children's book is a book that teaches Islamic beliefs, worship or values, or shows Muslim family life in a positive and accurate way. We looked for four things:

1. **Accurate content.** Islamic information should be correct and mainstream. Books that teach beliefs should be clear and gentle.
2. **Respectful illustrations.** Many Muslim families prefer books that do not depict prophets, so we left out titles known for doing so. Families' standards on illustrations differ, so flip through any book before you buy it.
3. **Read-aloud quality.** Rhythm, rhyme and a real story keep children listening. A book that only lists facts rarely gets read twice.
4. **Mirrors and windows.** Children need books where they see themselves (mirrors) and books that show other Muslims around the world (windows).

We also confirmed that each title exists with the author and publisher listed. Editions and prices change, so check your bookstore or library for the current format.

## Islamic board books for babies and toddlers (ages 0–3)

Board books for the youngest readers should be short, sturdy and repetitive. The goal is not to "teach" a lesson in one sitting, but to make the names of Allah, the Quran and the masjid familiar and loved.

### 1. Allah Sees You and Me

**What it is:** A lift-the-flap board book by Zaheer Khatri (Learning Roots) that shows children Allah is aware of them wherever they are. It ends with a mirror page.
**Best for:** Babies and toddlers who love flaps and seeing their own face.
**Ages:** 0–4.
**Our take:** A gentle, positive first introduction to the idea that Allah is always with us.

### 2. Allah Knows All About Me

**What it is:** A board book by Yasmin Mussa (Learning Roots, 2015) that introduces the idea that Allah knows everything about us.
**Best for:** Toddlers starting to talk about themselves.
**Ages:** 0–4.
**Our take:** Pairs well with *Allah Sees You and Me* for a short bedtime set.

### 3. The ABC of Allah Loves Me

**What it is:** An alphabet board book from Learning Roots (2019) built around one comforting message: Allah loves me.
**Best for:** Toddlers learning English letters.
**Ages:** 1–4.
**Our take:** A good "two-for-one" book that builds early literacy and faith together.

### 4. My First Book about the Qur'an

**What it is:** A board book by Sara Khan, illustrated by Alison Lodge, published by The Islamic Foundation (Kube Publishing) in 2017. It introduces simple teachings from the Quran.
**Best for:** Families who want a first book specifically about the Quran.
**Ages:** 1–4.
**Our take:** Simple and warm. Read it before your child starts learning short surahs.

### 5. It's Ramadan, Curious George

**What it is:** A short picture book by Hena Khan (Houghton Mifflin Harcourt, 2016) in which Curious George joins his friend Kareem's family for Ramadan and Eid.
**Best for:** Toddlers who already love Curious George.
**Ages:** 2–5.
**Our take:** A fun Ramadan book that also works well to share with non-Muslim friends and classmates.

## Islamic picture books for preschool and kindergarten (ages 3–6)

At this age, children love rhyme, bright pictures and stories about kids like them. These are the books we would put on a kindergarten shelf.

### 6. Golden Domes and Silver Lanterns

**What it is:** A rhyming "book of colours" by Hena Khan, illustrated by Mehrdokht Amini (Chronicle Books). Each colour links to part of Muslim life, such as a blue hijab or a white kufi.
**Best for:** Preschoolers learning colours.
**Ages:** 3–6.
**Our take:** One of the most beautiful Islamic picture books available, and a good gift.

### 7. Crescent Moons and Pointed Minarets

**What it is:** A companion "book of shapes" by Hena Khan and Mehrdokht Amini (Chronicle Books, 2018), with a glossary of Islamic terms.
**Best for:** Three- to five-year-olds learning shapes.
**Ages:** 3–5.
**Our take:** Read it before a mosque visit and play "spot the shape" when you get there.

### 8. Hassan and Aneesa Go to Masjid

**What it is:** A picture book by Yasmeen Rahim, illustrated by Omar Burgess, from The Islamic Foundation. It is part of a series introducing young children to Muslim places and celebrations.
**Best for:** Children about to visit the mosque for the first time.
**Ages:** 3–6.
**Our take:** Practical and reassuring. Other books in the series include *Hassan and Aneesa Celebrate Eid*.

### 9. Ilyas and Duck: Search for Allah

**What it is:** A picture book by Omar S. Khawaja, illustrated by Leo Antolini (2012). Ilyas and his friend Duck ask where Allah is, in one scene after another.
**Best for:** Curious kids who ask big questions.
**Ages:** 3–6.
**Our take:** A child-friendly way into "Where is Allah?" conversations. Later books in the series cover salah, Ramadan and Eid.

### 10. Hilmy the Hippo series

**What it is:** A series of short picture books by Rae Norridge, published by The Islamic Foundation, in which Hilmy learns lessons such as sharing, honesty and gratitude.
**Best for:** Teaching adab (good manners) through simple stories.
**Ages:** 3–6.
**Our take:** Easy to read in five minutes. Choose the title that matches the lesson your child needs this week.

## Ramadan, Eid and identity picture books (ages 4–8)

These picture books are a little longer and work well for children in JK, SK and the early grades. Many are perfect for Ramadan and Eid, and several help children feel proud of hijab and Muslim family life.

### 11. Night of the Moon

**What it is:** A picture book by Hena Khan (Chronicle Books, 2008) about a young girl, Yasmeen, as she watches the moon through Ramadan and celebrates Eid.
**Best for:** Explaining how the moon marks Ramadan.
**Ages:** 4–8.
**Our take:** A classic. Pair it with a moon-tracking chart from our [Ramadan activities for kids](/blog/ramadan-activities-for-kids) guide.

### 12. Ramadan Moon

**What it is:** A picture book by Na'ima B. Robert, illustrated by Shirin Adl (Frances Lincoln Children's Books), that follows the month from the first crescent to Eid.
**Best for:** A poetic, gentle Ramadan read-aloud.
**Ages:** 4–8.
**Our take:** Lovely collage art and a calm tone for bedtime.

### 13. The Gift of Ramadan

**What it is:** A picture book by Rabiah York Lumbard, illustrated by Laura K. Horton (Albert Whitman, 2019). A girl wants to fast for the first time, and her grandmother helps her understand the real gift of Ramadan.
**Best for:** Children excited (or nervous) about their first fast.
**Ages:** 4–8.
**Our take:** Honest about how hard fasting can be, which children appreciate.

### 14. Moon's Ramadan

**What it is:** A picture book written and illustrated by Natasha Khan Kazi (Versify, 2023), in which the moon travels the world and sees how Muslims celebrate Ramadan.
**Best for:** Showing children the global Muslim community.
**Ages:** 4–8.
**Our take:** A strong "windows" book, with traditions from many countries.

### 15. Under My Hijab

**What it is:** A picture book by Hena Khan, illustrated by Aaliya Jaleel (Lee & Low Books, 2019), about a girl and the women in her life who wear hijab in different ways.
**Best for:** Girls who are curious about hijab, and classmates who ask questions.
**Ages:** 4–8.
**Our take:** Positive and everyday. See the publisher's page at [Lee & Low Books](https://www.leeandlow.com/books/under-my-hijab/).

### 16. The Proudest Blue

**What it is:** A picture book by Olympic medallist Ibtihaj Muhammad with S.K. Ali, illustrated by Hatem Aly (Little, Brown, 2019), about Faizah's first day of school, when her older sister wears hijab for the first time.
**Best for:** Talking about confidence, sisters and handling unkind comments.
**Ages:** 4–8.
**Our take:** Powerful, and good for starting conversations about kindness at school.

### 17. Mommy's Khimar

**What it is:** A picture book by Jamilah Thompkins-Bigelow, illustrated by Ebony Glenn (Salaam Reads, 2018), about a girl who wears her mother's khimar and imagines she is a queen, the sun and a superhero.
**Best for:** Joyful, imaginative play and family love.
**Ages:** 4–8.
**Our take:** A favourite for read-alouds because of its warmth and colour.

### 18. Lailah's Lunchbox

**What it is:** A picture book by Reem Faruqi, illustrated by Lea Lyon (Tilbury House, 2015). Lailah is fasting for the first time at a new school and is unsure how to explain it.
**Best for:** Children who will fast or attend school during Ramadan.
**Ages:** 5–8.
**Our take:** Very relatable for Canadian families, since Ramadan often falls during the school year.

## Early readers and chapter books (ages 5–12)

Once children start reading on their own, books with Muslim main characters help them keep reading. Younger children can enjoy many of these as read-alouds.

### 19. Yasmin series

**What it is:** An early-reader series by Saadia Faruqi (Capstone) about Yasmin, a Pakistani-American girl, with titles such as *Meet Yasmin!*, *Yasmin the Builder* and *Yasmin the Explorer*.
**Best for:** New readers in SK to Grade 2.
**Ages:** 5–8, depending on the title.
**Our take:** Short chapters and simple vocabulary make it a confidence-builder.

### 20. My First Quran Storybook

**What it is:** A large illustrated collection of stories from the Quran by Saniyasnain Khan (Goodword Books).
**Best for:** Families who want a "family storybook" to read a little each night.
**Ages:** 5–12 as a read-aloud; independent reading from about 8.
**Our take:** A long-term book. Read one story at a time and talk about the lesson.

### 21. Planet Omar: Accidental Trouble Magnet

**What it is:** The first book in Zanib Mian's funny illustrated Planet Omar series, about a boy with a big imagination who moves to a new school.
**Best for:** Reluctant readers who like humour and doodle-style illustrations.
**Ages:** 7–10.
**Our take:** Gets children laughing while showing everyday Muslim family life.

### 22. Zayd Saleem, Chasing the Dream: Power Forward

**What it is:** A chapter book by Hena Khan (Salaam Reads, 2018) about fourth-grader Zayd, who dreams of playing basketball.
**Best for:** Sporty kids and boys who want a relatable Muslim hero.
**Ages:** 7–10.
**Our take:** Short, fast-paced and part of a series, so a hooked reader has more to read.

### 23. Amina's Voice

**What it is:** A middle-grade novel by Hena Khan (Salaam Reads, 2017) about Amina, a Pakistani-American girl dealing with friendship, family and a hard event in her community.
**Best for:** Readers ready for deeper themes.
**Ages:** 8–12.
**Our take:** A thoughtful novel for older readers, and a good book to read and discuss together.

## Tips for reading Islamic books aloud

The first word revealed in the Quran was a command to read:

اقْرَأْ بِاسْمِ رَبِّكَ الَّذِي خَلَقَ

"Read in the name of your Lord who created" ([Surah Al-Alaq 96:1](https://quran.com/96/1-5)). Reading together is one of the simplest ways to bring that command into family life.

The Canadian Paediatric Society encourages families to [read, speak and sing with children from birth](https://cps.ca/en/media/for-lifelong-health-read-speak-and-sing-to-your-baby-from-birth), in whatever language they are most comfortable with. Its position statement, [Read, Speak, Sing](https://cps.ca/documents/position/read-speak-sing-promoting-literacy), also stresses storytelling and singing alongside books.

1. **Step 1: Start with Bismillah.** Saying "Bismillah" before you open the book turns reading into a small act of worship.
2. **Step 2: Preview the pictures.** Look at the cover and ask, "What do you think this story is about?"
3. **Step 3: Read with expression.** Change your voice for characters, and slow down at the important parts.
4. **Step 4: Pause and ask open questions.** "How do you think she feels?" works better than "What colour is the lantern?"
5. **Step 5: Connect it to real life.** "Remember when we went to the masjid? What did you see?"
6. **Step 6: Re-read favourites.** Repetition builds vocabulary and confidence. If your child asks for the same book every night, that is a good sign.
7. **Step 7: Mix languages.** If you speak Arabic, Urdu, Somali, French or another language at home, retell the story in that language too. Our guide to [raising trilingual kids in Canada](/blog/raising-trilingual-kids-canada) has more ideas.

> **Tip:** Keep a small basket of Islamic books in the place your child reads most, such as next to the bed or the prayer corner. Books that are easy to reach get read.

## Building a home Islamic library in Ottawa

You do not need to buy every book on this list. Many families in Ottawa borrow first and then buy the favourites.

- **Ottawa Public Library:** Search the [Ottawa Public Library](https://biblioottawalibrary.ca/en) catalogue for titles such as *Night of the Moon* or *The Proudest Blue*, and place holds before Ramadan, when demand is highest. Availability changes, so check the catalogue.
- **Mosque libraries and book fairs:** Some local mosques keep children's book corners or hold book sales during Ramadan and Eid.
- **Swap with friends:** Trade books with other families once your child has outgrown them.
- **Ask for books as Eid gifts:** Our [Eid gift ideas for kids](/blog/eid-gift-ideas-for-kids) guide includes book picks by age.
