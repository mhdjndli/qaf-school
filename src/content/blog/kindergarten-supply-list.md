A good kindergarten supply list is shorter than most parents expect. In Ontario, the classroom usually provides crayons, glue, paper and books. What your child needs is the gear that keeps them independent all day: a backpack they can open, a lunch they can manage, indoor shoes, a full change of clothes and weather-ready outdoor wear, all labelled. Here is the complete JK/SK checklist, with nut-free and halal notes for Muslim families.

> **Key takeaways**
> - The essentials are a **backpack, lunch bag, water bottle, indoor shoes, a spare set of clothes and seasonal outdoor gear**.
> - **Label everything** with your child's first and last name, including mitts, lids and water bottles.
> - Choose items your child can **open, close and put on alone**: velcro shoes, easy zippers and containers they have practised with.
> - Most kindergartens are **nut-aware or nut-free**. Islamic schools also expect **halal-only food**, so check snack labels for gelatin and other ingredients.
> - Wait for your school's own list before buying classroom supplies. Many schools already provide them.

## Kindergarten supply list at a glance

| Category | Item | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| **Bags** | Backpack (big enough for a lunch bag and a folder) | Carries everything home and back |
| | Folder or communication bag | Notes, art and forms |
| **Lunch** | Insulated lunch bag and ice pack | Keeps food safe until lunch |
| | Containers your child can open | Independence at lunch |
| | Leak-proof water bottle | Drinking water all day |
| **Clothing** | Indoor shoes (velcro, non-marking soles) | Clean, safe floors and gym |
| | Full change of clothes in a labelled bag | Spills, paint, puddles and accidents |
| **Outdoor** | Seasonal gear: rain jacket, splash pants, snowsuit, boots, mitts, hat | Outdoor play happens most days |
| **Health** | Medication and forms (if needed) | Allergies, asthma and other needs |
| **Comfort** | Small family photo or comfort item (ask first) | Helps with early separation |
| **Labels** | Name labels or a permanent marker | Lost-and-found fills up fast |

## Backpack and bag essentials

### Backpack

Choose a backpack your child can open and close alone. It should fit a lunch bag and a letter-sized folder without squeezing. Skip wheels: they are hard to manage on stairs and do not fit in cubbies. Avoid keychains and dangling toys, which get caught and lost.

**Our take:** A simple, lightweight bag with one big zipper is easier for a four-year-old than one with many small pockets.

### Folder or communication bag

Many classes send home a folder daily with art, notes and forms. A clear, labelled plastic envelope works well. Check it every evening. It is the fastest way to stay in the loop in the first weeks.

## Lunch and snack gear

Full-day kindergarten means your child eats at least twice at school. Most programs, including QAF, ask families to pack **lunch plus two snacks**.

### What to buy

- **Insulated lunch bag** that unzips fully, so your child can see everything.
- **Ice pack** or a frozen drink box. Ottawa Public Health advises [packing an ice pack to keep lunch cold](https://www.ottawapublichealth.ca/en/public-health-topics/food-safety.aspx), since meats, eggs and dairy should stay at 4°C or colder.
- **Containers your child can open.** Practise at home before school starts. If your child cannot open it at your kitchen table, they will not open it at school.
- **A thermos** for warm meals in winter. Fill it with boiling water for a few minutes, empty it, then add hot food.
- **Leak-proof water bottle** with a lid your child can open and close. Water is the best drink.
- **Spoon or fork,** labelled, if your child needs one.

### What to skip

- Hard-to-open packages and anything that needs cutting.
- Single-use cutlery your child will lose.
- Glass containers.

## Nut-free and halal notes

### Nut-free

Ontario's [Sabrina's Law](https://www.ontario.ca/laws/statute/05s07) requires school boards to have anaphylaxis policies, and many schools and kindergarten classes are nut-free. QAF is nut-free. Check labels for peanuts and tree nuts, and for "may contain" warnings if your school asks you to. Sunflower seed butter or a soy-based spread are common swaps for peanut butter, but some schools ask families to avoid look-alike spreads, so check your school's rules.

### Halal

At an Islamic school, all food must be halal, and that applies to snacks as much as lunches. Watch for:

- **Gelatin** in gummies, marshmallows, some yogurts and some candy. Look for halal certification or plant-based gelling agents.
- **Meat and deli slices** from a halal source.
- **Flavourings and additives** that may contain alcohol or animal-derived ingredients, if your family checks for these.

When in doubt, simple whole foods are easiest: fruit, vegetables, cheese, eggs, bread, rice and leftovers you made at home.

## Clothing for the classroom

### Indoor shoes

Most Ontario schools ask children to change into indoor shoes, especially in fall and winter when boots come in wet. Pick velcro closures your child can manage, non-marking soles for the gym and a snug fit. Light-up shoes can be distracting; many teachers prefer plain ones.

### A full change of clothes

Pack a labelled bag with:

- Underwear (two pairs for newly trained children)
- Socks
- Pants or leggings
- Top
- A plastic bag for wet or messy clothes

Kindergarten is messy. Paint, water play, puddles and the occasional toilet accident are all part of it. Swap sizes when the seasons change.

### Everyday clothes

Dress for play: elastic waistbands, pull-on pants and tops your child can manage alone. Avoid belts, overalls and tricky buttons. The province notes that clothes children can [take off and put back on by themselves](https://www.ontario.ca/page/preparing-kindergarten) make toileting easier. For girls who wear hijab, a pull-on sports-style hijab is easier and safer for active play than one fastened with pins.

## Outdoor gear by season

Ontario kindergartens go outside most days, in most weather. Your child needs to be ready for the weather at drop-off, not the weather at noon.

| Season | What to send |
|---|---|
| **September–October** | Light jacket, hat for sun, refillable water bottle |
| **Late October–November** | Rain jacket, splash pants, rain boots, warmer hat and mitts |
| **December–March** | Snowsuit or snow pants and winter coat, waterproof boots, two pairs of mitts, warm hat, neck warmer |
| **April–June** | Rain gear, light layers, sun hat, sunscreen applied at home |

For cold days, the Canadian Paediatric Society recommends [mittens instead of gloves and a neck warmer instead of a scarf](https://caringforkids.cps.ca/handouts/safety-and-injury-prevention/winter_safety). In Ottawa, winter is the big one. ## Health and safety items

- **Medication:** If your child has an epinephrine auto-injector, inhaler or other medication, your school will need a completed form and instructions. Ask about this before the first day.
- **Allergy and medical information:** Share it in writing with the teacher and office.
- **Sunscreen:** Apply at home. Many schools ask families not to send it, or to send it only with permission.
- **Tissues and wipes:** Some schools ask each family to contribute a box. Wait for your school's list.

## How to label kindergarten supplies

Labelling is the single most useful thing you can do. Children's mitts, hats and water bottles all look the same in a cubby.

Step 1: **Use your child's first and last name.** Many classes have more than one child with the same first name.

Step 2: **Label every part.** Water bottle and lid, lunch bag and every container, both mitts, both boots, hat, jacket, splash pants, indoor shoes and the spare-clothes bag.

Step 3: **Choose labels that last.** Iron-on or stick-on clothing labels hold up better than marker on fabric. A permanent marker works on tags and hard plastic.

Step 4: **Add a picture symbol** (a star or a heart) if your child is not reading names yet, so they can find their own things.

Step 5: **Check labels each season.** They fade in the wash.

## What not to send

- Toys from home, unless the teacher invites them (many allow a comfort item in the first week).
- Money, jewellery or electronics.
- Candy, gum or sweets as snacks.
- Anything with nuts, and anything that is not halal at an Islamic school.
- A brand-new item your child has never tried, such as new shoes or a new lunch container on the first day.

## Practise with the gear before day one

Supplies only help if your child can use them alone. In the two weeks before school:

1. **Pack and unpack the backpack** together so your child knows where everything lives.
2. **Open and close every container,** including the water bottle, at least five times.
3. **Put on indoor shoes and outdoor shoes** independently.
4. **Practise zipping a jacket.** Try the "flip trick": lay the coat on the floor, hood at the feet, arms in, flip it over the head.
5. **Eat a "school lunch"** at home from the lunch bag, in about 20–30 minutes.

## The bottom line

The best kindergarten supply list is about independence, not stationery. Send a backpack, lunch bag and containers your child can manage, indoor shoes, a spare set of clothes and weather-ready outdoor gear, all clearly labelled. Keep snacks nut-free and halal, wait for the school's own list before buying classroom supplies, and practise with everything before the first day.
